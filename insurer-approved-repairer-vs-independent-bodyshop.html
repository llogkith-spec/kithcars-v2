Approved Repairer or Independent Bodyshop?

Both can do good work and both can do bad work. The difference is not skill, it is who the commercial arrangement was designed to suit — and that shows up in the labour time allowed and the grade of parts specified.

The short answer

  • Both can be good. The difference is whose costs the rate was built to suit.
  • The squeeze usually shows in labour time and parts grade.
  • Approved networks are genuinely faster on admin and usually include a courtesy car.
  • The choice matters most on newer cars and difficult paint.

The row that does the work is the last-but-one. An approved shop depends on the insurer for its workflow. That is not corruption, it is just where the pressure points. If an engineer disputes two hours of labour, the shop that needs next month’s referrals has a different incentive to the shop that does not.

Where approved networks are genuinely good

Volume brings real advantages: dedicated insurer liaison, faster authorisation, courtesy cars on tap and well-drilled processes. For a straightforward bumper and wing on a three-year-old car, a good approved shop is a perfectly sensible choice, and the admin is lighter for you.

Worth saying plainly: we are not claiming the network is a scam. It is a supply arrangement that suits some jobs and some owners.

Where it goes wrong

  • Labour time squeezed. Pre-agreed rates mean the shop absorbs the gap or works to the allowed time. Blending paint into adjacent panels is often the first thing cut, and it is the thing you will see in sunlight.
  • Parts grade. “Matching quality” non-genuine panels fit well on common cars and badly on others. On a car still under manufacturer paint warranty this is worth checking.
  • Nobody argues for you. If the strip-down reveals more damage, someone has to raise a supplementary and defend it.

How to choose for your car

A rough rule: the newer, rarer or more expensively painted the car, the more the choice matters. A solid-white fleet hatchback is hard to get wrong. A three-stage pearl, a metallic that shifts in the light, or an aluminium panel is not.

Whoever you claim through, the repairer is your choice. KITH is on no insurer’s panel, which means no labour time has been pre-agreed on your car’s behalf — we estimate the job as it needs doing and argue it with the engineer if we have to.

Your questions answered

Are independents cheaper?

Not necessarily, and that is not the pitch. We are often the same or slightly more per hour than a pre-negotiated network rate, because the rate has not been discounted in advance. What you get for it is the time the job needs.

Will the insurer trust an independent estimate?

Yes. The engineer inspects and authorises the same way regardless. We produce estimates on the same industry systems insurers use.

Can I get genuine parts?

You can ask for them. The insurer may only authorise the cost of matching-quality equivalents, in which case you can pay the difference. We will show you both figures.

How do I judge any bodyshop?

Ask what paint system they use, whether they blend into adjacent panels, what the paint guarantee is and in writing, and ask to see a car they are working on. A shop proud of its work will show you.
