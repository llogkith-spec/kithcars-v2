In the workshop

How we handle car respray at Hackbridge

Colour is where most resprays are won or lost. We mix from your paint code, but a code is only a starting point: factories ran variants of the same shade, and fifteen years of sun will have moved the colour on the car in front of us. So we spray a test card, cure it, and hold it against a clean panel in daylight rather than under workshop lights. On a part respray that card decides where we blend. On metallics and pearls it also decides the spray technique, because the way the flake lies alters the shade as much as the mix does.

Preparation then runs to an order you could watch. Lights, handles, badges, aerials and rubbers come off and go into labelled bags, and anything that has perished is listed for you before we order it. Bare metal gets etch or epoxy primer the same day it is exposed, because steel left overnight in a damp workshop starts to flash rust. Panels are block-flatted by hand and checked with a guide coat, which shows up every low spot the eye misses.

Bubbling rust does not get painted over here, a hole gets fresh metal let into it rather than filler, and trim that ought to come off comes off. All three short cuts look perfectly acceptable for a season and then fail in front of you, which is why we decline the work rather than take it on those terms. We will also say when a respray is not the sensible spend and price two or three panels instead. Resprays start from £999, but the number on your quote comes from what we found on your car, and it lists what is included: which panels, whether shuts and openings are done, and what we have allowed for underneath.

The cars we see

Most full resprays we take on are cars their owners mean to keep: hatchbacks and saloons from the mid-2000s to mid-2010s whose roofs and bonnets have lost their lacquer, and older classics coming back after welding. Dark metallics and solid reds fade hardest on open driveways. Pearl and tri-coat whites need a three-stage process and more time in the booth. Aluminium bonnets and doors, common on larger models, need their own primer and have to be kept away from steel dust in the workshop.

Car Respray: what’s involved

The quality of a respray is decided by the preparation, not the paint. The existing finish has to be flatted, dents repaired, and lights, handles, badges and seals removed rather than masked over, so paint goes where it should and edges don't lift later. Any rust must be cut out and repaired first; painting over it only hides it for a while.

A same-colour respray usually covers the exterior panels. A colour change is a much bigger job: to look right, the door shuts, boot and bonnet openings and often the engine bay need painting too, which means far more dismantling. If you change your car's colour you must tell DVLA so your V5C logbook is updated, and you should tell your insurer. Because the condition of the car makes such a difference to the work involved, we quote in writing only after inspecting it.

Signs your car may need a respray

Lacquer peel

Clear coat lifting and flaking, often on the roof, bonnet and boot. It can't be polished out and the affected panels need refinishing.

Faded or chalky paint

Years of sunlight break down the paint, and older solid reds and other single-layer colours are particularly prone to fading.

Chips and scratches on many panels

When damage is spread all over the car, a respray gives a more consistent result than a series of patch repairs.

Mismatched panels

Previous repairs in slightly different shades that make the car look patched.

Rust breaking through in several places

Bubbling on arches, sills and door bottoms, which needs rust repair before any paint goes on.

A colour change

Whether for personal preference or to return a car to its original colour.

Restoration projects

Classic and older cars being brought back after bodywork and rust repair.

When is a respray worth it?

A respray makes sense when paint problems are spread across the car rather than confined to one or two panels, for example lacquer peel on the roof, bonnet and boot, or fading all over. If the damage is limited, individual panel repairs are usually better value. For classics and cars you plan to keep, a respray done properly after rust repair protects the body for years. On a lower-value car, compare the quote with what the car is worth before going ahead.

  • Lacquer peel or fading on three or more panels
  • Chips and scratches across most of the car after years of use
  • Classic or restoration projects, after bodywork and rust repair
  • A colour change you've decided on, remembering DVLA and your insurer
  • Previous poor repairs leaving panels visibly different shades

How we respray a car

  1. Inspection and written quoteWe assess the paint, look for rust, filler and previous repairs, and agree the scope with you, including whether shuts and openings are painted.
  2. DismantleLights, bumpers, handles, badges, trim and seals are removed as agreed, then bagged and labelled for refitting.
  3. Bodywork and rust repairDents are repaired, rust is cut out and repaired with new metal, and poor previous repairs are put right.
  4. Flatting and primingThe whole surface is flatted, bare metal is primed, and primer-filler is applied and block-flatted so the panels are straight.
  5. Mask and cleanThe car is masked, degreased and tack-wiped in a clean, controlled environment so dust doesn't settle in the paint.
  6. Basecoat and clear coatColour, matched from your paint code or your chosen colour, is applied in several coats, followed by a two-pack clear coat.
  7. Curing and finishingAfter controlled curing, any dust nibs or imperfections are flatted and the paint is polished to a uniform gloss.
  8. Reassembly and handoverEverything is refitted with new clips and seals where needed, fit and function are checked, and we inspect the finish with you.

Paint and materials

We use a primer system suited to what's underneath, basecoat mixed to your paint code or chosen colour, and two-pack clear coat, which cures chemically to a hard, glossy and fuel-resistant finish. Rubbers, clips and badges that break or have perished when removed are replaced with genuine or OE-equivalent parts, and we'll tell you in advance where older parts are likely to need replacing.

If it’s left

Failing lacquer leaves the colour coat unprotected, so it fades and breaks down faster. Where rust is showing, it spreads under the paint and the amount of metal to cut out grows each winter. Waiting doesn't make the respray itself more expensive, but it usually adds preparation and rust repair to the bill.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about car respray

Judging a quote on the paint

Materials are a small share of a respray. Nearly all the difference between two prices is hours of stripping, flatting and rust repair, so ask what preparation each one includes.

Polishing lacquer that is peeling

Once clear coat has started to flake there is nothing sound left to cut back to. Machine polishing only thins what remains and brings the next patch forward.

Getting a price from photographs

A photograph flattens a panel, hides filler and tells nobody how thick the paint is. A full respray quoted without the car being seen usually changes once the work starts.

Expecting a warranty on old paint

Our workmanship warranty covers what we sprayed. Where we have blended into original paint that is already breaking down elsewhere on the car, that older paint is not covered, and we will show you where the risk sits.

Questions

Car Respray: your questions answered

Will my insurance pay for a respray?

Only where the paint was damaged in an incident you are claiming for, and that is handled as an accident repair rather than a respray. Lacquer peel, fading and years of stone chipping count as wear, so a comprehensive policy will not cover them. Bring the car in and we will tell you honestly which of the two you have.

Can I manage without my car for that long?

A respray is not a job you wait for, and the car has to stay with us through curing. We collect and deliver locally, and a courtesy car may be available depending on what is free that week, so ask when you book. If the timing is awkward we can usually agree a start date rather than taking the car immediately.

What happens to the price if you find more rust once you start?

The written quote states what we could see and what we have allowed for. If stripping reveals more than that, we stop, photograph it and give you a revised figure before any further time goes on the car. You can then go ahead, reduce the scope or stop there.

Can you just do the panels that look bad?

Often, and it is frequently the better value. The question is whether fresh paint alongside faded original will match, which is why we blend into the neighbouring panels rather than stopping at a join. On a badly faded car we will say plainly if a part respray will look patched, and price the alternative so you can choose.

How much does a full respray cost?

Prices start from £999, but the final cost depends on the size and condition of the car, whether door shuts and openings are painted, whether the colour is changing and how much rust or dent repair is needed. We give a written quote after inspecting the car. Very cheap resprays save money by skipping preparation and masking over trim, which usually shows within months.

Do I need to tell DVLA if I change my car's colour?

Yes. You need to update the colour on your V5C logbook with DVLA, and you should also tell your insurer, as a colour change can be treated as a modification.

Will a respray affect my car's value?

A good same-colour respray can improve the value and saleability of a tired car. A poor one, or an unusual colour change, can put buyers off. Buyers may ask why a car has been repainted, so keep the invoice showing what was done.

Can you paint over the existing paint?

If the original paint is sound, it's flatted and used as the base for the new paint. If the lacquer is failing, the paint is cracking or there are many layers from previous resprays, it needs stripping back to a sound layer or bare metal. We decide this at inspection and include it in the quote.

How should I look after new paint?

The paint is cured when you collect the car but continues to harden for a few weeks. Hand wash gently during that time, keep pressure washers at a distance and avoid waxing or sealing it for about a month.
