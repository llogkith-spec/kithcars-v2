Recovering Your Excess After a Non-Fault Accident

It is yours to get back and almost nobody chases it. Excess recovery is the quietest leak in motor claims — paid out at the start, forgotten by the time liability settles six months later.

Why you paid it at all

Your own insurer settles the repair first and argues about who pays later. That is the point of the cover: you get your car fixed now rather than waiting for two insurers to finish disagreeing. The excess is your contribution under your own policy, and it applies whoever caused the accident.

Once liability is settled against the other driver, their insurer reimburses yours for everything — the repair, the hire car, the recovery, and your excess. That last part is supposed to come back to you.

How to actually get it back

  1. Say it at the first call. “I want my excess recovered from the third party. Please record that on the claim.”
  2. Keep the proof. The receipt or the bank entry showing what you paid and when.
  3. Diarise it for three months. Put a reminder in your phone now. This is the step everyone skips and it is the reason the money goes missing.
  4. Chase in writing. “Please confirm the status of my claim and when my excess will be refunded.” Email, so there is a record.
  5. Escalate if it stalls. A formal complaint, then the Financial Ombudsman Service if the final response is unreasonable. Both are free.

What the timeline actually looks like

StageTypical timing
You pay the excessAt collection
Liability admitted or disputedWeeks to months
Insurers settle between themselvesMonths
Excess refunded to youAfter settlement — only if chased

If liability is admitted early the whole thing can run in a couple of months. If it is disputed, or there is injury involved, it can take considerably longer, and that is the gap in which people forget the money exists.

When you should not have paid it at all

If the other driver admitted fault immediately and their insurer accepted liability at the outset, ask your own insurer to waive the excess rather than collect and refund it. Some will. It is always worth the question, and the answer costs you one phone call.

The other route is claiming directly against the at-fault insurer rather than your own. There is no excess that way because it is not your policy paying. The trade is that you give up the protection of your own insurer if the claim turns awkward, and you are negotiating on your own.

Your questions answered

How long do I have to claim it back?

There is no separate deadline for the excess — it travels with the claim. What matters is that the claim itself is settled and that you have told them you want it back. Chase at three months, not at twelve.

They have refunded the repair but not my excess.

That happens, and it is usually an oversight rather than a refusal. Email, quote the claim number, state the amount and the date you paid it, and ask for a payment date.

Can I claim the excess from the other driver directly?

You can, if their insurer has accepted liability and your own insurer is not already pursuing it. Doing both at once causes confusion, so tell your insurer which route you are taking.

What if liability is split?

Then recovery is usually split the same way. On a 50/50 you would typically get half your excess back, which is worth knowing before you expect the full amount.
