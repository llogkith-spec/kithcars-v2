Servicing

Car Servicing: what’s involved

A service replaces the parts and fluids that wear out or degrade with time and mileage, and gives a trained technician the chance to look over the car before small faults become expensive ones. Engine oil is the most important item: it breaks down with heat and picks up soot and fuel, and modern engines with turbochargers, timing chains and particulate filters rely on oil of the exact specification the manufacturer approves.

We offer three levels. An interim service (every 6 months or 6,000 miles) covers oil, oil filter and safety checks for higher-mileage drivers. A full service (every 12 months or 12,000 miles) adds filters and a wider inspection. A major service follows the manufacturer's longer-interval items, such as spark plugs, fuel filter and brake fluid, where they are due for your car.

Signs your car is due a service

Service indicator on the dash

Most modern cars count down time and mileage, or calculate oil life from how the car is driven. When the spanner or service message appears, the car is due.

Dark or low engine oil

Oil that is black and thin on the dipstick, or needs topping up between services, has done its job. Some engines use a little oil normally, but a rising rate of use needs looking at.

Past 12 months or 12,000 miles

If you can't remember the last service or the book hasn't been stamped for a year, it is due regardless of mileage, because oil and brake fluid age even when the car sits still.

Rougher running or poorer economy

Worn spark plugs, a dirty air filter or old fuel filter can cause hesitation, lumpy idle and higher fuel use. A service often restores smooth running.

Squeaks, knocks or brake noise

New noises from brakes or suspension are worth reporting when you book, so we can pay particular attention to them during the inspection.

Before a long trip or an MOT

A service ahead of a long journey or the MOT catches worn tyres, brakes and bulbs before they cause a breakdown or a failure.

You've just bought a used car

Unless the history clearly shows recent work, a full or major service gives you a known starting point and shows whether longer-interval items like the cambelt are due.

When should it be done?

Follow your manufacturer's schedule, which is based on time or mileage, whichever comes first. As a general guide, an interim service suits drivers covering high mileage or mostly short trips, a full service is due every 12 months or 12,000 miles, and a major service usually falls every second year or at the manufacturer's longer interval. Cars on variable or long-life schedules can run longer between services, but short journeys, towing and stop-start town driving are harder on oil.

  • Interim service: every 6 months or 6,000 miles
  • Full service: every 12 months or 12,000 miles
  • Major service: typically every 24 months or 24,000 miles, or at the manufacturer's longer interval
  • Long-life or variable schedules: follow the car's service indicator, but no longer than the manufacturer's maximum time limit
  • Mostly short journeys or town driving: consider an interim service between full services

How we service your car

  1. Check-in and historyWe look up your car's manufacturer schedule and service history, and ask about any warning lights, noises or concerns so we know what to look for.
  2. Drain and replace engine oilWe drain the old oil, fit a new sump plug washer where required, replace the oil filter and refill with oil that meets your manufacturer's approval.
  3. Replace filters and service itemsDepending on the service level and what is due, we replace the air filter, cabin filter, fuel filter and spark plugs, and change the brake fluid if it is due or has absorbed too much moisture.
  4. Underbody and brake inspectionWith the car on the lift we check brake pads, discs, hoses and pipes, suspension joints, steering components, driveshaft gaiters, the exhaust and for oil or fluid leaks.
  5. Tyres, lights and fluidsWe check tread depth and condition on every tyre, set pressures, test all lights, wipers and the horn, and check coolant, washer, power steering and brake fluid levels.
  6. Battery and diagnosticsWe test the battery and charging system and scan for stored fault codes where the service level includes it, so hidden faults don't go unnoticed.
  7. Reset and recordWe reset the service indicator, stamp your service book or update the digital service record, and note any items due at the next service, such as the cambelt.
  8. Road test and handoverWe road test the car and go through the written report with you. Anything else we find is quoted separately, and nothing extra is done without your approval.

Oils and parts we use

We use engine oil that carries your manufacturer's approval, not just the right viscosity, and we are proud to use Castrol. Many modern engines need a specific approval, such as VW 504 00/507 00, BMW Longlife-04 or Mercedes 229.51, and using the wrong oil can harm the engine and particulate filter. Filters and plugs are OE or OE-equivalent, from suppliers such as MANN, Mahle, Bosch and NGK, so your manufacturer warranty is protected.

  • Castrol engine oil to your manufacturer's specification
  • OE or OE-equivalent oil, air, cabin and fuel filters from suppliers such as MANN, Mahle and Bosch
  • Spark plugs to the correct specification, from suppliers such as NGK and Bosch
  • DOT 4 or manufacturer-specified brake fluid
  • New sump plug washers and seals where required

If it’s left

Stretching service intervals mostly harms the engine slowly. Old oil loses its ability to protect turbochargers, timing chains and bearings, and sludge can block oil passages. Missed services also leave gaps in the service history, which reduces resale value and can complicate warranty claims. Just as importantly, worn brakes or tyres that a service would have spotted can go unnoticed until the MOT or a breakdown.

Questions

Car Servicing in Wallington: your questions answered

Can I drop my car before the school run and pick it up after?

Yes. We open at 8am, so you can leave the car first thing and, in most cases, collect it by afternoon pick-up. We'll ring if anything needs your approval or will take longer.

Do you service diesels that only do the school run?

Yes, and we'll give you honest advice about them. A diesel that only does short trips around Wallington can struggle to regenerate its particulate filter, so we check for stored faults and use low-ash oil to the correct specification. If it keeps having trouble, a longer run every week or two usually helps.

Is it worth having the service and MOT together?

Many drivers do, because it means one visit instead of two. The MOT checks roadworthiness on the day and the service is maintenance, so they complement each other. Booking both at once also means any MOT advisories can be looked at while the car is here.

Will an independent service affect my manufacturer warranty?

No. Under the UK motor vehicle block exemption rules, a manufacturer can't make warranty conditional on dealer servicing. As long as the car is serviced to the manufacturer's schedule using parts of matching quality and the correct oil, your warranty stays intact. We record the work so you have proof.

What is the difference between interim, full and major?

An interim service is an oil and filter change with key safety checks, suited to high-mileage drivers between full services. A full service adds more filters and a wider inspection once a year. A major service adds the longer-interval items your manufacturer specifies, such as spark plugs, fuel filter and brake fluid.

Is a service the same as an MOT?

No. The MOT is a legal inspection of roadworthiness and emissions on the day, and it doesn't replace any oil or parts. A service is maintenance that keeps the car healthy. Many customers book both together to save a second visit.

Do you update digital service records?

Yes, for makes where the manufacturer's digital service record system is available to independent garages, and we stamp the paper service book where the car has one. Either way you get a written record of what was done.

Will you do extra work without asking?

No. If we find something that needs attention, such as worn brake pads, we contact you with a written quote first. You decide what goes ahead, and nothing is added to the bill without your approval.
