Term-time traffic and tight parking: Norbury bumper repairs

School-run traffic builds on Norbury's local roads in term time, and with residential streets 20mph, narrow and parked on both sides, there's little room for error. Up towards Streatham Common the roads are wider, but parking on slopes and in driveways brings its own scrapes. Most of the bumper damage we see from Norbury falls into three groups: scuffed corners, pushed-in rear bumpers with broken tabs, and splits running from a sensor hole or edge.

Each needs a slightly different fix. Scuffs are sanded back, treated with adhesion promoter and flexible primer if the plastic is exposed, then painted to your paint code. Pushed-in bumpers are reshaped with controlled heat where the plastic isn't torn, and broken tabs rebuilt. Splits are plastic-welded or bonded from behind so they don't open again with vibration. Where a sensor hole has cracked, we check the sensor and its bracket as well. You get a written quote first, explaining whether repair or replacement is better value.

Bumper Repair: what’s involved

Most modern bumpers are a painted plastic cover over an energy-absorbing foam or plastic core and a metal reinforcement bar. The cover flexes, so it survives small knocks, but it scuffs easily, its mounting tabs snap and cracks spread from the edges. Plastic needs its own preparation: an adhesion promoter on bare plastic and a flexible primer, otherwise paint can crack or flake off when the bumper flexes.

Most scuffs and many cracks can be repaired rather than replaced. We plastic-weld or bond cracks from behind, rebuild broken tabs and refinish only as much as needed. Bumpers also carry parking sensors and, on many newer cars, radar for adaptive cruise, emergency braking or blind-spot monitoring. We check these are secure and working after refitting, and arrange recalibration where the car requires it.

Signs your bumper needs repair

Scuffed or scraped corners

Typical parking damage. If the paint has been scraped through to the plastic, it needs refinishing rather than polishing.

Cracks or splits

These often start at edges or around sensor holes and grow with vibration and temperature changes.

Gap between bumper and wing

Usually broken mounting tabs or brackets, so the cover is no longer held in its correct position.

Loose or sagging lower edge

A section hanging down can catch on kerbs or be pulled further off by wind at speed.

Paint flaking or peeling

Often a sign of a previous repair done without proper plastic preparation, or stone chips letting the paint lift.

Parking sensor error or constant beeping

A sensor pushed out of its bracket or a damaged wiring connector behind the bumper.

Driver assistance warning after a knock

Radar behind the bumper may be misaligned, leaving adaptive cruise, emergency braking or blind-spot monitoring unavailable.

Dent or distortion in the cover

Plastic that has been pushed in but not torn can often be reshaped with controlled heat.

When should it be done?

Get a bumper repaired soon after the damage, particularly if it's cracked, loose or carries sensors. Cracks spread and loose sections can be torn off at speed, which turns a repair into a replacement. If a parking sensor or driver assistance warning has appeared, have it checked before relying on those systems. Lease and PCP customers often have bumper scuffs repaired before the car goes back, as end-of-contract damage charges can be higher than the cost of a repair.

  • Paint scraped through to the plastic on a corner or edge
  • Any crack, split or broken mounting tab
  • Parking sensor, radar or blind-spot warning after a knock
  • Bumper loose, sagging or not lining up with the wings
  • Before a lease or PCP return inspection

How we repair a bumper

  1. Inspection and quoteWe check the cover, mounting tabs, reinforcement bar and sensors, then give you a written quote and explain whether repair or replacement makes more sense.
  2. Remove the bumper where neededMany repairs are best done off the car, so we can reach the back of cracks and check the brackets and reinforcement behind.
  3. Repair the plasticCracks are plastic-welded or bonded from behind, broken tabs are rebuilt, and distorted areas are reshaped with controlled heat.
  4. Prepare the surfaceThe area is sanded and filled with flexible filler where needed, then bare plastic gets adhesion promoter and a flexible primer.
  5. PaintColour matched from your paint code and blended where needed, then finished with clear coat and controlled curing.
  6. Refit and alignThe bumper is refitted with new clips where the old ones broke, and gaps to the wings and lights are set evenly.
  7. Check sensors and hand overWe test the parking sensors, scan for fault codes, arrange ADAS recalibration where required and inspect the finish with you.

Parts and materials

Where a bumper can be repaired we keep the original cover, which already fits the car exactly. If it's too badly damaged, or repair would cost close to a new part, we fit a genuine or OE-equivalent bumper and tell you which. Broken clips and brackets are replaced rather than glued, and we use adhesion promoter, flexible primer and two-pack clear coat so the finish can flex with the bumper.

  • Plastic welding rod or two-part structural plastic adhesive suited to the bumper material
  • Adhesion promoter and flexible primer for bare plastic
  • Paint mixed to your paint code with two-pack clear coat
  • New clips, brackets and fixings where the originals are broken
  • Genuine or OE-equivalent bumper covers when replacement is the better option

If it’s left

A scuff left alone is mostly cosmetic, but cracks and broken tabs get worse. Vibration spreads cracks, a loose bumper can be pulled off by wind at motorway speed, and water getting behind it can reach sensor connectors. A bumper that is insecure or has sharp protruding edges can also fail an MOT.

Questions

Bumper Repair in Norbury: your questions answered

What's the best time to drive from Norbury to avoid the roundabout queues?

Mid-morning or early afternoon is usually easiest, as Thornton Heath Pond and the Lombard roundabout queue at peak times. If timing is difficult, collection and delivery can be arranged.

My bumper has a crack starting from a parking sensor hole. Can it be repaired?

Usually. We weld or bond the crack from behind and check the sensor bracket, which may also be cracked. If the damage around the hole is too extensive, we'll quote for a replacement bumper instead.

Will a repaired bumper still look right next to my car's metal panels?

Paint is mixed from your paint code and blended where needed. We check the colour against the adjacent wing in daylight before the car is handed back.

Can a cracked bumper be repaired or does it need replacing?

Most cracks can be repaired if the plastic isn't shattered or missing large pieces. Replacement makes more sense when there are several cracks, the mounting points are badly torn, or the repair would cost nearly as much as a new painted bumper. We'll tell you which applies when we quote.

Will the repaired bumper match the rest of the car?

Paint is colour matched from your paint code and blended where needed. Plastic bumpers sit next to metal wings and can look very slightly different even from the factory, so we check the match in daylight before handing the car back.

My car has parking sensors. Will they still work?

Yes. Sensors are removed before painting, refitted into their brackets afterwards and tested before the car is handed back. If a sensor or bracket was damaged in the knock, we'll quote to replace it.

Does my car need radar recalibration after bumper work?

If your car has radar behind the bumper or badge for adaptive cruise, emergency braking or blind-spot monitoring, removing or replacing the bumper or sensor bracket can mean the manufacturer requires recalibration. We check this at the quote stage and arrange it where needed.

How soon can I wash the car after a bumper repair?

A gentle hand wash is fine after about a week. Keep a pressure washer well away from the repair and avoid machine polishing or waxing it for a few weeks while the paint finishes hardening.
