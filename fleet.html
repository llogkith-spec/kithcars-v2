Work for a business is not a different kind of spanner, it is a different set of priorities. A private customer wants the car right; a business wants it right and back by Thursday with paperwork the accounts office can use. So this side of the operation is arranged around scheduling as much as repair. MOT dates are tracked against the registration, services are planned into your quieter weeks, and jobs are grouped so a vehicle comes off the road once instead of three times. We cover cars used for work, light commercial vans, private hire and taxi vehicles, and small mixed fleets of both. Anything unusually heavy or specialised deserves a phone call before you book, so we can tell you plainly what we can take and what we cannot.

Booking starts with two things: the registration and the date you need the vehicle back. From the registration we can see what is due, what was advised last time and whether the MOT is close enough to be worth doing in the same visit. Keys can be dropped from 8am and collected until 8pm Monday to Friday and until 5pm on Saturday, which for most drivers removes the need to give up a working day. If the vehicle is going to have to stay overnight, you are told that at the point we know, not at five o'clock. Anything found beyond the booked work goes to whoever signs off spending with a price beside it, and the vehicle waits until that answer comes back.

For a business the number that matters is cost per vehicle over a year, not the labour rate on the wall, so every job is quoted in writing and every registration is itemised. That makes it obvious when one vehicle is quietly costing more than the rest of the fleet. Servicing for vans and fleet vehicles starts from £182 and the MOT is £50, under the DVSA maximum of £54.85, the same as for any private customer. Where a repair can safely wait, we will say so and put a date against it; where it cannot, we say that too. Purchase order numbers, VAT invoices and payment terms are agreed before the first vehicle arrives, and business repairs carry the same three-month workmanship warranty as the rest of our work.

Questions

Common questions

What size vehicles can you MOT and service?

Class 4 takes in cars and any goods vehicle whose design gross weight is 3,000kg or under, so most car-derived and medium panel vans sit inside it. Vans above that and up to 3,500kg fall into Class 7. Servicing and repair reach further up the weight range than testing does. Give us the plated weight for each vehicle and we will set out what can be tested with us and what we can work on.

Can we open an account and pay on invoice?

Yes. Tell us how your purchasing works and we will match it, whether that means a purchase order number on every job, named people authorising spend up to a set limit, or a single VAT invoice covering a period. Getting that agreed before the first vehicle arrives avoids a repair sitting finished while somebody hunts for approval.

Do you look after private hire and taxi vehicles?

We do, and they wear differently from private cars because of the mileage and the stop-start nature of the work. Brakes, suspension bushes and hybrid twelve-volt batteries are the usual casualties. The licensing inspection itself has to be carried out by your licensing authority's own approved station, but we can prepare the vehicle for it and put right anything it picks up.

How quickly can you turn a van around?

A service or MOT booked in advance is normally same-day, and we will tell you when to collect at the point of booking. Repairs depend on parts, so if the vehicle is critical, say so when you ring and we will check availability before you commit to a date rather than after. Where a part is going to take days, it is often better to keep the van working and book it for when the part lands.
