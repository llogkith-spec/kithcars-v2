Wallington drivers

What we see on cars from Wallington

Wallington's car population shifted after the ULEZ expanded across London in August 2023. A good number of households moved to something newer and compliant, while plenty of well-kept older cars stayed put for local use, and the two want different things from a garage. A newer car still inside its manufacturer warranty can be serviced here to the manufacturer's own schedule using parts of matching quality without putting that warranty at risk, which is what the block exemption rules exist to protect. An older one needs time spent underneath it, on brake pipes, flexible hoses, sills and subframe mountings, because it is corrosion rather than mileage that usually ends a car's MOT career around here. We weld and repair rust where the car is worth it and say so plainly when it is not.

The streets do the rest of the work. Residential roads across the town are lined with parked cars and run single file in places, the junctions where Woodcote Road meets Stafford Road and Manor Road bunch up at rush hour and again on Saturdays, and crossing the A232 at Wallington Green queues at peak times. None of that breaks a car quickly, but it wears at the edges: kerbed alloys, scuffed bumper corners, mirrors clipped in passing, clutches and brakes ageing faster than the odometer suggests. Out towards Roundshaw, the aviation-named streets of the estate built on the old Croydon Airport site are wide but heavily parked, and Plough Lane and Sandy Lane South carry their share of school traffic.

Having the garage this close changes what counts as a problem. A car that will not start on a driveway near Mellows Park or Bandon Hill can be recovered rather than coaxed along the A232 on a flat battery. A car booked in for paint can be collected and returned without anyone writing off a morning. And because Soho Mills is on London Road near Hackbridge station, with Beddington Park and the Wandle just to the east, handing the car over and getting yourself home again takes minutes. Parking near Wallington station and the town centre is controlled, so if you are leaving a car anywhere other than with us, read the signs first.

Questions

Wallington: your questions answered

Are you actually in Wallington?

We are in Hackbridge, at the Hackbridge end of London Road, which falls in the SM6 Wallington postal district and in the same London Borough of Sutton. From Wallington town centre it is about 1.7 miles and six minutes up the A237. So: your postal town, a mile and a half up the road, rather than in the town centre itself.

Can I leave the car and get home easily?

Yes. Hackbridge station is close to the workshop, and for most of the town the drive back is only a few minutes if someone can follow you down. If neither suits, we can collect the car from you and return it when the work is finished.

My car will not start on the drive. Can you come to it?

Yes, we run recovery as well as the workshop, so a car that will not start or is not safe to drive can be brought in. Tell us what the car is doing when you call and we will say whether it is a jump start, a battery or something that needs the vehicle on a lift here.

Do you handle insurance claims, or only work I pay for myself?

Both. The bodyshop takes insurance and self-pay accident repair, and plenty of the smaller local jobs, like a creased wing or a bumper caught on a narrow street, cost less to settle privately than an excess and a lost no-claims bonus. We quote in writing either way so you can decide with the numbers in front of you.
