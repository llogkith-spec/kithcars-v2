Coolant loss on the A22 and the BMW EGR cooler question

A BMW diesel that keeps needing coolant with no visible leak is a classic specialist case, and the A22 Godstone Road towards Riddlesdown Common is as good a place as any to notice it. The warning pops up, you top up, and there is nothing on the driveway. On N47, B47 and N57 engines, one likely cause is the EGR cooler, which uses engine coolant to cool recirculated exhaust gas. If it leaks internally, coolant is drawn into the intake, sometimes with white smoke or a sweet smell.

BMW has run a recall on EGR coolers covering many of these engines, and we can check whether it may apply to your car. If it does, the recall work is carried out free by a BMW dealer, and we will point you there. If it does not, we pressure test the cooling system, inspect the EGR cooler and intake, and give you a written quote. We also check whether the leak has affected anything else, such as the EGR valve.

Purley's hills matter here too. The valley roads around the town centre are steep, and climbing with a loaded engine raises exhaust temperatures and pressure, which can expose a cooler or EGR valve that is already weak. The Purley Cross gyratory then adds stop-start idling, which is where a sticking EGR valve causes rough running. We test the valve's movement and the intake for soot as part of the same diagnosis.

Not all Purley BMWs are diesels. The four-cylinder N20 petrol, found in many 2011 to 2015 models, has a known weakness in its timing chain guides, which can show as a whine or rattle. We check those too. The route to us goes up Foxley Lane to the A237 and north through Wallington, about 15 minutes. If you are losing coolant fast, do not drive the car; we can arrange collection.

Local tip

If your BMW diesel needs regular coolant top-ups with no leak underneath, stop topping up and have it checked, as internal EGR cooler leaks can worsen and let coolant into the engine intake.

BMW Specialist Repairs: what’s involved

BMW's four-cylinder N47 diesel, used from around 2007 in the 1, 3 and 5 Series and X models, has its timing chain at the back of the engine, against the gearbox. When the chain or its guides wear, a proper repair usually means removing the engine. The later B47 keeps the rear chain layout and has proved far more reliable, but the same work is involved if it ever needs doing.

Modern BMWs are also heavily networked. One failed component often shows as faults in several modules, and many new parts, from batteries and diesel injectors to control units, must be registered or coded before the car works correctly. Specialist work means reading the fault data properly, testing before replacing, and finishing each job with the right adaptations.

BMW problems we specialise in

N47 timing chain rattle

A rattle or whirring from the back of the engine, often most noticeable at idle, is the classic sign. If the chain breaks, the engine is usually badly damaged.

Diesel EGR valve and cooler faults

N47, B47 and N57 diesels can suffer sticking EGR valves and leaking EGR coolers. BMW has run a recall on EGR coolers covering many of these engines, and we can advise whether it may apply.

DPF warnings and failed regenerations

Short-trip driving, faulty pressure sensors and EGR problems stop the DPF regenerating, leading to warning lights and limp mode. We find the cause rather than just clearing the blockage.

Diesel injector faults

Rough idle, smoke, misfires or hard starting can come from worn injectors. We use leak-off tests and live correction values to identify the faulty injector, and code replacements to the engine ECU.

VANOS and valve timing faults

On petrol engines, VANOS solenoid or unit faults cause rough running, poor pick-up and camshaft timing fault codes.

N54 fuel pump and injector faults

Twin-turbo N54 petrol engines have a history of high-pressure fuel pump and injector problems, showing as long cranking, hesitation and reduced power.

Coding and registration

Batteries, injectors, control units and some retrofits must be registered or coded. Without it you can get warning lights, poor charging or features that do not work.

Intermittent electrical faults

Faults across several modules, a battery that drains overnight, or warnings that come and go need systematic testing of power, earths and network communication, not guesswork.

When should you book specialist work?

Specialist work is usually triggered by a symptom rather than a mileage: a new noise, a warning light, poor running or a repair elsewhere that has not fixed the problem. On an N47, any chain noise should be investigated promptly. If you are buying a used N47-engined BMW, a pre-purchase check with a chain listen and diagnostic scan is well worth it. After replacing any electronic part, the car should be coded and adapted before it goes back on the road.

  • N47 diesel (roughly 2007–2015): investigate any rattle from the rear of the engine straight away
  • Diesels used mainly for short trips: check DPF soot load and EGR condition at each service
  • After battery, injector or control unit replacement: registration or coding required
  • Before buying a used BMW: diagnostic scan and inspection of known weak points

How we handle specialist BMW work

  1. Talk through the symptomsWe ask when the fault happens, hot or cold, at what speed and since when, and what has already been tried.
  2. Full diagnostic scanEvery module is read with dealer-level diagnostic equipment, including fault history and freeze-frame data, so we see the whole picture.
  3. Targeted testingWe confirm the fault with live data, leak-off or smoke tests, electrical measurements or a listening check as appropriate, before any parts are ordered.
  4. Explanation and written quoteWe explain what has failed, why, and what the repair involves, with a written quote and time estimate.
  5. RepairFor an N47 chain this normally means removing the engine and replacing the chains, guides, tensioner and sprockets as a kit, with new seals and fluids.
  6. Coding and adaptationsReplaced parts are registered or coded, injector correction codes are entered, and adaptations are reset where required.
  7. Rescan and checksFault memories are cleared and every module rescanned to confirm nothing new has been logged.
  8. Road test and handoverThe car is road tested under the conditions that caused the original fault, and we explain the work and what to watch for.

About Purley

Purley is a green, hilly suburb on the edge of the North Downs about three miles south of Croydon, which grew rapidly in the 1920s and 1930s as a place of spacious homes. Its small town centre sits in a valley around Purley Cross, where the A23, A22, A235 and A2022 all meet, while the surrounding roads climb up to detached and semi-detached houses in Woodcote, Russell Hill, Riddlesdown and the private Webb Estate. BMW Specialist Repairs in Purley: your questions answered

My BMW diesel is losing coolant but there is no leak. What could it be?

An internal EGR cooler leak is a common cause on BMW diesels, though a head gasket or a leak that evaporates on hot parts is also possible. We pressure test the system and inspect the cooler to find out which.

Can you check whether my BMW is covered by the EGR cooler recall?

We can check whether it may apply. If it does, the recall repair is done free by a BMW dealer, so we will point you there. If not, we can quote for the repair ourselves.

Does the petrol N20 engine have timing chain problems too?

The N20 four-cylinder petrol has a known weakness in its timing chain guides. A whining or rattling noise from the front of the engine should be checked promptly, and we can inspect it and quote for a kit if needed.

Does the engine have to come out for an N47 timing chain?

In almost all cases, yes. The chain sits at the back of the engine next to the gearbox, so proper access means removing the engine. That is why the job takes longer and costs more than on engines with the chain at the front.

Is the B47 engine affected like the N47?

The B47, which replaced the N47 from around 2014, keeps the chain at the rear but has proved far less troublesome. When we see one, we still listen for chain noise and check for known faults.

Can you code parts to my BMW?

Yes, for common requirements such as battery registration, injector correction codes and many replacement modules, using dealer-level diagnostic equipment. Some work, such as certain key and immobiliser jobs, may need BMW's online systems, and we will tell you before starting if so.

How is this different from your BMW servicing?

Our BMW servicing covers routine services, MOTs and everyday repairs. Specialist work is for faults that need deeper diagnosis or major engine work, such as timing chains, injectors, EGR and DPF problems, electrical faults and coding.

Do you check for BMW recalls?

We can check whether an open recall, such as the diesel EGR cooler recall, may apply to your car. Recall work itself is carried out free of charge by a BMW dealer, so we will point you there if it does.
