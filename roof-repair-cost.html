What roof actually is

Roof is pressed steel, welded to the shell along the gutters. It does not come off. Everything is done on the car.

Behind it sits the headlining, the aerial base, the interior lights and grab handles, the curtain airbags down the sides, and the sunroof mechanism where one is fitted. All of that comes out or gets protected before any paint goes near the car, and anything electrical is tested again before you collect it.

The largest single panel on the car and the hardest to get right. There is nowhere for a colour difference to hide on a flat horizontal surface that size, which is why a roof is painted complete and blended into both quarters rather than patched in the middle. It is also the most expensive panel on our rate card, and that is the reason why.

How roofs get damaged

Four causes account for most of what comes through the door:

  • Hail, which dents the whole panel at once rather than in one place.
  • Falling branches and masonry.
  • Loading roof bars badly, or something sliding off them.
  • Carrying something through a low garage opening.

The thing worth checking. The sunroof, if there is one. A dented roof around a sunroof aperture can distort the frame enough to stop it sealing, and a roof that leaks after a repair is a considerably bigger problem than the dent that started it. The curtain airbags run down the sides of the roof lining too.

Repair or replace

Replacing a roof skin means cutting the old one off the gutters and welding a new one on. It is done, but it is a last resort rather than a cost option, and on most cars heavy roof damage is where a write-off conversation starts.

How long it takes. Three to four days. The headlining often has to come down to work a dent from inside, and that adds a day on its own.

Hail damage is a different problem

Hail does not make one dent, it makes two hundred small ones spread across every horizontal surface: roof, bonnet, boot lid and the tops of the wings. Counting and pricing them individually is meaningless, so hail is assessed by panel and by density rather than by dent.

It is also the one kind of damage where the right answer is frequently an insurance claim even on an older car, because the total across four or five panels adds up fast. Photograph it in raking light rather than straight on, because hail is close to invisible in a flat photograph and insurers assess from photographs first.

Your questions answered

Can you repair roof without painting it?

Only where the damage is in the lacquer alone, and we will tell you if yours is one of those rather than selling you a respray. Once it is through the colour, refinishing is the only repair that lasts. On dents there is no paint-free option at all — we do not do paintless dent removal, so the panel is straightened and then painted.

Will the colour match?

It is mixed to the paint code on your car, then sprayed into both rear quarters, both A-pillars and the windscreen surround rather than stopped at the shut line. Two cars with the same code are rarely identical after a few years of sun, and blending is what makes the difference invisible instead of nearly right.

Is it guaranteed?

One year on paint, three months on mechanical work, in writing.

Should I claim on insurance?

At £285 to £720 it is worth checking your excess first. Plenty of single-panel jobs cost less than the excess, and paying directly avoids declaring a claim for five years. We will price it either way and we are not on any insurer’s panel, so we have no reason to push you one way.

Do you need to see the car first?

Not to give you a price band — put your registration in the box above. We confirm the exact figure when we see it, because how far the damage goes is the one thing photographs do not show reliably.
