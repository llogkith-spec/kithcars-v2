In the workshop

How we handle cabin filter replacement at Hackbridge

The interesting part of this job is not the filter, it is what surrounds it. With the housing open we look for leaf litter and silt, standing water, and the tide mark that says water has been sitting there for some time. On cars where the filter lives under the scuttle at the base of the windscreen, that area drains through a pair of small channels which block with leaf mulch, especially on a car parked under trees. Blocked drains send the water somewhere else, and on a good number of cars somewhere else turns out to be the passenger footwell or a module mounted beneath it.

The new filter goes in the right way up, with the airflow arrow pointing the way the car pushes air, and seated so the frame meets the housing all the way round. A filter a few millimetres undersize, or one forced in at an angle, lets air take the easy route past it, which is why bargain filters sometimes seem to change nothing. Afterwards we run the blower through every speed and check flow at the face vents, the screen and the footwells, because a restriction further downstream feels identical from the driver's seat. If the evaporator behind it smells, or the drain is plugged, we say so rather than selling you another filter.

We will not fit a filter into a housing with water standing in it and call the job done, and we will not sell an air conditioning treatment as a cure for a smell that is coming from a blocked drain. If the filter was changed recently and is still clean, we will tell you and charge you nothing. The price depends entirely on how long it takes to reach, and your registration gives us that before you book, so you know in advance whether you are looking at the starting figure or an hour of labour.

The cars we see

Cabin Filter Replacement: what’s involved

All the air that comes through your car's vents passes through the cabin filter first, whether the fan is on fresh air or the air conditioning is running. A standard pollen filter traps dust, pollen and road grime. An activated carbon filter adds a layer that absorbs some traffic fumes and odours, which is worthwhile if you spend a lot of time in heavy traffic.

As the filter clogs, less air reaches the cabin, so the fan has to work harder and the heater and air conditioning feel weaker. A damp, dirty filter can also harbour mould and bacteria, which is often the cause of a musty smell when you switch the fan on. The filter is usually behind the glovebox or under the scuttle panel below the windscreen.

Signs your cabin filter needs replacing

Weak airflow from the vents

If the fan sounds busy but little air comes out, even on the highest setting, a blocked cabin filter is the most common cause.

Windows slow to demist

Reduced airflow means the windscreen takes longer to clear, which is a safety issue in cold, wet weather.

Musty or damp smell

A smell when you first switch on the fan or air conditioning often comes from a damp, dirty filter or bacteria on the evaporator behind it.

Hay fever symptoms in the car

If sneezing and itchy eyes get worse when you drive, the filter may no longer be trapping pollen effectively.

Dust on the dashboard

A filter that is saturated, damaged or missing lets dust blow straight through the vents.

Leaves or debris in the vents

Debris coming through suggests the filter is missing, torn or wrongly fitted, or the scuttle drain area is blocked.

Whistling from the fan

A restricted filter can make the blower whistle or strain, and adds load to the blower motor over time.

When should it be done?

Most manufacturers recommend changing the cabin filter every 12 months or around 10,000–15,000 miles, often at the annual full service. Cars used in heavy traffic, on dusty roads or parked under trees may need it sooner. Spring, before the pollen season, is a sensible time for anyone with hay fever. If the filter has been missed at recent services, it is usually worth doing straight away.

  • Every 12 months or around 10,000–15,000 miles, depending on the manufacturer
  • At the annual full service on most cars
  • Before the pollen season if you suffer from hay fever
  • Whenever there is a musty smell or weak airflow
  • Sooner for cars in heavy traffic or parked under trees

If it’s left

An overdue cabin filter won't damage the engine, but it does affect comfort and safety. Weak airflow makes the windscreen slower to demist, the blower motor works harder, and a damp filter can keep a musty smell circulating. For drivers with asthma or hay fever, it also means more dust and pollen in the air they breathe.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about cabin filter replacement

You can wash and reuse it

Pleated paper and carbon media are not made to be cleaned. Blowing one through with an airline opens the media up and drives the trapped dirt deeper, and a washed filter never seals or filters the same again.

It only matters for hay fever

The same filter handles every bit of air that demists your windscreen. When it clogs, the screen takes longer to clear on a cold wet morning, which is a visibility problem before it is a comfort one.

Every service includes one

Plenty of interim and basic services do not, and on some schedules the filter is a two-year item. It is worth reading the invoice rather than assuming, particularly on a car that has passed through several owners.

Either way round is fine

The arrow matters. Filters are built to collect dirt on one face, and fitted backwards they hold less, block sooner and can shed what they have already caught back towards the blower.

Cabin Filter Replacement: your questions answered

Why is the price given as from £35 rather than fixed?

Because the filter is cheap and the access is not. A glovebox-mounted filter really is the starting figure. One under the scuttle, with wiper arms and a seal to remove first, takes closer to an hour and the quote reflects that. Send us your registration and you will have the exact price before booking.

Do I need to book, or can I just turn up?

Booking is better, because the filter is ordered against your registration rather than kept on the shelf for every model on the road. A day's notice is usually enough. The workshop runs Monday to Saturday, 8am to 8pm, so it fits around work without costing you a morning.

There is damp in my footwell. Will a new filter stop it?

No, and the cause is worth chasing. Water in a footwell normally comes from blocked scuttle drains, a failed windscreen seal, a door membrane that has come unstuck, or a leaking heater matrix. We check the drains as part of the filter change and tell you where to look if it is coming from somewhere else.

Can it be done while the car is in for something else?

Yes, and that is the sensible way to do it. Fitted alongside a service, an MOT or air conditioning work it adds very little time to the visit. Mention it when you book so the correct filter is waiting here rather than being ordered on the day.

Is a cabin filter the same as an air filter?

No. The cabin filter cleans air going into the passenger compartment, while the engine air filter cleans air going into the engine. They are different parts in different places with different intervals.

Is an activated carbon filter worth it?

If you drive mostly in town traffic or are sensitive to smells, yes. The carbon layer absorbs some odours and gases that a standard filter lets through. It doesn't make the air perfectly clean, but it is a noticeable improvement for many people.

Will a new filter cure a smell from the air conditioning?

Often it helps, as a damp filter is a common source. If the smell comes from bacteria on the evaporator, the system may also need an antibacterial treatment, which we can quote for.

Does the cabin filter affect the air conditioning?

A blocked filter reduces the amount of cooled air that reaches the cabin, so the air conditioning feels weaker. It doesn't affect the refrigerant, so if the air is warm rather than just weak, the system may need a regas.

Can my car run without a cabin filter?

It will run, but dust and leaves can get into the heater and evaporator, where they are harder to clean out and can cause smells or blockages. We always recommend fitting one.
