Carshalton drivers

What we see on cars from Carshalton

Carshalton is a difficult place to keep a car looking tidy. The Victorian and Edwardian streets around the village and The Wrythe are narrow with cars parked on both sides, so passing means threading through gaps barely wider than the mirrors. Folded mirror casings, scuffed door edges, creased bumper corners and alloys grazed along a kerb are the everyday results, and they are exactly the sort of damage the bodyshop deals with. A car park ding is straightened and the panel repainted, and a scuff through the lacquer is prepared and resprayed the same way, so the colour match holds up in daylight on a metallic finish.

Then there is the terrain. Park Hill and Beeches Avenue climb steadily from the village up to Carshalton Beeches and Carshalton on the Hill, and the A232 past the ponds is narrow, slow and full of buses and pedestrians. Repeated hill starts on a gradient with a load on board are what wear a clutch out, and a biting point that has crept high, a smell of hot friction material on the climb, or revs that flare without extra speed all point the same way. Cooling systems get a harder time here too, so we look at coolant condition and check for weeping hoses and radiator seams while a car is on the ramp.

Plenty of Carshalton cars are older and well looked after, kept going because they suit their owners. Those are the ones where corrosion decides the MOT result: sills, subframe mountings, brake pipes and suspension mounting points within the prescribed areas. Surface rust caught early can be cut out and welded; left to spread it becomes a structural failure. Wrythe Lane is busy all day with St Helier Hospital traffic, ambulances, visitors and buses, and hospital staff are among the drivers who make most use of our longer opening hours, dropping a car early or collecting it in the evening.

The route

From Carshalton to Soho Mills

From the village, head north on West Street past Carshalton station, join Wrythe Lane briefly, then take Wrythe Green Road onto Nightingale Road and Hackbridge Road. That drops you into Hackbridge, where you turn onto London Road, the A237, for Soho Mills near Hackbridge station. It is about 1.2 miles and roughly five minutes. From Carshalton Beeches, come down Park Hill into the village first and pick up the same route.

From Carshalton village, head north on West Street (B278) past Carshalton station, briefly onto Wrythe Lane, then Wrythe Green Road onto Nightingale Road and Hackbridge Road (both B277). At Hackbridge, turn onto London Road (A237) for Soho Mills, near Hackbridge station.

Questions

Carshalton: your questions answered

Can I book a courtesy car while my car is in?

Courtesy cars are subject to availability, so the honest answer is that it depends on the week and what is already out. Ask when you book and we will tell you what we can hold for you. If nothing is free we can usually collect and deliver instead.

I work shifts at St Helier. Can I drop off early or collect late?

Yes. We are open 8am to 8pm Monday to Friday and 8am to 5pm on Saturday, so an early drop-off before a day shift or a collection after one is straightforward. Let us know roughly when you will arrive so someone is expecting you.

How do I get back into Carshalton after leaving the car?

Carshalton is one stop from Hackbridge on the same line, so the train back into the village is straightforward. Carshalton Beeches is on a different line and would mean changing at Sutton, so from there a lift back or collection and delivery is often quicker. Tell us which you would rather do when you book.

My car is older and I am worried about the MOT. Will you tell me before spending money?

Always. You get a written quote before any work begins, and if a car needs welding or major corrosion repair we will show you what we have found and explain what it will take. If we think the repair is not worth the money on that vehicle, we will say so.
