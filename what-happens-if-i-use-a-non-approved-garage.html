What Happens If I Use a Non-Approved Garage?

Four things can change, and only one of them usually costs you money. Knowing which is which before you book the car in is the whole trick.

The short answer

  • Four things can change — excess, courtesy car, guarantee and the authorised rate.
  • Only the rate usually costs you anything.
  • Your claim, your no-claims and the insurer’s duty to repair do not change.
  • Get the rate and the excess confirmed in writing before booking in.

The four things that can change

What changesHow likelyWhat to do about it
Higher excessOccasional — policy dependentCheck the repairs clause before you commit
No courtesy carCommon if the car is tied to their networkAsk; on a non-fault claim there are other routes
No insurer guaranteeStandardGet your repairer’s own guarantee in writing
Authorised at their rate, you pay the gapThe one that bitesSettle the rate in writing before work starts

The shortfall, in plain terms

Insurers indemnify you for a reasonable cost of repair, and they treat their own negotiated rate as the benchmark for reasonable. If your repairer charges more per hour, the insurer may authorise only up to their figure and leave the balance to you.

On a small job the gap is often tens of pounds or nothing. On a large job with a lot of labour it can be a few hundred. That is exactly why we get the authorised rate confirmed before the car comes in, rather than discovering it at handover.

What does not change

  • Your right to claim at all.
  • The insurer’s duty to put the car back as it was.
  • Your no-claims position — that follows fault, not the choice of garage.
  • Your manufacturer warranty on mechanical parts.

A sensible order of operations

  1. Report it. Promptly. Do not wait while you decide on a garage.
  2. Read the repairs clause. In the policy wording, not the certificate. Look for excess and courtesy car wording.
  3. Ask the two questions. “What rate are you authorising, and does my excess change?” In writing.
  4. Then decide. With the numbers in front of you rather than the handler’s script.

Your questions answered

Can they refuse the claim entirely?

Not because of your choice of garage. They can decline a claim for non-disclosure, late notification or a policy breach — none of which is picking your own repairer.

What if the work is done and they then underpay?

That is why the rate goes in writing first. If it has already happened, put a complaint in writing and escalate to the Financial Ombudsman Service if their final response is unreasonable.

Is it worth it for a small job?

If the repair is close to your excess, the better question is whether to claim at all. Get a direct price from us and compare.

Do you handle the insurer side?

Yes — the estimate, the engineer, and any supplementary if the strip-down finds more than the photographs showed. You make the first notification call.
