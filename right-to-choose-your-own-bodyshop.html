Your Right to Choose Your Own Bodyshop

The car belongs to you. The contract with the insurer obliges them to put it back the way it was, not to decide whose hands do the work. Knowing where that line sits makes the phone call much shorter.

The short answer

  • The choice is yours by default. The insurer needs a policy term to restrict it.
  • Terms usually change what the choice costs, not whether you have it.

Where the right comes from

It is not a special motoring rule — it is ordinary contract and property law. Your policy is a promise to indemnify you: to restore your position after a loss. It is not a promise to deliver a repair through a particular supplier, unless the policy explicitly says so and you accepted that when you bought it.

That is why the honest answer to “am I allowed?” is that the question is the wrong way round. You do not need permission to choose. The insurer needs a term in the contract to restrict you, and most restrict the consequences rather than the choice itself.

What a policy can legitimately say

Read the claims-handling or repairs section, not the summary document. Common terms that are perfectly enforceable:

  • A higher excess applies if you use a repairer outside their network.
  • The courtesy car is only provided through their network.
  • Their repair guarantee only covers work their network carried out.
  • They will pay no more than the cost they would have incurred themselves.

None of those stop you. They change what the choice costs you, which is a different thing, and the last one is the only one that usually matters financially.

Doing it without the claim stalling

  1. Report the incident first. Do that promptly whatever you decide about the garage. Late notification is the one thing that genuinely prejudices a claim.
  2. Name your repairer at the same time. Doing it on the first call avoids the car being booked into their network and then moved.
  3. Get the authorised rate in writing. One email. “Please confirm the labour rate authorised and any shortfall payable by me.”
  4. Send us the claim reference. We take it from there with the engineer.

If they will not budge

Occasionally a handler simply repeats the script. Ask for it in writing: “Please confirm in writing that you are refusing to allow my chosen repairer, and quote the policy clause.” Almost nobody puts that in writing, because the clause usually does not exist.

If it genuinely goes nowhere, insurers have a complaints procedure and a final response, after which the Financial Ombudsman Service will look at it for free. That is rarely needed over the choice of garage, but knowing the route exists changes the tone of the conversation.

Your questions answered

Will using you cost me more?

Only if your insurer authorises less than the job takes, and we settle that before starting rather than presenting you with a shortfall at the end. On many claims there is no difference to you at all.

Do I lose the courtesy car?

Possibly, if your policy ties the courtesy car to their network. Ask. If it is tied, weigh it against what you gain — and on a non-fault claim there is usually a replacement vehicle available regardless.

What about the repair guarantee?

Theirs covers their network. We guarantee our own paint for a year and the bodywork we carry out, which is the guarantee that matters on your car.

Does this apply to a lease or company car?

Not always. If you do not own the car, the lease or fleet agreement may specify where it gets repaired, and that contract binds you. Check with the lease company first.
