Alternator Replacement: what’s involved

The alternator is driven by the auxiliary belt and generates electricity whenever the engine runs. It powers the car's electrics on the move and recharges the battery after starting. Inside, a regulator controls the output, diodes turn the alternating current it makes into direct current, and brushes feed the rotor. Many diesels and stop-start cars also use a freewheel or decoupler pulley that smooths out engine vibration.

On older cars the alternator simply holds the system at a steady voltage. Many modern cars use smart charging, where the engine ECU varies the alternator's output to save fuel, charging harder when you slow down and easing off when you accelerate. That makes testing more involved, because a reading that would look wrong on an older car may be normal. When an alternator fails, the car runs on battery power alone, usually for a short time before it stops.

Signs your alternator is failing

Battery warning light on

The red battery light while driving usually means the charging system isn't working, not that the battery itself is faulty. Get it checked promptly.

Repeated flat batteries

A car that needs jump-starting, even with a new battery, often has an alternator that isn't charging properly.

Dim or flickering lights

Headlights or dash lights that dim at idle and brighten with revs can point to low or unstable output.

Electrical faults while driving

As voltage drops, systems start to misbehave: warning lights appear, the radio cuts out and power steering or stop-start may stop working.

Whining or grinding noise

A whine that rises with engine speed can be worn bearings or the regulator. A rattle from the pulley can mean a failed decoupler or freewheel pulley.

Belt squeal

A squealing belt may be slipping on the pulley, which stops the alternator charging properly. It can also point to a worn tensioner.

Hot or burning smell

An overheating alternator or a slipping belt can give off a hot electrical or rubber smell and should be checked straight away.

When should it be done?

Alternators don't have a set replacement interval. Many last well over 100,000 miles, but bearings, brushes, regulators and pulleys wear, and oil or coolant leaks can shorten their life. Have the charging system tested if the battery light comes on, the battery keeps going flat or you hear a whine from the front of the engine. It is also worth checking whenever a new battery is fitted, so a charging fault isn't mistaken for a bad battery.

  • Battery warning light on while driving
  • Flat battery that returns after recharging or replacement
  • Whining, grinding or rattling from the alternator or its pulley
  • Oil or coolant leaks onto the alternator
  • Whenever a new battery is fitted

How we replace an alternator

  1. Check-in and symptomsWe ask about warning lights, flat batteries and noises, and whether a new battery has been fitted recently.
  2. Charging system testWe test battery health, alternator output under load and voltage ripple, and read any charging codes from the ECU, allowing for smart charging systems.
  3. Check belt, pulley and wiringWe inspect the auxiliary belt, tensioner and pulley, and check the main charging cable and earth connections. A bad earth can look like a failed alternator.
  4. Written quoteWe show you the results and give you a written quote. Work only goes ahead once you approve it.
  5. Remove the old alternatorWe disconnect the battery, remove the belt and take out the alternator. On some engines this means removing other components for access.
  6. Fit the new unit and beltWe fit the new alternator and a new auxiliary drive belt, check the tensioner and idler pulleys, and tighten the terminals to the correct torque.
  7. Retest and road testWe retest the charging voltage at idle and under electrical load, clear any codes, and road test the car to confirm correct charging.

If it’s left

Once an alternator stops charging, the car runs on the battery alone. It may keep going for a while, but as the voltage drops, systems shut down and the engine will eventually stop, possibly in traffic. A failing alternator can also deeply discharge and damage the battery, adding the cost of a new one.

Alternator Replacement in Thornton Heath: your questions answered

Can you collect my car on a Crystal Palace match day?

Yes, but roads around Selhurst Park close before kick-off and parking is tightly controlled, so we'll plan the collection for before the closures or after the roads reopen. Tell us the street when you book.

My older diesel keeps needing jump starts. Is it worth fixing?

If the engine and bodywork are sound, a charging repair is usually worth doing. A quality remanufactured alternator keeps the cost sensible, and sometimes the fault turns out to be a cable or earth. We'll give you the options in writing.

I drive private hire from Thornton Heath. Can you fit it in quickly?

We'll do our best to book it in promptly and give you a realistic time. Many alternators are a couple of hours' work once the part is here, so the car is often back the same day.

Could it just be the battery?

It could. Batteries and alternators produce similar symptoms, which is why we test both, along with the belt and wiring. A new battery on a car with a failing alternator will soon go flat again.

Why replace the drive belt too?

The belt has to come off to change the alternator, and an old belt is often glazed or cracked. Fitting a new one at the same time costs little extra and avoids paying for the same labour again when it fails.

Can I drive with the battery light on?

Only a short distance, and with as few electrical loads on as possible. The car is running on battery power and could stop without much warning. If the light came on with a squeal or loss of power steering, the belt may have failed, so stop and get advice.

New or remanufactured?

A quality remanufactured alternator is rebuilt with new wear parts and tested to OE specification, and is a sensible choice for many cars. We'll tell you which options are available for yours and the price of each.

What is smart charging?

On many modern cars the engine ECU controls alternator output, reducing it when the battery is well charged and increasing it when you slow down, to save fuel. It means voltage readings vary more than on older cars, so testing needs to take this into account.
