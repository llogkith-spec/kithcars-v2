Clutch Replacement: what’s involved

The clutch connects the engine to the gearbox. A friction plate is clamped between the flywheel and a spring-loaded pressure plate. When you press the pedal, the release bearing pushes on the pressure plate springs and frees the plate so you can change gear. Most modern cars use a hydraulic system, and many have a concentric slave cylinder that combines the release bearing and slave cylinder inside the bellhousing.

Most diesels and many petrol cars also have a dual mass flywheel, which uses internal springs to absorb engine vibration. It wears alongside the clutch and is a common cause of judder and rattles. Because the gearbox has to come out to reach any of these parts, we inspect them all together so you only pay for the labour once.

Signs your clutch needs replacing

Slipping under load

Engine revs rise but the car does not accelerate to match, most noticeable in a high gear going uphill or overtaking. This is a worn friction plate or oil contamination.

High bite point

As the friction plate wears, the clutch engages higher up the pedal travel. A bite point right at the top is a sign the plate is near the end of its life.

Judder when pulling away

Shaking as the clutch engages is often a worn dual mass flywheel, but can also be a contaminated plate or worn engine mounts. We check which before quoting.

Rattle at idle or a clunk when switching off

A metallic rattle at tickover or a knock as the engine stops are typical dual mass flywheel symptoms, caused by worn internal springs or excessive free play.

Noise when pressing the pedal

A whirr, chirp or grinding that appears or changes as you press the pedal usually points to a worn release bearing.

Difficulty selecting gears

Crunching into gear or struggling to get reverse with the engine running suggests the clutch is not fully releasing, often a hydraulic fault or a failing concentric slave cylinder.

Heavy, soft or sticking pedal

A pedal that sinks, stays down or feels spongy points to a leaking master or slave cylinder. A very heavy pedal can mean a worn pressure plate or a sticking cable.

Burning smell

A sharp smell after hill starts or heavy traffic is the friction material overheating. Occasional smells happen; regular ones mean the clutch is slipping.

When should a clutch be replaced?

A clutch has no fixed service interval. It is replaced when it slips, judders or stops releasing properly. Life depends heavily on driving style and use: a car that tows, carries heavy loads or spends its life in stop-start traffic will wear through a clutch far sooner than one used mostly on open roads. Riding the clutch and holding the car on the bite point on hills also shortens its life. If your car has a dual mass flywheel, it is often worn by the time the clutch needs replacing.

  • Typical clutch life: 60,000–100,000 miles, but under 40,000 in heavy town or towing use
  • Replace when slipping, a very high bite point or judder appears
  • Replace the dual mass flywheel if free play or rock is outside the manufacturer's limit
  • Replace the concentric slave cylinder with the clutch, as it needs the gearbox removed to reach
  • Fix any engine rear main seal or gearbox input seal leak at the same time

How we replace a clutch

  1. Check-in and road testWe drive the car to check the bite point, slip under load, judder and any noises, and confirm the fault is the clutch rather than engine mounts or the gearbox.
  2. Written quoteWe quote for the clutch kit and tell you in advance whether the dual mass flywheel is likely to need replacing, so there are no surprises.
  3. Gearbox removalThe engine is supported, then the driveshafts, linkages, starter and, on some cars, the subframe are removed to lower the gearbox.
  4. InspectionWe check the flywheel for heat marks, free play and rock, and look for oil leaks from the engine rear main seal or gearbox input shaft seal that would ruin a new clutch.
  5. Fit new clutchThe new friction plate is centred with an alignment tool, the pressure plate bolted down evenly to the correct torque, and the release bearing or concentric slave cylinder fitted.
  6. Refit gearboxThe gearbox goes back in with new fixings where the manufacturer requires them, and all linkages, driveshafts and mounts are refitted and torqued.
  7. Bleed and adjustThe hydraulic system is bled with fresh fluid and the pedal checked for full release and a correct bite point.
  8. Road test and handoverWe road test for smooth take-up, clean gear changes and no slip, then show you the old parts if you would like to see them.

Clutch parts we fit

We fit complete OE or OE-equivalent clutch kits from manufacturers such as LuK, Sachs, Valeo and Exedy, who supply clutches to car makers. A kit includes the friction plate, pressure plate and release bearing, or a concentric slave cylinder where your car uses one. If the dual mass flywheel needs replacing we fit a matching unit from the same quality of supplier rather than a cheap alternative, because the flywheel and clutch are designed to work together.

  • Friction plate and pressure plate
  • Release bearing or concentric slave cylinder

  • Clutch hydraulic fluid to manufacturer specification

If it’s left

A slipping clutch gets worse, and eventually the car will not drive up a hill or pull away at all. Overheating from slip can also glaze or crack the flywheel. A failing concentric slave cylinder can leave you unable to select gears, and a dual mass flywheel that breaks up internally can damage the gearbox bellhousing and starter.

Clutch Replacement in Worcester Park: your questions answered

I live on the Cuddington side, outside the ULEZ. Does that affect the trip to you?

It does if your car is not ULEZ-compliant, because the route to Hackbridge runs inside the zone and the daily charge would apply. Check your car on the TfL vehicle checker, then talk to us about the best way to get it here.

Can you look at the clutch when the car comes in for a service?

Yes. Mention a high bite point, slip or judder when you book and we will road test it and tell you how much life we think is left. That gives you time to plan the clutch rather than wait for it to fail.

Is there a courtesy car if the job takes all day?

A courtesy car may be available, subject to availability, so ask when you book. Collection and delivery can also be arranged, which saves you making the 20-minute trip twice.

Do I have to replace the dual mass flywheel as well?

Not always. We measure free play and rock against the manufacturer's limits once the gearbox is out. If it is within limits and not heat-damaged, it can be reused. If it is worn, fitting a new clutch against it usually brings the judder or rattle back.

What about solid flywheel conversion kits?

They are cheaper, but they remove the vibration damping the dual mass flywheel provides. Many owners notice more vibration and gearbox noise, especially at low revs. We generally recommend a like-for-like replacement and will explain the trade-off if you ask about a conversion.

Why is the concentric slave cylinder replaced with the clutch?

It sits inside the bellhousing, so the gearbox has to be removed to reach it. Slave cylinders commonly start leaking once disturbed or with age, and replacing it separately later would mean paying the same labour twice.

Can I make my clutch last longer?

Avoid resting your foot on the pedal, use the handbrake rather than the bite point on hills, and pull away with low revs. Selecting neutral at long stops also takes load off the release bearing.

Do you replace clutches on automatic or dual-clutch gearboxes?

Dual-clutch gearboxes such as DSG have their own clutch packs and are quoted individually after diagnosis. Conventional automatics use a torque converter rather than a clutch, and faults with those are handled through our gearbox services.
