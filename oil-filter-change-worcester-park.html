Second cars, standing time and the ULEZ boundary

Plenty of Worcester Park households run two cars, and the second one often leads a very quiet life. A trip along Central Road for the shops, the school run, a run up Cheam Common Road to the North Cheam crossroads and back, then a fortnight parked on the drive. Oil in a car used like that is not worn out by work, it is worn out by time. Acids from brief warm-ups sit in the sump, the additive package depletes, and the oil drains off the upper engine between uses.

The boundary running through the area adds a wrinkle. The London-wide ULEZ applies on the Sutton and Kingston side, while the Cuddington part of Worcester Park is in Surrey and outside it, so some households have kept an older car that would cost them to drive north of the line. There is nothing wrong with running an older engine, and clean oil is what makes it viable. Hardened seals, a well-used chain and an older turbo all suffer first and worst when the oil is left too long.

We will also be clear that this is not a service and will not stamp the book.

Oil & Filter Change: what’s involved

Engine oil does more than lubricate. It carries heat away from the pistons and the turbocharger bearings, holds soot and combustion acids in suspension, and on most modern engines it doubles as a hydraulic fluid, operating variable valve timing and the timing chain tensioner. The additive package that makes all of that possible is used up gradually, and the oil itself is thinned by fuel that slips past the bores on short journeys.

The filter traps the debris the oil collects, and once its element is full it opens a bypass valve so the engine still gets oil, only now unfiltered. That is why the filter is always renewed with the oil rather than left for next time. A standalone oil and filter change earns its place between annual services, before a long trip, or on a car that has stood for months. It is not a service, and we are clear about what it leaves out.

When an oil and filter change is the right call

Oil life counter running down

Many cars display a percentage of oil life or a distance to the next oil change, worked out from engine temperature, load and running time rather than mileage alone. When it reaches zero the oil is at the end of its useful life even if the annual service is months away.

Thin oil or a fuel smell on the dipstick

Oil darkens quickly on a diesel and that alone is not a fault. Oil that feels thin between your fingers, smells of fuel or sits above the maximum mark has been diluted and is no longer protecting properly.

Mostly short, cold journeys

A two or three mile trip never gets the oil hot enough to drive off condensation and unburnt fuel, so contamination builds up in the sump far faster than the mileage suggests.

A long trip or towing coming up

If the rest of the service is not yet due but you are about to load the car up or cover a lot of motorway miles, fresh oil going into that is cheap insurance for the turbo and the bearings.

The car has been standing

Oil ages on the shelf and in the sump. A car laid up for a winter, or one used a handful of times a month, can be due on time even with almost no miles added.

Regular top-ups between services

A little consumption is normal on some modern turbo petrol engines. When top-ups become frequent it is worth starting again with fresh oil of the exact grade, so the rate of use can be judged from a known point.

Brief chain rattle on a cold start

Timing chain tensioners are fed by oil pressure, and long oil intervals make a cold-start rattle more likely. Fresh oil will not repair a stretched chain, but running clean oil stops the wear accelerating while you decide what to do.

You have just bought the car with no paperwork

Where there is no record of when the oil was last done, changing it gives you a known starting date before you commit to a full or major service.

When should it be done?

Manufacturers set the oil interval by time and by distance, whichever comes first, and cars on a variable schedule work it out for themselves from temperature, load and how long the engine runs. As a working guide, fixed-interval cars are due every 3 months or around 10,000 miles, and long-life cars can run further but should never pass the manufacturer's maximum time limit, usually two years. Short journeys, towing and long spells idling in traffic all shorten how long the oil stays useful.

  • Fixed schedules: typically every 3 months or around 10,000 miles, whichever comes first
  • Long-life or variable schedules: follow the car's own counter, but not beyond the manufacturer's maximum time limit
  • Mostly town or short-trip driving: consider every 6 months or 5,000 to 6,000 miles
  • Diesels that attempt regeneration often in traffic: check level and condition between changes, because fuel dilution raises the level
  • After a long period standing: change on the date even though the mileage is low

How we change your oil and filter

  1. Confirm the oil specificationWe look up the approval your engine is built for from the vehicle details, not just the viscosity, along with the exact refill capacity and the filter part number. Getting this right matters more on modern engines than anything else in the job.
  2. Warm the engineWarm oil drains more completely and carries more of the suspended contamination out with it. We bring the engine up to temperature rather than draining it stone cold.
  3. Drain through the sump plugWe drain from the plug at the lowest point of the sump so the sediment that has settled there comes out too, and fit the new washer or replacement drain bolt the manufacturer specifies. The plug goes back to its torque setting, not to feel.
  4. Renew the filter properlySpin-on canisters are pre-filled and the seal lightly oiled before fitting. Cartridge filters get the new O-rings supplied in the kit, and the housing is tightened to its figure, because plastic housings crack when they are over-tightened.
  5. Refill to capacityWe refill to the correct quantity for your engine, run it to fill the filter and circulate the oil, then let it stand before checking the level.
  6. Check the level the way the car expectsOn a dipstick car the level is checked with the car standing level and the oil settled. Cars without a dipstick read the level electronically and only give a valid figure at a set oil temperature, so we take the reading properly rather than trusting the first number on the display.
  7. Reset the indicatorWhere the car has an oil service counter or an oil life display, we reset it and tell you what the next service point will be.
  8. Look over what we can seeWhile the car is up we tell you about anything obvious, such as a weeping seal, a split gaiter or a tyre down to the wear bars. This is not the structured inspection you get with a service, and we do not pretend otherwise.
  9. Handover and disposalThe old oil and filter go to a licensed waste oil contractor, and the date, mileage, oil grade and filter are written on your invoice.

Oil and filters we use

We are proud to use Castrol, and the grade we put in is the one your engine is approved for rather than the nearest viscosity on the shelf. Many engines call for a specific approval, and low-ash oils are required where a petrol or diesel particulate filter is fitted. Oil filters are OE or OE-equivalent from suppliers such as MANN, Mahle and Bosch, fitted with the seals that come in the kit and a fresh sump washer.

  • Castrol engine oil carrying your manufacturer's approval, in the correct viscosity
  • OE or OE-equivalent oil filter from suppliers such as MANN, Mahle or Bosch
  • New cartridge O-rings or a new spin-on canister, whichever your engine uses
  • New sump plug washer, or a replacement drain bolt where the manufacturer specifies one
  • Low-ash oil where a petrol or diesel particulate filter is fitted

If it’s left

Oil that has run well past its useful life stops protecting the parts that need it most. Turbocharger bearings run hot enough to bake thickened oil into hard deposits, chain tensioners lose the oil pressure they rely on and let the chain slap, and sludge can restrict the pickup strainer in the sump. None of that shows on the dashboard until oil pressure drops, and by then the damage is already done.

About Worcester Park

Worcester Park is a quiet, largely inter-war suburb about 10 miles south-west of central London, built up after the railway arrived and named after the 4th Earl of Worcester, keeper of Henry VIII's Great Park of Nonsuch from 1606. It sits across several council boundaries: most of it is in Sutton, the Cuddington side is in Surrey's Epsom and Ewell, and the station itself and part of Old Malden are in Kingston. Housing is mostly semi-detached family homes on long residential roads, focused on the half-mile Central Road shopping street. Driving is generally easy away from Central Road, though the single main road through the centre gets slow at peak times.

Oil & Filter Change in Worcester Park: your questions answered

Our second car does a few hundred miles a year. Does it need annual oil changes?

It needs them on date, yes. Oil deteriorates in the sump whether the car is driven or not, and the short cold trips a second car typically does accelerate it. Changing on the anniversary is cheap, and it means the low mileage genuinely counts in the car's favour.

I keep an older car on the Surrey side of the boundary. Is regular oil worth it?

On an older engine it is the most useful money you can spend. Clean oil of the correct grade keeps the chain tensioner fed, the turbo bearing cool and the sump pickup clear, which are the three things that usually end an older engine. It will not reverse existing wear, but it stops it accelerating.

Six miles each way is a nuisance. Can you collect from Worcester Park?

We arrange collection and delivery locally and Worcester Park is towards the edge of that, so tell us when you book and we will confirm whether we can cover it on the day you want. Otherwise the job itself is usually under an hour and you are welcome to wait.

Is an oil change the same as a service?

No. This job replaces the oil, the filter and the sump washer and nothing else. A service also renews the other filters appropriate to its level and works through a structured inspection of brakes, tyres, steering, suspension, lights and fluids, with a written report at the end. If your annual service is due, an oil change is not a substitute for it.

Do you stamp the service book for an oil change?

No. A stamp records a service carried out to the manufacturer's schedule, and this job is deliberately narrower than that. We put the date, mileage, oil grade and filter on your invoice so you have proof the oil was changed, and the next service still shows as due when it falls.

Can I save money with a cheaper oil?

Viscosity such as 5W-30 is only half the story. Many engines need an oil carrying a specific manufacturer approval, and a particulate filter needs a low-ash oil to avoid clogging it prematurely. Oil chemistry is also linked to low-speed pre-ignition on some turbocharged direct injection petrol engines. We fit what your engine is approved to run.

My car has no dipstick. Can you still check the level properly?

Yes. Those cars take the level electronically and the reading is only meaningful at a set oil temperature with the car standing level. We refill to the exact capacity for your engine, run it to fill the filter, then take the reading the way the manufacturer specifies rather than trusting the display straight after a refill.

Does the filter really need changing every time?

Yes. A used filter holds around half a litre of old contaminated oil and its element is already partly loaded, so leaving it in puts dirt straight back into clean oil and shortens how long the new oil lasts. On cartridge types we renew the O-rings from the kit as well, because reusing hardened seals is how oil leaks start.
