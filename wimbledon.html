Wimbledon drivers

What we see on cars from Wimbledon

Wimbledon runs a high share of newer and premium cars, a lot of SUVs and a growing number of hybrids and electric vehicles, and it parks them on some of the most tightly controlled streets in south London. Most town-centre and Village zones operate Monday to Saturday, and zones W3 and W4 run until 11pm with Sunday and bank holiday hours on top. Cars parked that closely together get hurt slowly rather than dramatically: alloys kerbed on the Village High Street, door edges caught in narrow bays, bumper corners lost reversing on Worple Road. That is bodyshop work, and on a modern finish it asks more than it looks. Metallic and pearl paints have to be matched to the car in front of you and blended into the adjacent panels, not simply sprayed to a code.

The landscape works on the running gear. Wimbledon Hill Road drops steeply from the Village to the town centre through buses, lights and crossings, and the A219 carries that traffic the length of the area from Parkside to South Wimbledon. Hill starts and hill stops are hard on an automatic gearbox's fluid and on a manual's clutch, and on a heavy SUV they eat front tyres and suspension bushes far quicker than motorway miles ever would. The A3 crosses Wimbledon Common as a fast dual carriageway and then queues at its junctions, which gives you the other half of the pattern: short bursts of speed, long stationary spells, and a diesel particulate filter that gets just enough of a run to be confusing rather than genuinely clean.

Two practical points about timing. For a fortnight from late June into July, Merton Council closes Church Road, Somerset Road, Marryat Road, Bathgate Road and other streets around the All England Club to most vehicles between 8.30am and 11.30pm, and traffic is heavy across the Village, Wimbledon Park and Southfields throughout, so where work is not urgent it is easier booked either side of it. For the rest of the year the drive is uncomplicated. If you would rather not make it twice, the tram from Wimbledon runs to Mitcham Junction, the next station up the line from Hackbridge, though collection and delivery is usually simpler still.

Questions

Wimbledon: your questions answered

Should I book around The Championships?

If the work can wait, yes. For that fortnight the roads around the All England Club are closed to most vehicles for much of the day and the wider area is congested, so getting out of Wimbledon takes longer than the drive to us. If something is unsafe, do not wait: call and we will arrange collection.

Can you collect the car from Wimbledon?

Yes, collection and delivery can be arranged and Wimbledon is well within range at about five and a half miles. It is the usual choice for customers in the town centre and the Village, where the controlled parking hours make leaving a car at home inconvenient. Settle the timing and the handover point at the point of booking.

What is the best way back into Wimbledon without the car?

The tram runs from Wimbledon to Mitcham Junction, which is the next station up the line from Hackbridge, and the workshop is near Hackbridge station. Check the live times before you set off. If that is more effort than it is worth, we will deliver the car back to you instead.

Can you colour match the paint on a newer car?

Yes. We start from the manufacturer's colour code, then match to the car itself, because paint fades and varies between batches and a code alone rarely gets it right on a metallic or pearl finish. The repair is blended into the adjacent panels and finished in two-pack lacquer so it reads as one continuous colour in daylight.
