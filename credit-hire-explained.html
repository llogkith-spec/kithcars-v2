Credit Hire, and What You Are Signing

Credit hire gets you a proper replacement car with nothing to pay up front, and the cost is recovered from the at-fault insurer. It is legitimate. It is also a hire agreement in your name, and that is the part worth understanding.

That last row is the whole thing. A courtesy car is given to you; credit hire is lent to you against an expectation that someone else will pay. Usually they do.

Why it exists

Before credit hire, a non-fault driver either paid for a hire car themselves and tried to recover it later, or went without. Neither is reasonable when someone else caused the damage.

Credit hire fixed that by letting a company supply the car and pursue the at-fault insurer directly. The driver gets a proper replacement immediately and pays nothing. The model works, and it is well established in law.

What to check before you sign

  1. Ask what happens if liability is disputed. This is the one question. Get the answer before you sign, not after.
  2. Check the daily rate and the expected duration. Credit hire rates are higher than ordinary hire, which is normal, but you should know the numbers.
  3. Confirm the car is genuinely like-for-like. You are entitled to a replacement that does your car’s job.
  4. Check the insurance and the excess on it. You are driving somebody else’s car on somebody else’s policy.
  5. Give it back when your car is ready. Entitlement runs while yours is off the road, not afterwards.

When to be careful

Credit hire is not a problem when liability is clear. It gets complicated when it is not, and the two situations that cause trouble are these: liability turns out to be split, so only part of the hire is recovered; or the at-fault insurer disputes the rate or the duration as unreasonable.

In both cases there can be a balance, and the agreement you signed says who carries it. If liability is genuinely contested, a courtesy car through your own policy is the quieter option even if it is a smaller car.

Your questions answered

Do I have to use the hire company the insurer suggests?

No, and the same goes for the repairer. Both are your choice.

Can I refuse credit hire and just claim the cost back?

Yes. Hire a car yourself, keep the invoice, and claim it as part of your losses. You pay up front, which is exactly the problem credit hire was invented to solve.

How long can I keep it?

While your car is genuinely off the road. If a repair is delayed waiting for parts, that is covered. If you simply have not collected your car, it is not.

Is it true credit hire pushes up everyone’s premiums?

It is an argument insurers make, and there is something in it. It is also the mechanism that gets a non-fault driver a proper car without paying for someone else’s mistake. Both things can be true.
