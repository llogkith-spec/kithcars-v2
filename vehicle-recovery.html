In the workshop

How we handle vehicle recovery at Hackbridge

The questions on the call are not idle ones. Where the vehicle is sitting decides which truck can reach it and how it comes out: an underground car park with a two-metre barrier, a residents' bay boxed in at both ends, a steep driveway or a narrow terraced street all change the job. So does whether the wheels turn, whether the steering unlocks and whether the car is stuck in park, because a vehicle that will not roll goes on skates rather than being driven onto the bed. An honest answer at that stage saves an hour and sometimes a second visit.

On arrival the first job is to establish what has actually happened. If a red charge light and a snapped auxiliary belt explain everything, you will be told that at the roadside, and now and then a car that cannot be driven is a five-minute fix rather than a recovery. Where it is genuinely a recovery, the vehicle is loaded under winch control and strapped through the wheels, so nothing is pulled through a suspension arm, a towing eye or a bumper. Electric and hybrid cars go on the bed with all four wheels clear of the ground, without exception, because dragging a driven axle turns the motor into a generator.

We do not tow on a rope or a pole, and we will not work in a live lane on a fast road, where the scene belongs to the police or traffic officers until the lane is closed. At the workshop the vehicle is booked in, the keys are logged and it is parked at our unit, and you get a call with what we have found and a written quote before anything is done to it. You are under no obligation to have the repair carried out here. If you would rather it went elsewhere, we will price that move for you before it happens.

The cars we see

Most of what goes on the bed is ordinary: family cars that will not start, private hire hybrids that have covered enormous mileages, and vans that have lost a clutch halfway round a delivery round. Electric cars need all four wheels carried and enough space to winch them on with no drive, and they are heavier than they look. Low and lowered cars need extra ramps to clear the bed angle without taking the front bumper off. Anything larger than a long-wheelbase van is a conversation when you ring rather than an assumption.

Recovery

Vehicle Recovery: what’s involved

Recovery is for any time a vehicle cannot or should not be driven: a breakdown at the roadside, an accident, a car that will not start on a driveway, or one that is no longer roadworthy. Rather than towing on a rope or pole, we load the vehicle onto our recovery truck, secure it and move it without further strain on the engine, gearbox or damaged parts.

Because we are also a working garage and bodyshop, recovery to our workshop means diagnosis or repair can start soon after the vehicle arrives, with a written quote before any work. If you would prefer the vehicle taken home or to another garage, we can do that too. Availability outside our normal opening hours varies, so call us to check.

When you need recovery

Roadside breakdown

The engine has cut out and will not restart, or a red warning light such as oil pressure or temperature is telling you to stop.

Accident damage

The vehicle is unsafe or illegal to drive after a collision, for example with damaged wheels or steering, broken lights or leaking fluids.

Non-runner at home

A car that has been standing for a long time, will not start, or has dangerous defects needs transporting rather than driving.

Wrong fuel

If you have put petrol in a diesel or diesel in a petrol car, do not start the engine. The vehicle needs moving so the fuel system can be drained.

Clutch or gearbox failure

No drive, gears that will not select or a strong burning smell mean the vehicle should be carried rather than driven.

Transport to our workshop

For planned repairs on a vehicle that is not safe to drive, such as failed brakes, steering faults or a broken timing belt, we can collect it.

Moving an untaxed or unroadworthy vehicle

A car you have bought, or one without tax, MOT or insurance, can be transported on the truck rather than driven.

When should you call?

Call once you and your passengers are safely away from traffic. On a motorway or fast dual carriageway, leave the vehicle by the nearside doors and wait behind the barrier if it is safe to do so. If you cannot get out of a live lane, call 999 first. After an accident, check whether your insurer requires you to use their own recovery service before booking ours, as this can affect your claim.

  • The engine will not start, or has cut out and will not restart
  • A red warning light for oil pressure, temperature or brakes
  • Accident damage affecting wheels, steering, lights or causing fluid leaks
  • Wrong fuel added: do not start the engine
  • Planned transport of a non-runner to our workshop or another address

How we carry your vehicle

Vehicles are carried rather than towed wherever possible, loaded using a winch and held with wheel straps rather than chains through the suspension or wheels, which avoids damage to alloys, arms and bodywork. Carrying the vehicle also puts no strain on a damaged gearbox or four-wheel-drive system, which matters for automatics and 4x4s that should not be towed with wheels on the ground.

  • Recovery truck carrying the vehicle off the ground
  • Winch loading for non-runners
  • Wheel straps to protect wheels and suspension
  • Warning beacons and high-visibility kit for roadside work
  • Suitable for most cars and small vans; ask about larger vehicles

If it’s left

Trying to drive a vehicle that has told you to stop can turn a modest repair into a major one: running with no oil pressure or overheating can wreck an engine in minutes, and driving on the wrong fuel spreads it through the fuel system. A broken-down vehicle left at the roadside is also a safety risk and may be removed by the authorities.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about vehicle recovery

A rope tow is free and fine

Towing on a rope needs two competent drivers, working brakes and steering on the towed car and a very short distance. What we usually see from it is damaged towing eyes, bumpers and, on automatics, gearboxes.

Any truck can reach anywhere

Height barriers on multi-storey and underground car parks stop a recovery truck at around two metres. Knowing the barrier height, or that the car is on level three, decides whether the truck comes or the car is moved out first.

Breakdown cover is for later

If you already pay for cover, use it first; you have bought it. We are the sensible call when there is no cover, when it will not take the car where you want it, or when the move is planned rather than an emergency.

Neutral is enough to load

An automatic with a flat battery may not come out of park at all, and an electric parking brake with no power stays on. Both are workable, but only if we know before setting off.

Questions

Vehicle Recovery: your questions answered

I have breakdown cover. Should I still ring you?

Use the cover you are paying for first, particularly at the roadside. Where we make more sense is when you have no cover, when the policy will only take the car to the nearest garage rather than the one you have chosen, or when it is a planned move of a vehicle that is not roadworthy.

Can you reach a car in an underground or multi-storey car park?

Tell us the barrier height when you ring. A recovery truck will not pass a standard two-metre barrier, so the car normally has to be brought out to open air first, which is a different job at a different price. It is workable in most car parks, but it is not something to discover on the day.

Do I have to have the repair done with you?

No. Plenty of vehicles are recovered here, diagnosed, quoted and then collected by the owner or moved on elsewhere, and that decision is yours once you have the written quote in front of you. Just tell us what you want to do, so the vehicle is not sitting here waiting on a decision nobody has made.

How and when do I pay?

The price is agreed with you on the phone before we set off, and settled once the job is done. If you need the invoice made out to an employer, a dealer or an insurer, say so during the call so it is raised correctly the first time.

Do you offer 24-hour recovery?

Our garage is open Monday to Saturday, 8am to 8pm. Recovery availability outside those hours varies, so please call 020 4629 9469 to check whether we can reach you.

How much does recovery cost?

Recovery starts from £108. The final price depends on distance, location and the vehicle's condition, and we confirm it with you before we set off.

Can you take my vehicle somewhere other than your garage?

Yes. We can deliver it to your home, another garage or another address. Just tell us the destination when you call so we can price the job correctly.

Should I use my insurer's recovery after an accident?

Check your policy first. Some insurers require you to use their approved recovery and repairer, and using someone else can affect the claim. If you are paying yourself or your insurer allows it, we can recover the vehicle and quote for repairs at our bodyshop.

Can you recover an automatic or four-wheel-drive car?

Yes. Because we carry the vehicle rather than tow it with wheels on the ground, there is no strain on the gearbox or drivetrain. Let us know if it is stuck in park or will not roll so we can come prepared.
