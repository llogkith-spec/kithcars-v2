The one thing worth knowing first

You choose who repairs your car. Not your insurer. They will steer you toward their own network because it costs them less, and the script can sound like a rule, but the car is yours and the choice is yours.

KITH is on no insurer’s panel and no manufacturer scheme. That means no labour time has been pre-agreed on your car’s behalf — we estimate the job as it needs doing, argue it with the engineer where we have to, and guarantee the paint for a year ourselves.

Just had an accident

  • Non-Fault Accident: What to DoPhotograph before anything moves, and get a witness name.
  • The Other Driver's Insurer Called Me DirectlyLegal, common, and not done for your benefit.
  • It Was My Fault. What Happens Now?Do not admit liability at the scene — it can breach your policy.
  • Split Liability: Both Partly at FaultLiability apportioned as a percentage, and everything splits with it.

Who pays, and how much

  • Who Pays My Excess?You pay it up front — even when the accident was not your fault.
  • Recovering Your Excess After a Non-Fault AccidentIt comes back to you only if you chase it.
  • Compulsory vs Voluntary ExcessYour excess is two numbers added together, and both are payable.
  • Should I Claim, or Pay for It Myself?Under your excess? Nothing to decide.
  • Will a Non-Fault Claim Affect My No-Claims?Discount and premium are different. You can keep one and still pay more.
  • Repair It, or Let Them Write It Off?Their sum compares repair against market value. Yours should compare it against replacement.
  • Protected No-Claims: What It Really CoversProtects the discount, not the premium it comes off.
  • Is Excess Protection Worth Buying?It reimburses your excess, it does not reduce it.
  • Paying for Bodywork YourselfYou keep your discount, your premium and a clean five years.

Getting your car back

  • Am I Entitled to a Courtesy Car?Fault claim: only if your policy includes one.
  • How a Bodywork Claim Works, Step by StepNine stages. Name your repairer on the first call.
  • What the Insurance Engineer Actually DoesThey are instructed by the insurer, not by you.
  • Supplementary Estimates: Why the Price Went UpA revised estimate for damage only visible once the panel came off.
  • Credit Hire, and What You Are SigningA proper like-for-like car with nothing to pay up front.

Choosing who repairs it

  • Do I Have to Use My Insurer’s Approved Repairer?No. You choose who repairs your car. Your insurer cannot overrule that.
  • Your Right to Choose Your Own BodyshopThe choice is yours by default. The insurer needs a policy term to restrict it.
  • Approved Repairer or Independent Bodyshop?Both can be good. The difference is whose costs the rate was built to suit.
  • What Happens If I Use a Non-Approved Garage?Four things can change — excess, courtesy car, guarantee and the authorised rate.

If they write it off

  • Category N Write-Off: What It Actually MeansN is for non-structural. The chassis and crumple zones are intact.
  • Write-Off Categories ExplainedA and B never return to the road. S and N can.
  • Category S Write-Off: Structural DamageS means something that carries load was damaged.
  • Category A and B Write-OffsBoth mean the car never returns to the road.
  • Can I Keep My Written-Off Car?Say it before you accept the settlement. After salvage collection it is gone.
  • Your Write-Off Valuation Is Too LowThey owe you market value — enough to buy a comparable car.
  • Selling a Cat S or Cat N CarYou must disclose it, and a history check finds it anyway.

When the repair goes wrong

  • The Repair Was Done Badly. Now What?If your insurer arranged it, your insurer answers for it.
  • When the Replacement Does Not MatchUnmatched part in a set? The approach is the damaged part plus about half the cost of the rest.
  • Can They Fit Second-Hand Parts?Generally not fair, except on very old, classic or limited-edition cars.
  • The Repair Is Taking Too LongThere is no fixed number of days — it turns on what was reasonable and what you were told.

Particular situations

  • Flood Damage: What Happens to the CarDo not try to start it. Cranking a flooded engine destroys it.

Your questions answered

Do you deal with my insurer?

Any of them. We handle the estimate, the engineer and any supplementary. You make the first notification call, because the policy is yours.

Is it cheaper to pay you directly than to claim?

On most single-panel damage, yes, once you count your excess and what a claim does to five years of premiums. Put your registration in the box above for a real figure, then compare.

Are you an approved repairer?

No, and deliberately not. Approved networks are discount arrangements negotiated before your accident. We would rather price the job in front of us.

What is guaranteed?

One year on paint, three months on mechanical work, in writing, whoever is paying.
