Alloy Wheel Refurbishment: what’s involved

Most alloy wheels have one of two finishes. Painted or powder coated wheels have a solid colour coating, usually silver, grey, black or gunmetal, over the whole wheel. Diamond cut wheels have a bright, finely machined face produced on a lathe and sealed with clear lacquer, often with painted sections between the spokes. They look different and are repaired differently, so the first step is identifying which you have.

Powder coating is a full refurbishment: the wheel is stripped and repaired, then the powder is applied electrostatically and cured in an oven, giving a thick, tough finish in almost any colour. Diamond cutting machines a very thin layer from the face and re-lacquers it; because it removes metal, a wheel can only be re-cut a limited number of times. Wheels that are cracked or badly buckled are checked for safety first, and sometimes need replacing rather than refurbishing.

Signs your alloys need refurbishing

Kerb rash

Scuffs and gouges on the rim edge from parking. Once through the coating, corrosion can start underneath.

White patches under the lacquer

Milky corrosion creeping under the clear lacquer is common on diamond cut wheels and spreads over time.

Flaking or bubbling paint

Corrosion under the coating, often starting at the rim edge, around the spokes or at the wheel bolt holes.

Slow puncture with no obvious cause

Corrosion on the bead seat, where the tyre seals against the rim, can let air escape slowly.

Dull or stained finish

Brake dust baked on over time, or damage from harsh acidic wheel cleaners.

Vibration at speed

Can point to a buckled wheel or balance problem. We check the wheel runs true before refurbishing it.

Visible crack

A crack in the rim or spoke is a safety issue, not a cosmetic one, and needs assessment before anything else.

When should it be done?

Refurbish wheels once the finish has been broken, not just when they look bad. Kerb damage and chips let water and road salt reach the aluminium, and corrosion then creeps under the surrounding paint or lacquer, which is why diamond cut wheels often develop spreading white patches. Wheels losing air slowly through the bead should be done promptly. Many owners refurbish all four together so they match, or have it done before selling or returning a lease car.

  • Kerb rash that has broken through the paint or lacquer
  • White or milky corrosion spreading under diamond cut lacquer
  • Tyres slowly losing pressure through corroded bead seats
  • When fitting new tyres, as the tyres are coming off anyway
  • Before a sale or lease return

How we refurbish alloy wheels

  1. Inspect and identifyWe identify the finish, assess the damage and check each wheel for cracks and buckles, then quote per wheel.
  2. Remove tyres and weightsTyres and balance weights are removed, and tyre pressure monitoring sensors are taken out and kept safe.
  3. Strip or prepareFor a full refurbishment the old coating is stripped; for a cosmetic repair the damaged area is sanded back.
  4. Repair the damageKerb damage is filled and shaped, corrosion is removed and the bead seats are cleaned so the tyre seals properly.
  5. Apply the finishPowder coat is applied and cured in an oven, or paint is applied and cured. For diamond cut wheels, the face is machined on a lathe after the colour coat, then sealed with clear lacquer.
  6. Refit tyres and balanceTyres are refitted with new valves where needed and each wheel is balanced with new weights.
  7. Refit and hand overWheels are refitted and tightened to the manufacturer's torque setting. We recommend a torque check after about 50 miles.

Finishes and materials

Powder coat gives the most durable finish and comes in a wide range of colours. Diamond cut wheels are machined and sealed with clear lacquer to protect the bare aluminium, and painted sections are matched to the original finish as closely as possible. New valves are fitted where needed, and wheels are balanced with new weights before they go back on the car.

  • Powder coat primer and colour coat
  • Clear lacquer for diamond cut faces

If it’s left

Broken finish lets corrosion get under the coating, and on diamond cut wheels it spreads steadily under the lacquer. Corrosion on the bead seat causes slow punctures, and running on low pressure wears the tyre and affects handling. The longer corrosion runs, the more preparation is needed, and badly corroded diamond cut wheels may not have enough material left to re-cut.

About Morden

Morden is a suburban town at the southern end of the Northern line, and grew rapidly after Morden Underground station opened in 1926, with its population rising from about 1,300 in 1921 to over 12,000 by 1931. Much of the housing is 1920s and 1930s semis and terraces, including a large part of the London County Council's St Helier estate, laid out along wide avenues and cul-de-sacs. The town centre is compact and dominated by the station and the Crown House civic tower, with generous parks on either side. It is a family and commuter suburb where most journeys are by car or bus to the station, so the few main roads carry most of the traffic.

Alloy Wheel Refurbishment in Morden: your questions answered

I scraped a roundabout island. Could the wheel be damaged inside?

A light scrape is usually cosmetic, but a harder hit can buckle the rim or crack it. Signs include a vibration or a slow puncture. We check for both before quoting and won't refurbish a cracked wheel.

Can I choose a new colour for my wheels?

Yes, powder coat or paint can be done in almost any colour, and diamond cut faces can be combined with a new colour between the spokes. Remember to tell your insurer, as it can count as a modification.

Can you pick the car up from Morden and bring it back?

Yes, collection and delivery can be arranged when you book. It's handy for a full set, which usually takes 2–3 days. We'll agree timings with you.

What's the difference between diamond cut and powder coat?

Diamond cut wheels have a bright machined face protected by clear lacquer, often with a painted colour between the spokes. Powder coat is a solid, tough colour coating over the whole wheel. Because diamond cutting removes a thin layer of metal each time, a wheel can only be re-cut a limited number of times.

Can you change the colour of my wheels?

Yes. Powder coat or paint can be done in almost any colour, and diamond cut faces can be combined with a new colour between the spokes. Tell your insurer, as a change of wheel colour can count as a modification.

Can you repair a cracked or buckled wheel?

A mildly buckled wheel can often be straightened by a wheel specialist. A cracked wheel is a safety concern, and we won't cosmetically refurbish one; we'll generally advise replacement.

Can I refurbish just one wheel?

Yes, the price is per wheel. Painted and powder coated finishes can be matched closely. Diamond cut finishes vary between manufacturers and age, so a single refurbished wheel can look slightly brighter than the other three, which is why some owners do all four.

Will my tyre pressure sensors be affected?

Sensors fitted inside the wheel are removed before stripping and coating, then refitted with new seals where needed and checked. Cars with indirect systems, which use the ABS sensors instead, may need the system resetting after the wheels are refitted, which we do before handover.
