Photographs before the work, not after

The person authorising spending sees the worn component and its measurement before saying yes. It makes the decision quicker and removes any argument about whether it needed doing.

Wear recorded in millimetres every visit

Pad thickness and tread depth go on the record each time a vehicle comes in, so you can see the rate of wear and plan replacement rather than be told on the day.

In the workshop

How we handle fleet servicing at Hackbridge

Fleet vehicles go into named slots rather than the general queue, and the driver's own report comes into the workshop along with the keys. A driver who says it pulls left under braking, or rattles over speed humps when the van is loaded, has handed us half the diagnosis already, and that note gets tested against what we find rather than filed away. Whoever authorises the work then gets a photograph of the worn part with a measurement next to it and a price beside that, not a description read out over the phone.

Two things here are fixed. Work above the limit agreed with you does not happen without authorisation, however obvious it looks from where we are standing. And a vehicle with a dangerous defect does not go back out to finish the week's jobs, whatever the schedule says, because the responsibility for a vehicle used at work sits with the business and the driver and we are not going to paper over it. What we can do is group work sensibly, so brakes close to the limit are done at the service rather than becoming a second day off the road three weeks later.

Records are the part businesses notice the benefit of later. Every visit adds measurements, advisories and items coming due against that registration, so tyres can be budgeted for the month they are actually needed and an MOT date never arrives as a surprise. One invoice covers the period with each vehicle itemised, which makes allocating cost per vehicle or per department straightforward at the year end. Local collection and delivery keeps a driver from spending half a shift sitting with a car, and repairs carry the 12-month workmanship warranty exactly as they would for anyone else.

The cars we see

Most fleets we look after are mixed: hatchbacks and estates covering motorway miles, pool cars that barely move, and light vans up to 3,000kg on urban delivery work. The vans wear differently. Racking and a permanent load mean rear springs, bushes and tyres take punishment a car never sees, front brakes do far more work than the mileage suggests, and stop-start delivery routes are hard on clutches. If you run anything heavier, tell us the design gross weight when you call and we will confirm what we can test and service before you commit.

Fleet Servicing: what’s involved

For a business, the cost of a vehicle off the road is often greater than the repair itself. A fleet arrangement means your vehicles are booked in as a priority, work is authorised through an agreed process, and you receive one consolidated invoice rather than a pile of individual receipts. Each vehicle keeps its own service and repair history, which helps with budgeting, resale and replacement decisions.

We look after company cars, pool cars and mixed fleets of all makes, to each manufacturer's schedule with the correct oil and OE-matching parts, so warranties are protected on newer vehicles. Employers also have a duty to manage the risks of vehicles used for work, and regular, recorded maintenance is a large part of showing that vehicles are kept safe and roadworthy.

When a fleet arrangement makes sense

Several vehicles to keep on top of

Once a business runs more than a handful of vehicles, tracking service dates, MOTs and repairs by hand becomes easy to get wrong.

Downtime is costing you work

If a vehicle off the road means a missed job or an idle employee, priority scheduling and quick turnaround matter more than the headline price.

Invoices spread across garages

Using whichever garage is nearest leaves you with inconsistent records and invoices in different formats. One garage, one invoice is simpler to reconcile.

Mixed makes and ages

A fleet that includes several manufacturers needs a garage that services each to its own schedule and oil specification, not a one-size approach.

Drivers reporting faults

When drivers report warning lights or noises, you need a clear route to get the vehicle checked and a quote approved without delay.

Newer vehicles under warranty

Servicing to the manufacturer's schedule with matching-quality parts keeps warranties intact without having to use a main dealer.

Preparing vehicles for resale

A complete, consistent service history supports resale or lease return values when vehicles are replaced.

How fleet servicing is scheduled

Each vehicle is serviced to its manufacturer's schedule, based on time or mileage, and we keep track of service and MOT due dates so they can be booked before they lapse. High-mileage vehicles may benefit from an interim service between full services. We agree with you in advance how much work can be approved without a call, and anything above that is quoted in writing first. Repairs and breakdowns are prioritised to get vehicles back to work.

  • Services to each manufacturer's time and mileage schedule
  • MOTs booked ahead of expiry, up to a month (minus a day) early
  • Interim services for high-mileage vehicles
  • Priority slots for breakdowns and driver-reported faults
  • Agreed approval limits for straightforward work

Parts and fluids we use

Fleet vehicles get the same standard as any customer: OE or OE-equivalent parts and oils that carry each manufacturer's approval, including Castrol. That consistency protects warranties on newer vehicles and gives reliable service life, which matters when a vehicle covers high mileage. Where there is a genuine choice of part quality, we explain the options and the cost difference so you can decide what suits the vehicle and how long you plan to keep it.

  • OE or OE-equivalent filters, brakes and service parts

If it’s left

Missed services and MOTs across a fleet add up. An expired MOT can mean a fine, and a vehicle used for work in an unsafe condition puts drivers and the business at risk. Deferred maintenance also tends to become bigger repairs at inconvenient times, with more downtime and less predictable costs than planned servicing.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about fleet servicing

Servicing the whole fleet on one date

Vehicles in the same fleet often cover wildly different mileages. Booking them all in each January suits the diary and nothing else, and it means some are serviced too early while others are well overdue.

Buying tyres on price alone

A loaded van needs the load and speed rating the manufacturer specified and a commercial construction rather than a car tyre in the same size. The saving evaporates the first time one lets go on a motorway.

Sitting on driver reports until the service

A noise reported in month one is usually a small part. The same noise found at the service six months later has generally taken something else with it, and the vehicle is off the road for longer.

Comparing garages on labour rate alone

The hourly figure tells you very little on its own. What a job costs a business is diagnosis time, whether it is right the first time, and how many days the vehicle spends not earning.

Questions

Fleet Servicing: your questions answered

How does invoicing and payment work?

We agree it with you at the start rather than assuming. Most fleets want one consolidated invoice for the period with every registration itemised so costs can be allocated internally. Tell us what your accounts process needs and we will set the paperwork up to match it.

What happens if a vehicle breaks down during the working day?

Ring us and we will give you an honest estimate of when we can get to it rather than a response time we cannot keep. We recover vehicles ourselves, and a fleet vehicle goes into the next available slot once it is here. If the repair is going to run past the day, we will tell you early enough to move work around.

Can you handle accident damage and liveried vans?

Yes, on insurance or self-pay, at the bodyshop on the same site. A van that looks shabby says something about the business, and it is one less supplier to chase. If a damaged panel carries graphics, mention it when you book so the paint and the signwriting can be sequenced properly.

Can you collect vehicles, and is there a courtesy car?

We collect and deliver locally, which usually works out cheaper than paying a driver to sit and wait. Courtesy cars exist but the pool is small, so tell us at booking whether you need one and we will say yes or no there and then. For a fleet, scheduling services into your quieter weeks is often the better answer than a loan vehicle.

How many vehicles do I need for a fleet account?

There is no strict minimum. Businesses with just a few vehicles often benefit from priority booking and a single consolidated invoice. Talk to us about your vehicles and we will suggest what makes sense.

Can you service vehicles still under manufacturer warranty?

Yes. UK competition law means warranties can't be made conditional on dealer servicing. We service to the manufacturer's schedule with matching-quality parts and the correct approved oil, and record everything, so the warranty stays intact.

Do you look after vans as well as cars?

Yes. We service and repair light commercial vehicles alongside cars. On MOTs, Class 4 runs up to 3,000kg design gross weight and anything above that is Class 7. Send us the plated weights for the fleet and we will tell you which vehicles we can test.

How does approval of extra work work?

We agree a process with you at the start, for example a limit under which routine items like bulbs and wiper blades can be done without a call. Anything above that is quoted in writing and waits for your approval.

Can we see each vehicle's history?

Yes. We keep a record for every vehicle with the work carried out, mileage, advisories and items coming due, and can share it with you on request.
