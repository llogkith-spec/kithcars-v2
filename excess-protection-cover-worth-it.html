Is Excess Protection Worth Buying?

It is a separate policy that pays your excess back after a claim. Whether it is worth it comes down to one comparison almost nobody makes at the point of sale.

How it works

Excess protection is its own small insurance policy, usually sold alongside the motor cover. You still pay your excess to the bodyshop or have it deducted from a settlement; you then claim it back from the excess policy.

It does not reduce your excess. It reimburses it, after the fact, on a successful claim.

The comparison worth making

  1. What does it cost per year? Usually a modest annual premium.
  2. What is the most it pays out? There is a cap, and it may be lower than your total excess.
  3. How many claims per year? Often limited to one.
  4. What is excluded? This is the one that matters — see below.
  5. Then divide. Premium against payout. If you would need to claim more than once every few years for it to pay, it probably does not.

What is usually excluded

  • Claims where you were not at fault and the excess is recoverable anyway — which removes a large slice of the cases you imagined it covering.
  • Glass and windscreen claims, which carry their own separate excess.
  • Claims below a stated minimum.
  • Anything your motor insurer declines.

That first exclusion is the important one, and it is rarely explained at the point of sale. On a non-fault claim your excess should come back from the at-fault insurer regardless, so excess protection is doing nothing you were not already entitled to.

When it genuinely makes sense

If your excess is high — because you chose a large voluntary excess to bring the premium down, or because the car or your history forces a high compulsory one — and you would struggle to find that money at short notice, then it is buying certainty rather than value, and that is a legitimate reason.

If your excess is modest and you could cover it without difficulty, it is usually a product sold rather than a product bought.

Your questions answered

Does it cover the whole excess?

Up to the cap on the policy, which may be less than your total excess. Check the cap against compulsory plus voluntary, not just one of them.

Can I buy it separately from my motor insurance?

Yes, standalone excess protection exists and is sometimes cheaper than the add-on.

Does it work on a non-fault claim?

Often it is excluded there, on the basis that your excess is recoverable from the other side anyway. Read that section specifically.

Would it be cheaper just to lower my voluntary excess?

Frequently, yes, and it is the comparison nobody offers you. Ask for the premium with a lower voluntary excess and compare it against the cost of protecting the higher one.
