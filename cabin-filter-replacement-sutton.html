Cabin Filter Replacement: what’s involved

All the air that comes through your car's vents passes through the cabin filter first, whether the fan is on fresh air or the air conditioning is running. A standard pollen filter traps dust, pollen and road grime. An activated carbon filter adds a layer that absorbs some traffic fumes and odours, which is worthwhile if you spend a lot of time in heavy traffic.

As the filter clogs, less air reaches the cabin, so the fan has to work harder and the heater and air conditioning feel weaker. A damp, dirty filter can also harbour mould and bacteria, which is often the cause of a musty smell when you switch the fan on. The filter is usually behind the glovebox or under the scuttle panel below the windscreen.

Signs your cabin filter needs replacing

Weak airflow from the vents

If the fan sounds busy but little air comes out, even on the highest setting, a blocked cabin filter is the most common cause.

Windows slow to demist

Reduced airflow means the windscreen takes longer to clear, which is a safety issue in cold, wet weather.

Musty or damp smell

A smell when you first switch on the fan or air conditioning often comes from a damp, dirty filter or bacteria on the evaporator behind it.

Hay fever symptoms in the car

If sneezing and itchy eyes get worse when you drive, the filter may no longer be trapping pollen effectively.

Dust on the dashboard

A filter that is saturated, damaged or missing lets dust blow straight through the vents.

Leaves or debris in the vents

Debris coming through suggests the filter is missing, torn or wrongly fitted, or the scuttle drain area is blocked.

Whistling from the fan

A restricted filter can make the blower whistle or strain, and adds load to the blower motor over time.

When should it be done?

Most manufacturers recommend changing the cabin filter every 12 months or around 10,000–15,000 miles, often at the annual full service. Cars used in heavy traffic, on dusty roads or parked under trees may need it sooner. Spring, before the pollen season, is a sensible time for anyone with hay fever. If the filter has been missed at recent services, it is usually worth doing straight away.

  • Every 12 months or around 10,000–15,000 miles, depending on the manufacturer
  • At the annual full service on most cars
  • Before the pollen season if you suffer from hay fever
  • Whenever there is a musty smell or weak airflow
  • Sooner for cars in heavy traffic or parked under trees

Cabin Filter Replacement in Sutton: your questions answered

My car's fan only works on the top speed. Is that the filter?

No, that's usually a failed blower motor resistor, not the filter. A blocked filter weakens airflow on every speed, though it can overwork the blower over time. We can diagnose the fault and quote for both if needed.

Does a carbon filter need changing more often?

Usually it's on the same schedule as a standard filter, around once a year or every 10,000 to 15,000 miles. The carbon becomes saturated over time, so if you notice smells returning in traffic before then, it's worth changing earlier.

Can I get the cabin filter done while I'm at work in Sutton?

Collection and delivery can be arranged locally, so you don't need to take time off. Let us know where the car will be and when you need it back.

Is a cabin filter the same as an air filter?

No. The cabin filter cleans air going into the passenger compartment, while the engine air filter cleans air going into the engine. They are different parts in different places with different intervals.

Is an activated carbon filter worth it?

If you drive mostly in town traffic or are sensitive to smells, yes. The carbon layer absorbs some odours and gases that a standard filter lets through. It doesn't make the air perfectly clean, but it is a noticeable improvement for many people.

Will a new filter cure a smell from the air conditioning?

Often it helps, as a damp filter is a common source. If the smell comes from bacteria on the evaporator, the system may also need an antibacterial treatment, which we can quote for.

Does the cabin filter affect the air conditioning?

A blocked filter reduces the amount of cooled air that reaches the cabin, so the air conditioning feels weaker. It doesn't affect the refrigerant, so if the air is warm rather than just weak, the system may need a regas.

Can my car run without a cabin filter?

It will run, but dust and leaves can get into the heater and evaporator, where they are harder to clean out and can cause smells or blockages. We always recommend fitting one.
