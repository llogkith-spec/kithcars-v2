What trim actually is

Trim is plastic, sometimes textured, sometimes painted to match the body. It clips off and is prepared separately, which is why it is the cheapest bodywork on our rate card.

Behind it sits usually only clips and a foam pad, though a mirror housing holds the glass, the motor, the heater element and often an indicator and a camera. All of that comes out or gets protected before any paint goes near the car, and anything electrical is tested again before you collect it.

Trim covers a lot of small jobs that are easy to put off: mirror caps, sill covers, arch mouldings, grilles and spoilers. The main decision is almost always repair against replace, and on a common car a new moulding is sometimes cheaper than preparing and painting the old one. We check the part price before quoting a repair.

How trims get damaged

Four causes account for most of what comes through the door:

  • Mirror housings clipped on gateposts and in car parks.
  • Sill covers scraped on kerbs and ramps.
  • Arch mouldings scuffed on parking posts.
  • Grilles cracked by stones and low-speed shunts.

The thing worth checking. The finish. Textured black trim cannot be polished back the way paint can, so a scuff on a textured moulding is a replace-or-repaint decision rather than a machine-polish one. Anyone who tells you they will buff a textured sill cover is going to leave it shiny in patches.

Repair or replace

Replacement is genuinely competitive on trim in a way it is not on panels. We price the part first and tell you honestly when buying new beats repairing.

How long it takes. Often same day, and next day where a painted part needs to cure.

Mirror housings, which are their own little job

A wing mirror is the most commonly damaged piece of trim on any car, and it is three separate things in one housing: the painted cap, the glass, and the motor and heater behind them. Often only the cap is damaged, and a cap alone is a cheap part that takes paint well.

It is worth asking specifically rather than accepting a quote for a complete mirror assembly. We will tell you which of the three actually needs replacing, and if the glass is intact and the motor still works we will say so.

Your questions answered

Can you repair trim without painting it?

Only where the damage is in the lacquer alone, and we will tell you if yours is one of those rather than selling you a respray. Once it is through the colour, refinishing is the only repair that lasts. On dents there is no paint-free option at all — we do not do paintless dent removal, so the panel is straightened and then painted.

Will the colour match?

It is mixed to the paint code on your car, then sprayed into whichever panel it is mounted on rather than stopped at the shut line. Two cars with the same code are rarely identical after a few years of sun, and blending is what makes the difference invisible instead of nearly right.

Is it guaranteed?

One year on paint, three months on mechanical work, in writing.

Should I claim on insurance?

At £95 to £480 it is worth checking your excess first. Plenty of single-panel jobs cost less than the excess, and paying directly avoids declaring a claim for five years. We will price it either way and we are not on any insurer’s panel, so we have no reason to push you one way.

Do you need to see the car first?

Not to give you a price band — put your registration in the box above. We confirm the exact figure when we see it, because how far the damage goes is the one thing photographs do not show reliably.
