Timing Chain Replacement: what’s involved

A timing chain does the same job as a cambelt, keeping the crankshaft and camshafts in exact step, but it is a metal chain running inside the engine in oil. It is guided by plastic-faced rails and kept tight by a tensioner, usually fed by engine oil pressure. Chains have no fixed replacement interval and are meant to last a long time, but that depends on clean oil at the right level.

Over time the chain's pins and bushes wear, which lengthens the chain and is known as stretch. The guides wear and the tensioner can no longer take up the slack. The cam timing drifts, which the engine management picks up, and eventually the chain can jump a tooth or break. On most engines that means pistons hitting valves. Caught early, a chain replacement is a planned repair rather than an engine rebuild.

Signs of a worn timing chain

Rattle on cold start

A metallic rattle for a few seconds after a cold start, before oil pressure builds in the tensioner, is the classic early sign. If it lasts longer or gets louder, the chain or tensioner is worn.

Cam and crank correlation fault codes

Codes such as P0016 to P0019 mean the engine computer sees the camshaft and crankshaft positions drifting apart, which is typical of a stretched chain.

Engine management light

The light may come on with timing, camshaft sensor or variable valve timing codes, sometimes before any noise is obvious.

Rattle or whine when warm

A persistent rattle at idle or a whine that rises with revs when the engine is warm suggests a worn tensioner, broken guide or a very stretched chain.

Rough running or loss of power

Incorrect cam timing can cause misfires, hesitation, poor starting and reduced power.

Metal or plastic fragments in the oil

Pieces of broken guide can turn up at an oil change, and debris can block the oil pick-up.

Known engine with high mileage

Some engines have a history of chain problems, and at higher mileages we recommend checking chain wear even without symptoms.

When should a timing chain be replaced?

Unlike a cambelt, a timing chain has no scheduled replacement interval. It is replaced when there is evidence of wear: noise, fault codes, or measured timing deviation outside the manufacturer's limits. How long a chain lasts depends heavily on oil. Long service intervals, the wrong specification of oil or running with the level low all speed up wear. Some engines are known for chain problems, so on those we check chain wear at higher mileage or when buying a used car, not just when the rattle starts.

  • No fixed interval; replace when wear is confirmed
  • Check immediately if you hear a rattle on cold start that lasts more than a few seconds
  • Investigate any cam and crank correlation fault codes
  • Consider a check at higher mileage on engines known for chain wear
  • Short oil change intervals with the correct oil help chains last

How we replace a timing chain

  1. Listen and scanWe listen to the engine from cold and read fault codes with dealer-level diagnostic equipment, including stored freeze-frame data.
  2. Measure chain wearWhere the engine supports it, we read live camshaft and crankshaft deviation values and compare them with the manufacturer's limits. On some engines we inspect the tensioner or guides directly.
  3. Written quoteWe explain what we found and quote for the full kit and labour, including whether the engine needs to come out to reach the chain.
  4. Lock the timing and strip downThe engine is locked with engine-specific timing tools and the timing cover and ancillaries are removed. On some engines, such as BMW's N47 diesel, the chain is at the gearbox end and the engine is usually removed.
  5. Replace chain and componentsWe fit a new chain, guides and tensioner, and replace sprockets, variable valve timing units or oil pump drive parts where worn or included in the kit.
  6. Seals, gaskets and oilAll gaskets and seals disturbed are renewed, and the engine is refilled with the correct specification oil and a new filter.
  7. Verify timing and resetThe engine is turned by hand to confirm the timing, then fault codes are cleared and any adaptation values reset with diagnostic equipment.
  8. Road test and handoverWe run the engine from cold, recheck the live timing data, road test and explain the work, with advice on oil change intervals.

Timing Chain Replacement in Coulsdon: your questions answered

Is it safe to drive from Coulsdon to Hackbridge with a chain rattle?

A brief rattle on cold start usually means the drive is fine, but it should be checked soon. A constant rattle, misfire or timing warning light means it is safer to have the car recovered. If in doubt, call us and describe the noise.

My car was bought in Surrey and I've moved to Coulsdon. Is the chain worth fixing given the ULEZ?

If the car is ULEZ compliant, a chain repair is usually far cheaper than replacing it with another compliant car. If it isn't compliant, you pay the daily charge in Coulsdon, and we can talk through whether the repair makes sense for you.

Will the chain rattle go away if I change the oil?

Fresh oil sometimes quietens a rattle for a while, but it doesn't undo wear that has already happened. If the chain has stretched, the noise usually returns. We check the timing data to see how far it has gone.

Aren't timing chains supposed to last the life of the engine?

Many do, but it relies on regular oil changes with the right oil. Chains wear gradually, and some engine designs are known to wear chains or tensioners early. There is no service interval, so replacement is based on symptoms and measurements.

Is the Peugeot and Citroën 1.2 PureTech a timing chain engine?

No. The three-cylinder 1.2 PureTech uses a timing belt that runs in oil, known as a wet belt. It has a replacement interval and its own common faults, so it is handled as a cambelt job rather than a chain replacement.

Which engines are known for timing chain problems?

Well-known examples include BMW's N47 diesel, early VW Group 1.2 and 1.4 TSI petrol engines, early 1.8 and 2.0 TFSI engines, and the 1.6 turbo petrol used by BMW MINI, Peugeot and Citroën. Plenty of these run fine, so we diagnose each car rather than assume.

Can I keep driving with a timing chain rattle?

A brief rattle on cold start can go on for a while, but it tells you wear has begun. A rattle that lasts longer, a warning light or timing fault codes mean you should book in soon. The cost of waiting can be the engine.

What is the BMW N47 problem?

The N47 diesel has its timing chain at the back of the engine, next to the gearbox. Chain and guide wear there causes a rattle, often heard from the rear of the engine, and fixing it usually means removing the engine. We quote these as a complete job with all chains, guides and tensioners.
