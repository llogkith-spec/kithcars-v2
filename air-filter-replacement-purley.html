Air Filter Replacement: what’s involved

Every litre of fuel your engine burns needs thousands of litres of air, and the engine air filter stops the dust, grit and debris in that air from reaching the cylinders and turbocharger. It is a pleated paper or synthetic element in a sealed box, usually at the front of the engine bay. As it traps dirt it slowly becomes more restrictive, especially in dusty conditions or heavy traffic.

On modern fuel-injected engines, a dirty filter mostly costs you performance rather than fuel, as the engine management adjusts fuelling to the air it measures. The bigger risk is a damaged or badly seated filter, which lets fine dust past to wear the cylinders and turbo, and to contaminate the mass airflow sensor, which the engine relies on to measure air accurately.

Signs your air filter needs replacing

Visibly dirty filter

A filter that is grey or black across the pleats, or has leaves and debris in it, is due. A little discolouration on the intake side is normal.

Sluggish acceleration

A heavily restricted filter can reduce power, most noticeable when accelerating hard or climbing hills, because the engine can't draw in enough air.

Past the service interval

If the filter wasn't changed at the last full or major service, or the history doesn't show when it was done, it is worth replacing.

Mass airflow sensor faults

Dust getting past a damaged filter can contaminate the airflow sensor, causing hesitation, flat spots and fault codes.

Unusual intake noise

A whistling or sucking sound from the airbox can point to a poorly sealed filter, a split intake hose or a damaged airbox lid.

Signs of water or oil

A damp or oily filter suggests water getting into the intake or crankcase breather problems, which need checking rather than just a new filter.

When should it be done?

Most manufacturers specify a new air filter somewhere between 20,000 and 40,000 miles or every two to four years, and it is often part of a full or major service. Check your schedule, as intervals vary widely by engine. Cars driven on dusty roads, near building sites or in constant stop-start traffic may need it sooner. We inspect the filter at every service and will tell you honestly if it still has life left.

  • Typically every 20,000–40,000 miles, depending on the manufacturer
  • Often every 2–4 years on low-mileage cars
  • Sooner in dusty conditions or heavy urban traffic
  • Whenever the filter is damaged, wet or oily

How we replace an air filter

  1. Check-inWe confirm the engine and the correct filter part number, as some models use different filters for different engine versions.
  2. Open the airboxWe release the clips or screws on the airbox lid, removing covers or intake pipes first on engines where access is tighter.
  3. Inspect the old filterWe look at the dirt pattern and check for oil, water or signs of dust bypassing the seal, which can point to other problems.
  4. Clean the airboxWe remove leaves and debris from the airbox and check the housing and seals for cracks or distortion.
  5. Fit the new filterThe new filter is fitted with its seal seated all the way round, and the lid and any pipes are secured correctly.
  6. Check and hand overWe start the engine, check for fault codes if needed and confirm the intake is sealed, then show you the old filter.

Parts we fit

We fit OE-equivalent air filters from suppliers such as MANN, Mahle and Bosch, which supply filters to many vehicle manufacturers. These match the original for size, pleat area, seal design and filtration efficiency. Cheap filters with thin media or poor seals can let dust through, which is a false economy on turbocharged engines. We don't fit oiled performance filters as a routine replacement, as excess oil can contaminate airflow sensors.

If it’s left

Leaving a filter too long usually means gradually reduced performance rather than sudden failure. The real risk comes if the filter tears, collapses or stops sealing, because unfiltered dust then reaches the engine and turbocharger and wears them from the inside. It can also contaminate the airflow sensor, which is a more expensive part than the filter.

Air Filter Replacement in Purley: your questions answered

Does the air filter affect how my car copes with Purley's steep hills?

It can. The engine needs the most air when it's working hardest, so a restricted filter is most noticeable pulling uphill with a load. A new filter restores lost performance rather than adding any, so if the car is still sluggish afterwards we'll look for another cause.

Is it worth changing the filter early if I drive through Purley Cross every day?

Changing it before the interval isn't usually necessary, but checking it more often is sensible. We look at it at each service and recommend a change only when its condition calls for it.

Can you check for water in the intake after I've driven through floodwater?

Yes. We'd check the air filter and airbox for water and silt first. If there's any sign that water reached the engine, stop driving and call us, as that needs proper investigation rather than just a new filter.

Will a new air filter improve my fuel economy?

On older carburettor engines it could make a noticeable difference. On modern fuel-injected cars the engine management adjusts to the air it measures, so a new filter mainly restores lost performance, with little change to fuel use. We won't promise mpg gains we can't back up.

Is the air filter the same as the pollen filter?

No. The engine air filter cleans the air going into the engine. The cabin or pollen filter cleans the air coming through the vents into the car. They are separate parts with separate intervals.

Can an air filter be cleaned and reused?

Standard paper filters should be replaced, not cleaned. Blowing them out with compressed air can damage the media and let dust through. Only filters designed to be washable should be cleaned.

Can I just change it myself?

On many cars it is a simple job, but on others the airbox is buried under covers and pipes. If you do it yourself, make sure the seal is seated properly and the lid clips are fastened, as a poor seal defeats the point.

Do performance air filters help?

On a standard engine, gains are usually very small. Oiled cotton filters can shed oil onto the airflow sensor if over-oiled, and some filter less finely than a quality paper element. For a daily car we recommend the OE-equivalent filter.
