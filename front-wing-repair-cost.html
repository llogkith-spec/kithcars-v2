What front wing actually is

Front wing is pressed steel on most cars, aluminium on a growing number. It unbolts. That single fact makes it one of the cheaper panels on the car to deal with, and it is why a wing and a rear quarter are not comparable jobs.

Behind it sits the arch liner, the indicator repeater on older cars, and on one side the washer bottle and often part of the air intake. All of that comes out or gets protected before any paint goes near the car, and anything electrical is tested again before you collect it.

The wing is bolted rather than welded, and that changes the economics completely. If it is beyond straightening, a replacement bolts straight on and the panel gaps can be set properly on refit. None of the welded panels on the car give you that option, which is why a wing that looks as bad as a quarter costs considerably less to put right.

How front wings get damaged

Four causes account for most of what comes through the door:

  • Clipping gateposts and wing mirrors, which creases the top edge.
  • Supermarket car parks, which dent the flat face at door height.
  • Kerbing at an angle, which damages the lower arch lip.
  • Corrosion along the arch lip and the bottom seam on older cars.

The thing worth checking. The A-pillar shut line. If the impact has pushed the back edge of the wing, check whether the door still opens and closes cleanly before anyone calls it a wing-only job. A door that catches means the damage went past the panel.

Repair or replace

Because it unbolts, replacement is a genuine option on a wing in a way it is not elsewhere. On a common car a new wing plus paint is sometimes cheaper than hours of straightening, and we will price both rather than assuming.

How long it takes. Usually two days: off the car, repaired or replaced, painted, blended into the door and bumper, refitted and the gaps set.

Steel or aluminium, and why it changes the bill

A growing number of cars use aluminium wings to save weight, and an aluminium panel is a different repair. It needs different filler, different preparation, and it has to be isolated from steel tooling and steel dust, because steel particles left in aluminium start a corrosion reaction that shows up as blistering months after the car left the workshop.

There is no surcharge on our rate card for aluminium. There is a difference in how it is handled, and a shop that treats the two the same is the reason some repairs bubble a year later.

Your questions answered

Can you repair front wing without painting it?

Only where the damage is in the lacquer alone, and we will tell you if yours is one of those rather than selling you a respray. Once it is through the colour, refinishing is the only repair that lasts. On dents there is no paint-free option at all — we do not do paintless dent removal, so the panel is straightened and then painted.

Will the colour match?

It is mixed to the paint code on your car, then sprayed into the front bumper, the bonnet and the leading edge of the front door rather than stopped at the shut line. Two cars with the same code are rarely identical after a few years of sun, and blending is what makes the difference invisible instead of nearly right.

Is it guaranteed?

One year on paint, three months on mechanical work, in writing.

Should I claim on insurance?

At £140 to £540 it is worth checking your excess first. Plenty of single-panel jobs cost less than the excess, and paying directly avoids declaring a claim for five years. We will price it either way and we are not on any insurer’s panel, so we have no reason to push you one way.

Do you need to see the car first?

Not to give you a price band — put your registration in the box above. We confirm the exact figure when we see it, because how far the damage goes is the one thing photographs do not show reliably.
