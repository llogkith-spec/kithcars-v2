Selespeed, narrow terraces and older Alfas in Thornton Heath

Thornton Heath's side streets, around Bensham Manor and off the A23 Thornton Road, are mostly narrow Victorian terraces with parking on both sides, leaving room for one car at a time. Driving there means constant stopping, pulling in to let others pass and moving off again. For an older Alfa Romeo with the Selespeed automated manual gearbox, that kind of low-speed start-stop work puts the system under steady load.

Selespeed uses a hydraulic pump and actuator to operate a conventional clutch and change gear. The pump and actuator are its known weak points. Owners may notice the pump running for longer than usual, slow or hesitant gear changes, a gearbox warning light, or in the worst case the car refusing to select a gear. Faults often show first in the kind of queues found near Thornton Heath Pond, or when moving off from a tight parking space.

We diagnose Selespeed faults with a scan and live data, check hydraulic pressure and fluid condition, and test the pump and actuator. Some faults are cured by fixing a leak, replacing the fluid or recalibrating the system. Others need a pump or actuator repair. We explain what we find and quote in writing, and we are honest about whether a repair makes sense for an older car's value. We also check the other known weak points on these cars, including front suspension wear, timing belt history and, on 1.9 JTDm diesels, the swirl flaps.

Alfa Romeo Servicing & Repairs: what’s involved

Many Alfa Romeos use Fiat Group engines with clever but sensitive technology. The MultiAir system, used on the 1.4 turbo petrol and the two-cylinder 0.9 TwinAir, replaces the conventional inlet camshaft with an electro-hydraulic actuator, driven from the exhaust camshaft, that uses engine oil to open the inlet valves. It improves efficiency, but depends on clean oil of the right specification at the correct level.

The JTDm diesels are strong engines but, like most modern diesels, rely on a working EGR valve and diesel particulate filter, both of which suffer on short journeys. Alfas also have their own wear points in suspension and gearboxes. Servicing with an independent garage to Alfa Romeo's schedule, using parts of matching quality, keeps your manufacturer warranty valid under the Block Exemption rules.

Common Alfa Romeo faults we fix

MultiAir actuator failure

Misfires, rough idle, a flashing engine light or limp mode on the 1.4 MultiAir often trace to the actuator unit. We check oil level and condition first, then test the unit before recommending replacement.

TwinAir rough running

The 0.9 TwinAir in the MiTo uses the same MultiAir system and is sensitive to oil condition. Rough running and MultiAir fault codes need proper diagnosis rather than replacing parts on a guess.

2.0 JTDm DPF and EGR problems

Giulietta and 159 2.0 JTDm diesels used mainly around town can block their DPF and EGR, causing warning lights, loss of power and limp mode.

Rising oil level on JTDm diesels

Repeated interrupted DPF regenerations can dilute the engine oil with diesel, raising the level and triggering an oil change warning early.

1.9 JTDm swirl flap faults

Older 147, 156, 159 and GT 1.9 JTDm 16v engines have inlet swirl flaps that can stick or fail, causing flat spots, fault codes and in bad cases engine damage.

2.2 JTS timing chain stretch

The 2.2 JTS petrol in the 159, Brera and Spider is known for timing chain stretch, showing as a rattle and camshaft timing fault codes.

TCT and Selespeed gearbox faults

The TCT dual-clutch gearbox in the MiTo and Giulietta can give jerky engagement or gearbox warnings, while older Selespeed units are known for pump and actuator problems.

Front suspension wear

159 and Brera front wishbones and bushes wear, causing knocks, vague steering and uneven tyre wear.

When should your Alfa Romeo be serviced?

Alfa Romeo typically specifies a service once a year, with a mileage limit that varies by model and engine, whichever comes first. Many Alfas display a service countdown, but the time limit still applies if you do low mileage. MultiAir and TwinAir engines benefit from oil changes on time and regular level checks in between. Timing belts on engines such as the 1.4 MultiAir and 2.0 JTDm must be changed at the specified mileage or age, whichever comes first.

  • Oil service: typically every 12 months, or sooner if your engine's mileage limit comes first
  • MultiAir and TwinAir: keep oil changes on time and check the level between services
  • Timing belt (1.4 MultiAir, 2.0 JTDm and others): at the specified mileage or age, whichever comes first
  • Brake fluid: typically every 2 years
  • MOT: first at 3 years, then every year

Alfa Romeo Servicing & Repairs in Thornton Heath: your questions answered

Do you still work on the Selespeed gearbox in older Alfas?

Yes. We diagnose Selespeed faults with a scan and live data, test the pump and actuator, and quote for the repair. Some faults are simple, such as a leak or low fluid.

My Alfa 147 will not select a gear on my Thornton Heath street. What should I do?

Do not keep trying, as it can drain the battery and strain the pump. We can arrange collection and diagnose the fault once the car is with us.

Is it worth repairing an older Alfa Romeo 156?

Often yes, if the body and engine are sound. We give an honest view of the car's overall condition and the cost of the repair, so you can decide.

Will servicing my Alfa Romeo with an independent garage affect the warranty?

No, provided the car is serviced to Alfa Romeo's schedule using parts of matching quality and the work is recorded. This is protected under the Block Exemption rules, which the UK retains through the Motor Vehicle Block Exemption Order. Warranty repairs and recalls are still carried out free of charge by an Alfa Romeo dealer, so we will tell you if something looks like a warranty or recall matter.

What oil does my Alfa Romeo need?

Most Alfas specify oil to a Fiat 9.55535 standard, with the exact grade depending on the engine. MultiAir and TwinAir engines are particularly sensitive to the right oil, so we check your engine's requirement rather than using a generic grade.

Is a MultiAir fault always the actuator?

No. Low or degraded oil, a faulty oil pressure or temperature sensor, ignition faults and wiring can all produce similar symptoms. We test before replacing the actuator, which is the more expensive part.

When does my Alfa's timing belt need changing?

It depends on the engine, but belts have both a mileage and an age limit, and the age limit often catches out low-mileage cars. If there is no record of the belt being changed, we recommend treating it as due.

Can you fix TCT gearbox problems?

We diagnose TCT faults with a scan and live data, then carry out clutch adaptations or repairs as needed. Some faults are cured by recalibration, others need clutch or actuator parts, and we quote before starting.
