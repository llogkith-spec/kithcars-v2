The ten stages

  1. Estimate. The damage is assessed and written up with the method, the parts and the labour times. This is also where repair against replace is decided.
  2. Strip-down. Panels, trim, lights and anything electrical come off. This is where hidden damage appears and where the estimate sometimes changes.
  3. Metalwork. The panel is straightened, or a replacement is fitted and the gaps set. Done in stages, because metal forced back too fast never sits flat again.
  4. Filler and flatting. The small difference the metalwork could not recover is filled and flatted level. Thin, over sound metal.
  5. Primer. Sealing the repair and giving the colour something consistent to sit on. Flatted again once it has cured.
  6. Masking. Everything that is not being painted is covered, and the blend areas on the neighbouring panels are prepared.
  7. Colour. Mixed to your paint code, sprayed in coats, and faded out into the adjacent panels rather than stopped at the shut line.
  8. Lacquer and bake. Clear coat over the whole area, then baked so it cures properly. This cannot be rushed and it is why bodywork takes days.
  9. Flatting and polishing. Any texture in the fresh lacquer is flatted out and the panel polished to match the finish of the rest of the car.
  10. Refit and check. Everything back on, gaps set, electrics tested, sensors checked and recalibrated where the job needs it.

Where the time goes, and why it cannot be compressed

People are often surprised that a day of work takes three days. Most of the difference is curing. Filler, primer and lacquer each need time to harden before the next stage can touch them, and a repair pushed through faster than the materials allow shrinks afterwards.

That is what causes a repair to look perfect at handover and show a faint outline of the repair area a few months later. The shape was right; the materials had not finished moving.

The corners that get cut, and how to spot them

Almost everything that separates a good repair from a cheap one is invisible on the day you collect the car.

  • Not blending. Paint stopped at the shut line instead of faded into the next panel. Looks fine indoors, shows as two shades in daylight.
  • Filler instead of metalwork. Quicker, and it cracks along the edges as the panel flexes.
  • Skipping corrosion protection. Cutting and welding destroys the factory coatings. If they are not rebuilt, it rusts from the inside out.
  • Masking glass instead of removing it. Leaves a paint edge at the rubber that shows the moment the seal moves.
  • Not recalibrating sensors. Nothing visible at all, which is precisely the problem.

If you are comparing two quotes with a big gap between them, the useful question is not "why are you more expensive". It is "what is included that the other one has not listed". Ask both.

Your questions answered

How long will my car be off the road?

A localised scuff repair is often same or next day. A panel off, painted and refitted is two to three days. Multiple panels, or anything structural, is longer and we will tell you the real figure rather than an optimistic one.

Can I see the car part way through?

Yes. Ask when you book it in and we will arrange it. We would rather you saw the strip-down than wondered about it.

Do you use genuine parts?

If that is what you want and the insurer authorises it. Where they will only authorise matching-quality equivalents, you can pay the difference and we will show you both figures.

What happens if you find more damage?

We stop, photograph it, and tell you before doing anything. On a claim that means a supplementary to the insurer. Paying privately, it means a revised figure agreed with you first. Either way you are not presented with a bill you did not expect.
