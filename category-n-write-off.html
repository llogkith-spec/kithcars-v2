Category N Write-Off: What It Actually Means

Category N means your insurer decided the repair cost more than the car was worth to them. It does not mean the car is unsafe, and it does not mean the damage is structural. Plenty of Cat N cars are back on the road and perfectly sound.

The short answer

  • N is for non-structural. The chassis and crumple zones are intact.

  • You can usually buy it back — but say so before you accept the settlement.

What the categories mean

Since October 2017 insurers have used four categories. The first two end the car’s life. The second two do not.

CategoryWhat it means
AScrap only. The entire vehicle is crushed, parts included.
BThe body shell is crushed. Serviceable parts may be removed and reused.
SStructurally damaged but repairable. Chassis, crumple zones, suspension mounts.
NNot structurally damaged. Repairable. Cosmetic, electrical or mechanical.

N stands for non-structural, not for the severity of the bill. A car with a minor crumpled wing can be a Cat N if the car was only worth £900 to begin with. A car with £6,000 of damage might not be written off at all if it is worth £40,000.

Why cheap cars get written off for small damage

The decision is arithmetic, not engineering. The insurer compares the repair cost plus any hire and storage against the car’s market value less what the salvage is worth. If repairing costs more, they write it off and pay you the value.

This is why a ten-year-old hatchback with a damaged door, wing and mirror gets written off while the same damage on a three-year-old car is simply repaired. The damage is identical. The value either side of it is not.

Can you keep the car?

Usually, yes. Ask the insurer for a buy-back, sometimes called retaining the salvage. They pay you the car’s value less what they would have got from the salvage firm, and you keep the vehicle.

  1. Say you want to retain it before you accept the settlement. Once the car has gone to salvage it is gone. Say it at the first call.
  2. Get the buy-back figure. Your settlement drops by roughly what the salvage was worth. Ask for both numbers.
  3. Get a proper repair estimate first. We will quote the real job. Compare that against what you keep after the deduction.
  4. Expect the Cat N to be recorded. It stays on the vehicle’s history permanently. Any HPI check will show it.

What it does to the value

A recorded Cat N typically knocks a meaningful amount off what the car will later sell for, and the effect is proportionally harsher on cheaper cars. Some buyers will not consider one at all, and some finance and insurance providers are more awkward about them.

It is still often worth keeping a Cat N you intend to drive for years. It is rarely worth keeping one you plan to sell soon, because you take the value hit without getting the use.

Repairing a Cat N properly

There is no mandatory inspection before a Cat N goes back on the road, which is exactly why the standard of repair matters. A Cat N repaired with filler over a crumpled panel and a mismatched respray is the reason the category has a poor reputation.

We repair them the same way we repair anything else: damaged panels straightened or replaced, paint matched to the code and blended into the adjacent panels, everything refitted and the gaps set properly. If the damage touched anything with a sensor behind it, that gets tested before the car leaves.

Your questions answered

Do I have to tell the DVLA?

For Category N there is no requirement to notify the DVLA and you keep the same V5C. Category S is different — that one must be notified and the car is issued a new V5C. The write-off is recorded against the vehicle either way, so it will show on a history check.

Can I insure a Cat N car?

Yes, though not every insurer will quote and some charge more. It is worth getting a quote before you commit to the buy-back, so the running cost is in front of you when you decide.

Is a Cat N car safe?

Properly repaired, yes — by definition the structure was not damaged. The risk is not the category, it is whether the repair was done properly or patched to look right in photographs. Get it inspected before you buy one.

I think the write-off valuation is too low. Can I argue?

Yes, and people often win. The insurer owes you the market value, not the cheapest figure in a guide. Send them advertised prices for the same model, age, mileage and spec from dealers within a sensible distance, and put the challenge in writing.

Can you tell me if mine is worth repairing before I decide?

Yes. Bring it in or send photographs and we will estimate it properly. That gives you a real repair figure to set against the buy-back, instead of guessing.
