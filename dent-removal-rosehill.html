What we do instead of dent

Dent repair is a genuine trade, done well by people who do nothing else. It works by pushing the metal back from behind while the original paint stays intact, and when the panel suits it the result is excellent. It also has real limits: a dent on a doubled edge, over a brace, on a panel that someone has already filled, or one where the paint has cracked, is not a dent job at all.

We are a bodyshop with our own spray booth, so we do the repair that has no such limits. The metal is pulled or filled until it is true, the panel is prepared, primed and painted in your car's own colour, and the new paint is blended out into the panels either side so there is no edge where it stops. It costs more than a dent repair would. It also works on every dent, and it looks the same afterwards whichever kind of dent it was.

How we do it

  1. Look at the panel, not just the dent. What is behind it decides the method: a door skin with access behind it is a different job from a bonded quarter panel or a bumper over a bracket.
  2. Tell you what it will take before you commit, including the times it is not worth doing. On an older car a shallow ding can cost more to put right than it takes off the value.
  3. Straighten the metal. Pulled from behind where there is access, pulled from the front where there is not, and filled only where the metal has stretched and will not come back.
  4. Prepare and prime. Flatted true and guide-coated, so a high spot shows up now rather than under the paint.
  5. Paint in your car's colour, mixed to the code and checked against the panel on a test card, then blended into the panels either side.
  6. Check it in the light before you see it. 12-month workmanship warranty, no deposit, paid on collection.

The dents we see most, and what moves the price

Almost every dent that comes through the door is one of five, and knowing which you have tells you roughly where in the range you will land.

The car park door ding

Someone else's door, usually low on your own door skin or along the crease line. Shallow, round, and the paint is often intact. There is normally access behind a door skin once the card and the window regulator come out, so the metal comes back without much drama. This is the cheap end: straightening at the bottom of the range, then the door at £200 plus VAT. The thing that pushes it up is position — a ding right on the swage line is held by the shape of the panel either side and fights you.

The kerbed or clipped bumper

A bumper is plastic, not steel, so it behaves differently: it will often take a shape back with heat, but it also cracks where a panel would simply bend. If the plastic has split, a repair means welding it from behind before any filler goes near it, and that is where the hours go. Bumpers are also the most expensive panel to paint at £240 plus VAT, so a bad one sits at the top of the range.

The trolley or bike handlebar

Small, deep, and usually through the paint. The dent itself is quick. What matters is that bare metal has been sitting in the weather, so there may be rust under the edge of the surrounding paint that only shows once it is flatted back. We will tell you if we find it rather than paint over it.

The crease

A long dent with a sharp line through it, from a post, a gatepost or another car's corner. The metal has stretched along the crease and will not simply come back, so it is pulled as far as it goes and then filled and levelled. Creases are the reason the straightening range runs to £300 plus VAT: a long one on a rear quarter is most of a day's work before paint.

The braced or doubled panel

Dents on a bonnet edge, a tailgate rib or anywhere the panel is doubled over or backed by a brace have nowhere to move. These are the jobs where we will sometimes tell you the improvement is not worth the money, and you are entitled to hear that before you spend it.

When a new panel is the cheaper answer

Repairing is usually cheaper than replacing, but not always, and a bodyshop that never says so is not being straight with you.

A new panel wins when the metal is torn rather than dented, when a crease runs across more than about half the panel, when the damage sits on a bonded or structural section, or when a used panel in your colour happens to be available cheaply for a common car. It also wins on some modern bumpers, where the part is cheap and the hours to repair it are not.

Repairing wins almost everywhere else, and on a car you may sell it has a second advantage: a repaired original panel keeps the car's original structure, where a replacement shows up on an inspection as a panel that has been off. We price both where both are realistic and tell you which we would choose, and why.

Dent repair: your questions answered

Do you do Dent repair?

No. We pull or fill the dent and then paint the panel. dent repair is a specialist trade with its own kit and its own limits, and we would rather tell you plainly that we do not do it than take the car in and find out halfway through. What we do instead is the repair that works on any dent, whatever the paint is doing: straighten it, prepare it, paint it, blend it into the panels either side so there is no hard edge where new meets old.

So what does a dent actually cost?

Two parts. Straightening the dent runs from 70 pounds plus VAT for a shallow ding to 300 pounds plus VAT for a deep crease or one on a braced edge. Painting the panel is then charged at the panel rate - 240 pounds plus VAT for a front bumper, 200 for a door, 150 for a wing. In practice that is £260 to £650 including VAT for most dents. The spread is wide because dents genuinely are: a car park ding and a kerbed crease are not the same job.

Why is a dent more than a scratch, when it looks like less damage?

Because a scratch only needs the panel painting, and a dent needs the metal made straight first. That is the part that takes the time: pulling, levelling, filling where the stretch will not come back, and flatting it true before any paint goes near it. The paint is then the same job it would have been anyway.

Will the colour match?

We mix to your car's own paint code and spray a test card before anything goes near the panel. On metallics and pearls the repair is blended out into the surrounding panels rather than stopping at a hard line, because paint that has weathered for a few years no longer matches the factory code exactly. That blending is included, not an extra.

How long will the car be off the road?

A single panel is usually one to two days, because the paint has to cure properly between stages and rushing that is what makes a repair fail six months later. We give you a date when we have seen the car, not before. A courtesy car is available at 30 pounds plus VAT a day, subject to availability.

Is it worth repairing at all?

Sometimes not, and we will say so. On an older car a shallow door ding can cost more to put right than it takes off the value, and you are entitled to hear that before you spend anything. Where the dent has broken the paint it is a different matter: bare metal rusts, and a rust repair later costs considerably more than the dent does now.
