How we handle full service at Hackbridge

Cars booked for a full service come in first thing and go straight onto a lift, because the checks that matter need the wheels off and the underside at eye level. Pad thickness is taken with a gauge and written in millimetres, disc thickness is compared with the minimum figure stamped on the disc itself, and brake fluid goes on a moisture tester so you get a percentage rather than an opinion. Coolant strength is tested, the battery goes on a conductance tester, and the control modules are scanned for stored and pending codes.

Old parts stay with the car until you have seen them. The air and cabin filters are laid next to the new ones, which settles most arguments about whether they needed doing. There are three things we will not do. We will not tick an item as checked if we could not physically get to it, and the report will say so and why. We will not fit a cabin filter into a housing whose seal or clips are broken without telling you, because it will let unfiltered air and damp straight past. And we will not quietly sign off brake fluid that has tested over the limit; you get the figure and a price to change it.

Full Service: what’s involved

A year changes a car whether it is driven hard or barely used. The air filter fills with dust, pollen and insects until it restricts the airflow the engine was mapped around. The cabin filter takes in leaves and damp and starts to smell. Brake fluid draws moisture out of the air through the reservoir and the flexible hoses, lowering its boiling point. Pads and tyres wear at a rate nobody notices from the driving seat.

The full service deals with the parts that reach the end of their life on a three-month cycle and, just as usefully, puts numbers against the parts that have not. Being told a front pad measures 6mm this year against 9mm last year says far more than being told the brakes are fine. Spark plugs, fuel filters and brake fluid renewal are longer-interval items that belong to the major service, so they are not included here.

Signs you are due a full service

three months since the last stamp

The annual service is a date as much as a mileage. If the book has not been stamped for a year, it is due whether the car has done 3,000 miles or 13,000.

The engine feels flat at higher revs

A blocked air filter restricts breathing most when the engine is asking for the most air. It rarely triggers a warning light, so it is usually found by looking rather than by scanning.

Misted windows or a musty smell

A saturated cabin filter holds damp against the heater matrix and slows airflow, so the screen takes longer to clear and the car smells. When should it be done?

The full service is a three-month job for most cars, or 12,000 miles if you reach that first. Low mileage does not postpone it, because filters, fluids and rubber components age with time and damp rather than distance, and a car doing 3,000 miles a year still needs its year checked. If you have had interim services in between, the full is still due on its own date. Cars on variable schedules should never run past the manufacturer's maximum time limit.

  • Every 12 months or 12,000 miles, whichever comes first
  • Low-mileage cars: annually regardless of the distance covered
  • Long-life or variable schedules: at the car's own service point, but never beyond the manufacturer's time limit
  • A few weeks before the MOT, so anything found can be dealt with in time
  • Soon after buying a used car whose history is unclear, to set a known baseline

  6. Underbody inspectionWe check suspension bushes, ball joints, track rod ends, driveshaft gaiters, exhaust mountings and brake pipes, and look for oil, coolant and fuel leaks with the car raised and the wheels off the ground.
  7. Fluids, battery and electricsBrake fluid moisture content is tested, coolant strength is checked, the battery is tested for condition and the charging output measured. Lights, wipers, washer jets and the horn are all operated.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about full service

A full service covers everything the car needs

It covers the three-month items. Spark plugs, fuel filters, brake fluid renewal and the cambelt run on longer intervals and are quoted separately when they fall due.

Low mileage means you can skip a year

Time-based wear carries on regardless. Fluids absorb moisture, rubber perishes, tyres age and brakes corrode, and a car doing 3,000 miles a year often needs more attention than one doing 15,000.

A new air filter frees up power

It restores the airflow a blocked filter was costing you and no more. Anyone selling a filter as a power upgrade on a standard engine is selling you a feeling rather than a result.

One filter is much the same as another

Cabin filters come in particle and activated carbon versions, and a frame that does not seal in the housing lets air bypass the element entirely. Fit is as important as the filter media.

Questions

Full Service: your questions answered

Can I choose which brand of parts you fit?

Yes, within reason. We fit OE or OE-equivalent as standard and can source genuine manufacturer parts if you prefer, which usually costs more and occasionally takes an extra day. Tell us when you book and the quote will show both options so you can compare before deciding.

Does the 3-month workmanship warranty apply to servicing?

It applies to the work we carry out. If something we fitted or adjusted is not right, we put it right at no labour cost within three months. It does not cover a separate component failing later, or wear items reaching the end of their normal life, and we would rather be clear about that up front.

How long do you need the car and how far ahead should I book?

Plan on dropping it in the morning and collecting it that afternoon. A few days' notice is usually enough, more if you want a specific day or need parts ordering for the same visit. If the trip over is the inconvenient bit, we will pick the car up locally and return it when it is finished.

Will servicing here affect a lease, PCP or used-car warranty?

Servicing to the manufacturer's schedule with matching-quality parts and the correct oil keeps a manufacturer warranty intact under the block exemption rules. Lease and PCP agreements are contracts, so check yours for any wording about where work is carried out. Used-car warranties normally require a documented service history, which is exactly what our invoice and stamp provide.

Does a full service include spark plugs?

No. Plugs are a longer-interval item and belong to the major service, because most modern plugs are specified somewhere between 20,000 and 60,000 miles depending on the type fitted. We check the record and tell you when yours are due rather than changing them early.

I have had two interim services this year. Do I still need a full one?

Yes. Interims keep the oil fresh and catch wear in between, but they do not renew the air filter or include the wheels-off measurements. The full service is the one that completes the manufacturer's three-month schedule and keeps the history intact.

My car only does 3,000 miles a year. Is an annual service really necessary?

It is, and low-mileage cars often need more attention rather than less. Brake fluid absorbs moisture whether the car moves or not, rear discs corrode when the brakes are never worked hard, tyres age and crack, and condensation collects in an engine that rarely gets hot.

Will you check the cambelt at a full service?

We check the interval for your engine and the service record, then tell you when the belt is due and roughly what it will cost. We do not inspect belts by eye and declare them fine, because a belt can look serviceable shortly before it fails. Replacement is a separate job with its own quote.

Will a service cure a rough-running engine?

Sometimes, if the cause is something the service renews, such as a heavily blocked air filter. More often a misfire, hesitation or warning light is a fault that needs diagnosing properly. We will tell you honestly which of the two you are looking at rather than selling a service as a repair.
