Category S Write-Off: Structural Damage

S means something that carries load was damaged — chassis, crumple zone, suspension mounting. It is repairable. It is also the category where how it was repaired matters more than anything else.

The short answer

  • S means something that carries load was damaged.
  • Crumple zones work once, and only if rebuilt as designed.
  • Must be notified to the DVLA; the car gets a new V5C.
  • Buying one? Pay for a structural inspection. The advert tells you nothing.

What counts as structural

Not the panels you can see. The structure is what holds the car together and what is designed to deform in a crash to protect you.

  • Chassis legs and the front or rear crash structure.
  • Crumple zones designed to collapse in a controlled way.
  • Suspension mounting points and subframe mounts.
  • The A, B and C pillars, the sills and the floor pan.
  • Anything welded into the shell that carries load.

A rear quarter panel is welded to the shell but is not, on its own, structural. A sill is. That distinction is what separates an S from an N.

Why it matters more than N

Crumple zones only work once, and they only work as designed if they are rebuilt as designed. A structure that has been straightened without being measured, or welded with the wrong method, can look perfect and behave differently in the next impact.

That is the real reason a Cat S carries more stigma and more value loss than a Cat N. It is not that the damage was worse in a general sense — it is that the part that protects you was involved.

The DVLA step

Category S must be notified to the DVLA, and the vehicle is issued a new V5C. That is the practical difference in paperwork from a Cat N, which keeps its original V5C.

Either way the category is recorded against the vehicle permanently and will appear on any history check, so neither is something that quietly goes away.

What a proper structural repair involves

  1. Measured, not eyeballed. The shell is measured against manufacturer dimensions before and after, on a jig or with an electronic measuring system.
  2. Pulled on a jig. The structure is drawn back to the correct dimensions under controlled tension rather than forced.
  3. Sections replaced where specified. Some structural parts are designed to be cut at defined points and replaced, not straightened.
  4. Welded to the right method. Modern high-strength steels and aluminium need specific techniques. The wrong weld weakens the area around it.
  5. Corrosion protection restored. Cutting and welding destroys the factory coatings. They have to be rebuilt or the repair rusts from inside.

Buying one

A properly repaired Cat S can be a sound car at a real discount. A badly repaired one is a car you do not want at any price, and you cannot tell which is which from the advert.

Pay for an independent inspection, specifically of the structure rather than a general once-over. Ask for the repair invoices. Look at panel gaps on both sides and at whether the car tracks straight. If the seller cannot say who repaired it, that is the answer.

Your questions answered

Is a Cat S car legal to drive?

Yes, once repaired and with a valid MOT. There is no mandatory structural inspection before it returns to the road, which is precisely why the quality of the repair matters so much.

How much does Cat S knock off the value?

More than Cat N, and the effect is sharper on newer and more desirable cars. Some buyers will not consider one at all.

Can I insure a Cat S car?

Yes, though fewer insurers will quote and some charge more. Get a quote before you commit to buying or retaining one.

My car is Cat S. Can you repair it?

We will look at it and tell you honestly. Structural work has to be measured and rebuilt to the manufacturer’s dimensions, and if a job needs equipment or certification we do not have, we will say so rather than taking it on.
