Repair It, or Let Them Write It Off?

The insurer does this sum on their numbers. You should do it on yours, because the two are not the same and your answer is allowed to be different.

The insurer’s sum and yours

Theirs is simple: if repairing costs more than the car is worth less what the salvage fetches, write it off. They are comparing a repair bill against a market value.

Yours is different, because replacing the car is not free and the thing you own is not an average. Your sum is: what you keep after retaining the salvage, minus the repair, against what it would cost to buy something equivalent.

  1. Get the settlement figure. And challenge it first if it looks low — everything downstream depends on it.
  2. Get the retention figure. What you would be paid if you keep the car.
  3. Get a real repair quote. From us, on the actual car, not a guess from a photograph.
  4. Price a replacement. Four or five real adverts for something equivalent, today.
  5. Compare. Retention minus repair against settlement minus replacement cost.

What tips it toward repairing

  • You know the car. Full history, you have owned it years, nothing hidden.
  • The spec is hard to replace — a manual in a sea of autos, a towbar, an unusual trim.
  • You are keeping it for years, so the recorded category never actually costs you.
  • The damage is cosmetic on a mechanically sound car.
  • Replacing it means buying somebody else’s unknown problem.

What tips it toward taking the money

  • It is a Category S and you have no confidence in who would do the structural work.
  • You were going to change the car within a year or two anyway.
  • The repair quote lands close to the settlement — a lot of work to stand still.
  • You need a car now, and retention plus repair takes weeks.
  • You cannot get an insurance quote on it afterwards at a sensible price.

Your questions answered

Can you tell me which to do?

We can give you the one number you are missing — what the repair actually costs, properly estimated. The decision is yours, and we have no stake in it either way because we are on no insurer panel.

What if I repair it and find more damage?

That is the real risk of retention, and it is why the quote should follow a proper look rather than photographs. We will tell you which parts of the estimate are uncertain and why.

Will it ever be worth what it was?

No. A recorded category is permanent and it costs value for the life of the car. Keeping it only makes sense if you are getting use rather than resale out of it.

Can I challenge the write-off itself rather than the value?

Yes, if you think the repair costs less than they assumed. A proper estimate that comes in under their figure is the document that does it.
