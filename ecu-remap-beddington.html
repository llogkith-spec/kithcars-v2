ECU Remapping: what’s involved

The engine control unit (ECU) decides how much fuel to inject, how much boost the turbo makes and when to ignite the mixture, using maps written by the manufacturer. Those maps are often set conservatively so one engine can suit different markets, fuel qualities and power ratings. A remap adjusts them within safe limits for boost, fuelling and timing to give more torque and a stronger mid-range, and on some diesels it can improve economy when driven gently.

A remap only works well on a healthy engine. Worn injectors, a boost leak, a slipping clutch or a tired dual mass flywheel will show up quickly once there is more torque going through them. That is why we check the car first and remap it within the limits of its drivetrain. A Stage 1 remap is software only on a standard car. Stage 2 goes further and relies on supporting hardware upgrades.

Is a remap right for your car?

Turbocharged engine

Remapping delivers worthwhile gains on turbo petrol and diesel engines, where boost can be adjusted. Naturally aspirated engines gain very little.

Flat mid-range or towing

More low and mid-range torque makes overtaking easier and helps vans and cars that tow or carry heavy loads.

Engine in good health

The engine should be free of fault codes, boost leaks and misfires, with a clean service history. We check this before any software is written.

Clutch and flywheel up to the job

A clutch or dual mass flywheel already near the end of its life may slip or fail under extra torque. We check for slip and judder before remapping.

ECU that can be remapped

Some newer ECUs are locked or need to be removed and read on the bench. We confirm what is possible for your exact car before quoting.

Happy to tell your insurer

A remap is a modification and must be declared to your insurer. If you can't or won't declare it, a remap isn't right for you.

When should it be done?

The best time to remap is when the car is in good mechanical condition, freshly serviced and with no outstanding faults. If the clutch, dual mass flywheel or injectors are due for replacement, do that first, and consider an uprated clutch if the gain in torque is large. Check with your insurer before booking, and bear in mind that if the car is still under manufacturer warranty, a remap may affect claims on related parts.

  • After a service, with no fault codes or boost leaks present
  • When the clutch and dual mass flywheel are in good condition, or after replacing them
  • After confirming cover with your insurer
  • Stage 2 only once the supporting hardware is fitted

How we remap an ECU

  1. ConsultationWe discuss how you use the car and what you want from it, confirm the exact engine and ECU, and explain what gains are realistic and safe.
  2. Health checkWe scan all modules for faults and check live data for boost, fuel pressure, injector corrections and misfire counts, and check the clutch for slip.
  3. Fix any issues firstIf we find a fault that would make a remap unsafe, we quote to fix it first. We won't remap a car that isn't ready.
  4. Read and save the originalWe read the ECU through the diagnostic port, or on the bench where the ECU requires it, and keep a copy of the original software.
  5. Write the new mapThe adjusted map is written to the ECU with boost, fuelling, timing and torque limits set to suit the engine, gearbox and clutch. Emissions systems remain active.
  6. Data logging and road testWe road test the car while logging live data to confirm boost, fuelling and temperatures are within safe limits and there is no clutch slip.
  7. HandoverWe explain the changes and remind you to declare the remap to your insurer. The original software can be restored if you ever need it.

Software and supporting hardware

A Stage 1 remap needs no new parts, just a healthy engine. Stage 2 relies on hardware that lets the engine cope with more power, such as an uprated intercooler or intake. Any exhaust changes must keep the catalytic converter or DPF in place. Where the clutch is the limit, we can fit a new or uprated clutch and flywheel from reputable OE suppliers such as LuK or Sachs, depending on what is available for the car. We do not remove DPFs, EGR valves or AdBlue systems.

  • Stage 1: software only on a standard, healthy engine
  • Stage 2: supporting hardware such as an uprated intercooler or intake
  • Uprated clutch and flywheel where torque capacity is a concern
  • Original ECU software saved for reinstatement

If it’s left

There is no risk in not remapping. The risk lies in remapping a car that isn't ready. More torque through a worn clutch, a tired dual mass flywheel or a leaking boost system speeds up their failure. Driving an undeclared remapped car can also leave you without valid insurance. Fixing faults first and declaring the change protects both the car and you.

Questions

ECU Remapping in Beddington: your questions answered

Will a remap help my van pull a full load out of Beddington Lane?

It usually improves torque low down, which is what you feel pulling away loaded. We set the map within what the clutch and gearbox can handle, and we check both first. If the van is struggling because of a fault such as a boost leak, we fix that rather than mapping over it.

My van needs to stay ULEZ compliant. Does a remap change that?

Our remaps keep the DPF, EGR and AdBlue systems fully working, so the emissions equipment the van relies on stays in place. A remap doesn't change the Euro standard the vehicle was built to. We won't remove or disable any emissions equipment.

Can I drop the van off first thing and collect it the same day?

In most cases, yes. We open at 8am Monday to Saturday, and a typical remap with health check and road test takes two to four hours. If the ECU has to come out for a bench read, or we find a fault, it takes longer and we'll tell you before going ahead.

Do I need to tell my insurer?

Yes. A remap is a modification that changes the car's performance and must be declared. If it isn't, your insurer may refuse to pay out after a claim. It's worth getting a quote from your insurer before you book.

What is the difference between Stage 1 and Stage 2?

Stage 1 is a software change on a standard car with no hardware changes. Stage 2 raises the limits further but depends on supporting upgrades, such as a better intercooler or intake, and usually a clutch able to take the extra torque. Any exhaust upgrades must keep the emissions equipment in place.

Will a remap damage my clutch or flywheel?

A good map keeps torque within what the drivetrain can handle, but every clutch and dual mass flywheel has a limit. If yours is already worn, extra torque will find it. That's why we check for slip first and may recommend an uprated clutch.

Can you remove the DPF, EGR or AdBlue while you're at it?

No. Removing or disabling emissions equipment is illegal for road use, can fail the MOT and can invalidate your insurance. Our remaps leave the DPF, EGR and AdBlue systems fully working.

Can the remap be reversed?

Yes, in most cases. We save your original software before writing the new map, so it can be restored later, for example before selling the car. A dealer may still be able to see that the ECU has been reprogrammed.
