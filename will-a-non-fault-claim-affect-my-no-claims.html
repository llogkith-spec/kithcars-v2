Will a Non-Fault Claim Affect My No-Claims?

If your insurer recovers everything from the other side, your discount should survive intact. Your premium can still go up, and those two facts confuse almost everybody.

The short answer

  • Discount and premium are different. You can keep one and still pay more.
  • What decides it: whether your insurer recovers from the other side.
  • A claim still open at renewal is priced as if it were your fault.
  • Ask for a retrospective review once it settles.

Discount and premium are different things

Your no-claims discount is a percentage off. Your premium is the number it comes off. Keeping the discount while the underlying premium rises leaves you paying more, and that is exactly what happens after a non-fault claim.

Insurers price on claim frequency as well as fault. Statistically, a driver who has been involved in one accident is more likely to be involved in another, whoever caused it. Whether that feels fair is a separate question from whether it is what happens.

The bit that actually decides it

What matters is whether your insurer gets its money back from the at-fault driver’s insurer. Until it does, the claim sits on your record as an outlay they have not recovered.

OutcomeEffect on your discount
Fully recovered from the third partyShould be unaffected
Partly recovered, split liabilityUsually reduced proportionally
Not recovered — untraced or uninsured driverOften treated as a fault claim
Still open at your renewalTreated as unrecovered until it settles

That last row catches people every year. If the claim has not settled by renewal, you can be quoted as though it were a fault claim. Ask for it to be reviewed once it settles, and ask for the premium to be adjusted retrospectively.

What to ask, and when

  1. At the start: “Will this be recorded as non-fault, and will you be recovering from the third party?”
  2. At three months: “Has liability been settled? Has my excess been returned?”
  3. At renewal: “Is this claim showing as settled in my favour? If not, why not?”
  4. After settlement: “Please review my premium now the claim has settled and refund any difference.”

Where it goes wrong

  • The claim settles and nobody updates your record. Ask for written confirmation.
  • You move insurer mid-claim and the new one prices it as unresolved.
  • The other driver was uninsured, so there is nobody to recover from.
  • Liability is split, and so is the recovery.

Your questions answered

Should I claim through the other driver’s insurer instead?

It avoids your excess and keeps the claim off your own policy, which can help. You give up the protection of your own insurer if it turns awkward, and you negotiate alone. On a clear-cut claim with liability admitted it is worth considering.

Does protected no-claims cover a non-fault claim?

It protects the discount, yes. It does not stop the underlying premium rising, which is the part people are actually annoyed about.

How long does a claim stay on my record?

You will generally be asked to declare claims for around five years, and most insurers price on the last five.

My premium went up despite non-fault. Can I argue?

You can ask for an explanation and you should. If the claim is settled in your favour and still being priced as unresolved, that is a straightforward correction.
