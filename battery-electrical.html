The whole circuit gets tested

Battery health, starter draw and charging output are all measured on every job, so you are never sold a battery to cure a charging fault or the other way round.

Sleep time given, not skipped

We let the car settle properly before measuring standing current. Pulling fuses while the modules are still awake produces numbers that mean nothing and sends the job in the wrong direction.

Aftermarket wiring checked too

Dashcams, trackers, alarms and phone chargers spliced into a permanent feed are a common cause of overnight drains, and they are among the first things we look at.

Diagnostic time agreed in stages

In the workshop

How we handle battery and electrical repair at Hackbridge

Testing starts with the battery in a known state. A surface charge left by a jump start makes a dying battery read a healthy voltage, so where it matters we charge and settle it first, then test state of health and cold cranking performance with a conductance tester and print the result for you. Cranking voltage is watched while the engine turns over, and alternator output is checked at idle and under load with headlights, heated screen and blower running, because a diode fault or a tired regulator often only shows when the car is asked for real current.

Parasitic drain work is where the time goes, and we are open about that. The car has to be shut up and left until every module has gone to sleep, which on some makes takes the best part of an hour, and only then does a measured draw mean anything. We use a clamp meter or a fused lead in series so the circuit is never broken while the car is awake, then work down from the total draw to the fuse, the circuit and finally the component. Poor earths are found by measuring voltage drop across the joint while it carries current, not by checking continuity, which reads perfectly through a corroded connection.

We do not fit a battery to a car with an untraced drain and call it repaired, because the new one will be flat within the same few days. We do not bridge a fault with a larger fuse or run a permanent live to make something work. Wiring repairs are soldered or crimped properly, sleeved and returned to the loom rather than twisted together under insulating tape. Diagnostic time on an intermittent fault is agreed in blocks before it starts, you hear what we found at the end of each block, and whether to carry on is your call.

The cars we see

Nearly everything now has stop-start, so AGM and EFB batteries make up most of what we fit, on cars from superminis to seven-seaters. Where the battery lives changes the job: boot floors and under-seat mountings on plenty of German saloons and estates mean trim out and a longer fitting time, and some models carry a small second battery to support the stop-start system. Hybrids and electric cars still rely on a 12V battery, and when that fails the car often will not wake up at all, however full the traction battery is.

Battery & Electrical Repairs: what’s involved

The battery starts the engine and keeps the car's electronics alive when it is switched off, while the alternator recharges it on the move. Modern cars ask much more of it than older ones. Stop-start systems restart the engine many times a journey, and dozens of modules draw a small current even when the car is locked. That is why stop-start cars use enhanced flooded (EFB) or absorbent glass mat (AGM) batteries built for repeated cycling.

Many cars also have a battery management system with a sensor on the negative terminal that tracks the battery's age and charge, and adjusts charging to suit. When a new battery is fitted, it often needs registering or coding to the car so the system knows it is new and what type it is. Skip that step, and the new battery can be charged incorrectly, stop-start may stop working and the battery's life can be shortened.

Signs of a battery or electrical fault

Slow cranking

An engine that turns over sluggishly, especially on cold mornings, is the classic sign of a battery losing capacity.

Stop-start stops working

The car disables stop-start when it judges the battery too weak to restart reliably. It is often the first sign of an ageing battery.

Flat battery after standing

A car that goes flat after a few days parked may have a tired battery or a parasitic drain, such as a module that isn't going to sleep.

Clicking but no start

A single click or rapid clicking can mean a flat battery, a poor connection or a failing starter motor. Testing tells them apart.

Warning lights and odd behaviour

Low voltage can set random fault codes and warnings across several systems, and make clocks, radio settings or windows reset.

Dim or flickering lights

Headlights that dim at idle or flicker can point to a weak battery, a charging problem or a poor earth connection.

Swollen case or corrosion

A bulging battery case, leaking acid or heavy corrosion on the terminals means the battery needs checking and usually replacing.

When should it be done?

Car batteries typically last around four to six years, but short journeys, heat, long periods parked and heavy electrical use all shorten that. Have the battery tested if it is more than four years old, before winter, or at the first sign of slow starting or stop-start dropping out. A battery that has been fully flattened several times is likely to have lost capacity, even if it seems fine once recharged.

  • Test annually once the battery is around four years old
  • Before winter, when cold weather exposes weak batteries
  • At the first sign of slow cranking or stop-start dropping out
  • After the battery has been fully flattened
  • Any unexplained flat battery or electrical fault

How we test and replace a battery

  1. Check-in and symptomsWe ask how the car behaves, how old the battery is and how the car is used, as short trips and long periods parked change the diagnosis.
  2. Battery testWe test the battery's state of health and cold cranking ability with a battery tester, not just its voltage, which can look normal on a failing battery.
  3. Starting and charging checkWe check cranking voltage, starter current draw and alternator output so we don't fit a new battery to a car with a charging fault.
  4. Drain and wiring testsFor unexplained flat batteries we measure current draw with the car asleep and trace any excess drain to the circuit or module causing it.
  5. Written quoteWe explain the results and give you a written quote. Only approved work goes ahead.
  6. Fit the correct batteryWe fit a battery of the right type, size and capacity, with AGM replaced by AGM and EFB by EFB or an approved upgrade, keeping memory support connected where needed.
  7. Register and codeWhere the car has a battery management system, we register the new battery and code its type and capacity so it is charged correctly.
  8. Final checksWe reset any windows or settings that need relearning, clear low-voltage codes and confirm the car starts and charges correctly.

Batteries and parts we fit

We fit quality batteries from reputable manufacturers, such as Bosch or Varta, matched to your car's specification for type, capacity and cold cranking current. Stop-start cars need an AGM or EFB battery. A standard battery won't cope with the cycling and will fail early. Where we find a fault beyond the battery, such as a starter motor or wiring problem, we use OE or OE-equivalent parts.

  • Standard lead-acid batteries for cars without stop-start
  • EFB batteries for entry-level stop-start systems
  • AGM batteries for cars with regenerative charging and heavier stop-start demands
  • OE or OE-equivalent starter motors and battery sensors
  • Terminals, earth straps and cabling as required

If it’s left

A weak battery rarely gives much notice before it fails to start the car, and cold mornings usually find it first. Running on a failing battery also puts extra load on the alternator and starter, and low voltage can cause confusing faults across the car's electronics. Testing is quick, and replacing a battery on your own terms beats a breakdown.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about battery and electrical repair

12.6 volts means it is healthy

Resting voltage shows state of charge, not capacity. A battery can sit at 12.6V and still collapse the moment the starter motor draws current, which is why we test state of health rather than trusting a voltmeter.

A new battery cures every gremlin

Low voltage genuinely does cause odd faults across a car. Fitting a battery to silence warning lights without checking the charging system or the standing drain usually just resets the clock on the same problem.

A trickle charge fixes it

Charging will recover a battery that was flattened once. One that has been deeply discharged several times has lost plate capacity for good, and a charge only hides that until the next cold morning.

Any live feed will do for extras

Accessories wired into whatever wire was nearest cause flat batteries and strange faults months later. Anything added should be properly fused and taken from a feed the car actually switches off.

Questions

Battery & Electrical Repairs: your questions answered

How is diagnostic time charged on an electrical fault?

We agree a block of time up front, tell you what we have found at the end of it, and you decide whether to continue. Some faults are found in twenty minutes and others need the car left with us so the problem can be caught happening. You always know where you stand before the next stage begins.

Does the battery itself come with a guarantee?

Batteries carry their manufacturer's own guarantee, and we give you the paperwork and an invoice showing the fitting date, which is what any claim needs. Our 3-month workmanship warranty covers the fitting, the registration and any wiring work. If a battery fails within its guarantee period we will help you put the claim in.

Will I lose my radio code or settings?

Most modern cars are fine, and we keep a memory supply connected where the car needs one. Some still need window one-touch, sunroof or steering angle settings relearning, and we do that before handover. Older audio units with a security code are the one case worth finding the code for before you come in.

My car will not start at all. Can you fetch it?

Yes, we can recover the vehicle to the workshop rather than have you gamble on a jump start and a drive across South London. If it only fails after standing overnight, leaving it where it is until we collect is genuinely useful, because we can measure the fault while the car is still in that state.

Why does a stop-start car need a special battery?

Stop-start restarts the engine many times on every journey and runs the electrics while the engine is off. EFB and AGM batteries are built to handle that repeated cycling. A standard battery fitted in their place wears out quickly and stop-start may stop working.

What is battery registration or coding?

Many cars, including a lot of BMW, Audi, VW, Ford and Mercedes-Benz models, track the battery's age and condition through a battery management system. After fitting a new battery, the car needs to be told it is new, and sometimes its type and capacity, so it charges it correctly. Without this, the car keeps charging the new battery as if it were the old, worn one.

Can I just jump-start it and carry on?

A jump start gets you going, but if the battery went flat for no obvious reason it is likely to happen again. A deeply discharged battery often loses capacity permanently, so it's worth having it tested.

Is it the battery or the alternator?

If the car starts after a jump but goes flat again while driving, or the battery warning light is on, the charging system is the likely cause. If it only struggles after being left parked, the battery or a drain is more likely. We test all three so the right part gets replaced.

Can I fit a cheaper standard battery?

Not on a stop-start car. An AGM battery must be replaced with an AGM, while an EFB can be replaced with an EFB or, on many cars, upgraded to an AGM. Going down a grade usually leads to early failure and electrical faults.
