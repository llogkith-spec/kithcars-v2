Older cars, school runs and the Rose Hill roundabout

Plenty of Rosehill cars are older and have done a lot of miles, and with age come electrical problems that aren't always the battery itself. Corroded terminals, a tired earth strap or a slipping alternator belt can all look like a failing battery: slow cranking, dim lights at idle or a car that goes flat on the way home. We check those before recommending a new battery, because replacing a battery that's fine wastes your money and doesn't fix anything. Battery & Electrical Repairs: what’s involved

The battery starts the engine and keeps the car's electronics alive when it is switched off, while the alternator recharges it on the move. Modern cars ask much more of it than older ones. Stop-start systems restart the engine many times a journey, and dozens of modules draw a small current even when the car is locked. That is why stop-start cars use enhanced flooded (EFB) or absorbent glass mat (AGM) batteries built for repeated cycling.

Many cars also have a battery management system with a sensor on the negative terminal that tracks the battery's age and charge, and adjusts charging to suit. When a new battery is fitted, it often needs registering or coding to the car so the system knows it is new and what type it is. Skip that step, and the new battery can be charged incorrectly, stop-start may stop working and the battery's life can be shortened.

Signs of a battery or electrical fault

Slow cranking

An engine that turns over sluggishly, especially on cold mornings, is the classic sign of a battery losing capacity.

Stop-start stops working

The car disables stop-start when it judges the battery too weak to restart reliably. It is often the first sign of an ageing battery.

Flat battery after standing

A car that goes flat after a few days parked may have a tired battery or a parasitic drain, such as a module that isn't going to sleep.

Clicking but no start

A single click or rapid clicking can mean a flat battery, a poor connection or a failing starter motor. Testing tells them apart.

Warning lights and odd behaviour

Low voltage can set random fault codes and warnings across several systems, and make clocks, radio settings or windows reset.

Dim or flickering lights

Headlights that dim at idle or flicker can point to a weak battery, a charging problem or a poor earth connection.

Swollen case or corrosion

A bulging battery case, leaking acid or heavy corrosion on the terminals means the battery needs checking and usually replacing.

When should it be done?

Car batteries typically last around four to six years, but short journeys, heat, long periods parked and heavy electrical use all shorten that. Have the battery tested if it is more than four years old, before winter, or at the first sign of slow starting or stop-start dropping out. A battery that has been fully flattened several times is likely to have lost capacity, even if it seems fine once recharged.

  • Test annually once the battery is around four years old
  • Before winter, when cold weather exposes weak batteries
  • At the first sign of slow cranking or stop-start dropping out
  • After the battery has been fully flattened
  • Any unexplained flat battery or electrical fault

How we test and replace a battery

  1. Check-in and symptomsWe ask how the car behaves, how old the battery is and how the car is used, as short trips and long periods parked change the diagnosis.
  2. Battery testWe test the battery's state of health and cold cranking ability with a battery tester, not just its voltage, which can look normal on a failing battery.
  3. Starting and charging checkWe check cranking voltage, starter current draw and alternator output so we don't fit a new battery to a car with a charging fault.
  4. Drain and wiring testsFor unexplained flat batteries we measure current draw with the car asleep and trace any excess drain to the circuit or module causing it.
  5. Written quoteWe explain the results and give you a written quote. Only approved work goes ahead.
  6. Fit the correct batteryWe fit a battery of the right type, size and capacity, with AGM replaced by AGM and EFB by EFB or an approved upgrade, keeping memory support connected where needed.
  7. Register and codeWhere the car has a battery management system, we register the new battery and code its type and capacity so it is charged correctly.
  8. Final checksWe reset any windows or settings that need relearning, clear low-voltage codes and confirm the car starts and charges correctly.

Batteries and parts we fit

We fit quality batteries from reputable manufacturers, such as Bosch or Varta, matched to your car's specification for type, capacity and cold cranking current. Stop-start cars need an AGM or EFB battery. A standard battery won't cope with the cycling and will fail early. Questions

Battery & Electrical Repairs in Rosehill: your questions answered

My older car's lights dim at idle. Is it the battery?

It could be the battery, but dimming at idle often points to the alternator, its belt or a poor earth connection. We test the whole starting and charging system so we replace the right part.

Is it worth putting a new battery in an older car?

Usually, yes, if the rest of the car is sound. A battery is a modest cost compared with the hassle of a car that won't start. We'll test it first, and if it's still healthy we'll tell you.

Why does my car start fine after a long drive but struggle after the school run?

Short trips don't fully recharge the battery, so it starts each morning at a lower charge. A healthy battery copes, but one that's losing capacity shows it quickly. A test will tell you how much life it has left.

Why does a stop-start car need a special battery?

Stop-start restarts the engine many times on every journey and runs the electrics while the engine is off. EFB and AGM batteries are built to handle that repeated cycling. A standard battery fitted in their place wears out quickly and stop-start may stop working.

What is battery registration or coding?

Many cars, including a lot of BMW, Audi, VW, Ford and Mercedes-Benz models, track the battery's age and condition through a battery management system. After fitting a new battery, the car needs to be told it is new, and sometimes its type and capacity, so it charges it correctly. Without this, the car keeps charging the new battery as if it were the old, worn one.

Can I just jump-start it and carry on?

A jump start gets you going, but if the battery went flat for no obvious reason it is likely to happen again. A deeply discharged battery often loses capacity permanently, so it's worth having it tested.

Is it the battery or the alternator?

If the car starts after a jump but goes flat again while driving, or the battery warning light is on, the charging system is the likely cause. If it only struggles after being left parked, the battery or a drain is more likely. We test all three so the right part gets replaced.

Can I fit a cheaper standard battery?

Not on a stop-start car. An AGM battery must be replaced with an AGM, while an EFB can be replaced with an EFB or, on many cars, upgraded to an AGM. Going down a grade usually leads to early failure and electrical faults.
