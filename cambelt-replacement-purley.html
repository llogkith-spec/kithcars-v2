When a Purley car's cambelt is due on age, not miles

A good share of the cars we see from Purley are low-mileage models that spend much of the week on a driveway in Riddlesdown or Woodcote, heading out for local shopping, trips to Riddlesdown Common or the occasional run up Purley Way. Owners are often surprised that a car with 25,000 miles on the clock needs a cambelt, but every manufacturer sets an age limit as well, commonly somewhere between four and ten years depending on the engine.

Rubber ages with heat cycles and time. A belt that has sat still for months can take a set, and the tensioner and idler bearings can dry out. When a car like that is finally used for a longer trip, such as down the A23 Brighton Road towards the M25, it is not unusual for an aged belt or bearing to give up. There is rarely any warning, and most modern engines are interference designs, so the valves are at risk. We would much rather change a belt a little early than see a car recovered from the Brighton Road.

Purley's hills add a little extra load too. The steep, winding roads climbing out of the valley ask a lot of the cooling system, and on engines where the belt drives the water pump, a pump that has been sitting unused can start to leak once it is worked hard. We replace a belt-driven pump with the belt as standard and check the rest of the belt drive for oil and coolant contamination.

If you are managing a car for a relative and are not sure of its history, bring whatever paperwork you have and we will piece it together. Without proof of a change, we recommend doing it. Collection and delivery can be arranged if driving through Purley Cross is a chore, and the work is covered by our 12-month workmanship warranty.

Cambelt Replacement: what’s involved

The cambelt is a toothed rubber belt that keeps the crankshaft and camshafts turning in exact step, so the valves open and close at the right moment relative to the pistons. On many engines the same belt also drives the water pump. The belt is guided by a tensioner and idler pulleys, which have bearings that wear at the same rate as the belt.

Most modern engines are interference designs, meaning the valves and pistons share the same space at different times. If the belt snaps or jumps a tooth, the pistons hit the open valves. The usual result is bent valves and often damaged pistons or a damaged cylinder head, and sometimes the engine is not worth repairing. A cambelt rarely gives any warning before it fails, which is why it is replaced by age and mileage rather than when it goes wrong.

Signs your cambelt needs attention

Due or overdue by age or mileage

The most important sign is on paper. If the interval has passed on either time or miles, the belt is due, even if the car runs perfectly.

No record of a change

If you have bought a used car with no invoice showing a cambelt change, assume it has not been done and have it replaced.

Oil or coolant on the belt

Leaking camshaft or crankshaft seals, or a weeping water pump, soften the rubber and can make the belt slip or strip teeth. The leak needs fixing at the same time.

Whining, ticking or rumbling from the belt end

A worn tensioner or idler bearing can whine or rumble from the timing end of the engine. Bearing failure can take the belt with it.

Coolant loss near the water pump

On engines with a belt-driven water pump, a crusty coolant trail or damp patch below the pump means its seal is failing.

Cracks, glazing or fraying

Visible cracking on the back of the belt, shiny glazed teeth or frayed edges seen through an inspection cover mean it is overdue.

Engine will not start after a noise

If the engine turns over faster than normal but will not fire after a slap or clatter, the belt may have broken. Do not keep cranking; have it recovered.

When should a cambelt be changed?

Every manufacturer sets its own interval in both miles and years, and the belt must be changed at whichever comes first. The rubber ages even when the car sits still, so a low-mileage car can be due on time alone. Intervals vary widely between engines, and some have been revised by the manufacturer since the car was new. We look up the current figure for your exact engine code. If your car's history is unknown, the safest approach is to change it now and start the clock again.

  • Typical intervals: 40,000–100,000+ miles or 4–10 years, whichever comes first, depending on engine
  • Change on age alone if the car does low mileage
  • Change straight away if there is no proof it has been done on a used car
  • Replace the water pump at the same time if it is driven by the belt
  • Change early if the belt has been contaminated by oil or coolant

How we replace a cambelt

  1. Confirm the interval and quoteWe check your engine code, the current manufacturer interval and your service history, then give you a written quote covering the belt, tensioner, idlers and water pump where applicable.
  2. Lock the engine timingThe engine is set to its timing position and locked with the correct engine-specific timing tools so the camshafts and crankshaft cannot move.
  3. Strip to the beltEngine covers, the auxiliary drive belt and pulleys are removed, and on many transverse engines the engine mount is removed with the engine supported.
  4. Replace belt, tensioner and idlersThe old belt, tensioner and idler pulleys come off. We check the camshaft and crankshaft seals for leaks before fitting the new parts.
  5. Replace the water pumpWhere the pump is belt-driven, it is replaced with a new gasket or seal while everything is accessible.
  6. Tension and verify timingThe new belt is tensioned to specification, the locking tools removed and the engine turned by hand through two full revolutions to confirm the timing marks line up again.
  7. Refill and bleed coolantThe cooling system is refilled with the correct coolant and bled of air.
  8. Road test and recordWe run the engine to temperature, check for leaks and noise, road test, then record the date and mileage on your invoice and a label in the engine bay.

A cambelt that fails on an interference engine usually bends valves and can damage pistons and the cylinder head. The repair is then a head rebuild or even a replacement engine, often costing several times the price of a belt change. On a non-interference engine the car simply stops, but you still need recovery and the belt fitting.

Cambelt Replacement in Purley: your questions answered

My car hardly leaves the drive in Riddlesdown. Why would the cambelt need changing?

Because the belt has an age limit as well as a mileage limit. The rubber deteriorates over time even if the car barely moves, and a belt past its age limit can fail without warning. We will look up your engine's figure.

How can I find out if a previous owner changed the cambelt?

Look for an invoice or a stamp in the service book that specifically mentions the cambelt, with the date and mileage. Some garages also leave a sticker under the bonnet. If there is no clear proof, the safe choice is to treat it as due.

I look after my parent's car in Purley. Can you collect it and deal with me by phone?

Yes. Collection and delivery can be arranged, and we can send the written quote and talk it through with you by phone before any work starts. Just let us know who we should speak to when you book.

How do I know if my car has a cambelt or a timing chain?

It depends on the engine, and some makers use both across their range. Tell us your registration and we will confirm what your engine has and what its interval is.

Why replace the water pump if it is not leaking?

When the pump is driven by the cambelt, a pump bearing or seal failure can wreck the new belt. Most of the labour is shared, so replacing the pump at the same time costs far less than doing it separately later.

My car has a wet belt. Is that different?

Some engines, including Ford's 1.0 EcoBoost and the 1.2 PureTech used by Peugeot, Citroën and Vauxhall, run the belt inside the engine in oil. These belts can degrade early if the wrong oil is used or changes are missed, and debris can block the oil pick-up. Manufacturers have revised some of these intervals, so we check the current guidance and quote these jobs individually.

Does a cambelt change affect my service history?

Yes, positively. It is one of the first things buyers and dealers check. We record the date and mileage on your invoice so you have proof it has been done.

Can I just replace the belt and not the tensioner?

We do not recommend it. The tensioner and idlers have bearings that have done the same miles as the belt, and a pulley failure can destroy the new belt. Belt manufacturers supply kits for this reason.
