Why We Blend Into the Next Panel

Blending is the difference between a repair you cannot find and one you can see from the far side of a car park. It is also the first thing cut from a cheap quote, because nobody can see it missing until the sun comes out.

What blending actually is

Instead of painting the damaged panel up to its edges and stopping, the new colour is carried a little way into the panels either side and faded out so there is no line where old meets new.

The eye is extremely good at finding a hard edge between two similar colours and very bad at finding a gradual transition. Blending exploits that. It does not make the match better — it makes the difference impossible to locate.

Why stopping at the shut line shows

A shut line is exactly where the eye goes looking. Two panels meeting at a crisp gap invite comparison, and any difference in shade, in how metallic flake has laid down, or in gloss level reads immediately.

It is worst on metallics, worst again in direct sunlight, and worst of all on a car a few years old where the original paint has faded unevenly. All three of which describe most cars we see.

How far the blend goes

A rear quarter is the extreme case, because it meets four other panels and one of them is the roof. Carrying colour up into the roof and fading it out is the only way to make a quarter repair disappear, and it is a large part of why a quarter costs what it does.

The quote difference nobody explains

If one quote is noticeably cheaper than another on the same damage, blending is the single most likely reason. It is extra material, extra masking and extra time on panels that are not damaged, and leaving it out is invisible on the day.

Blending is included in every figure on our rate card. It is not a line item, it is not an upsell, and we do not quote a lower number by leaving it out and then explaining afterwards.

If you are comparing quotes, ask each one directly: "does that include blending into the adjacent panels?" The answers will tell you more than the prices do.

Your questions answered

Does blending mean painting undamaged panels?

Partly, yes — a section of them. That sounds wasteful and it is the opposite: it is what stops the repaired panel standing out against them.

Will the blended area wear differently?

No. It is the same paint and the same lacquer, applied over properly prepared surface, and it is covered by the same one-year paint guarantee.

Can you repair a panel without blending if I want it cheaper?

We would rather not, and we will tell you why. On solid white or a very new car you might get away with it. On a metallic or anything more than a couple of years old, you will see it, and then it has to be done again properly.

Why did my last repair not match, then?

Most commonly because it was not blended. The second most common reason is a code match with no variant check and no spray-out card.
