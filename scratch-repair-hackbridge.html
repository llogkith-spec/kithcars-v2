Scratch Repair for Hackbridge drivers

Why Hackbridge produces the damage it does

Two things are happening to this place at once, and both end up on panels.

The first is the building. The old Felnex estate was about a million square feet of industry and, per the published figures for the Felnex and New Mill schemes, was approved in 2016 for 725 homes plus a supermarket, a surgery, a care home and a bus interchange. New Mill Quarter adds another 440. That is over eleven hundred homes going into a suburb whose central parade is Edwardian and was laid out for a fraction of the traffic, and it brings years of construction vehicles, delivery vans and contractor pickups through streets that were not drawn for them.

The second is where all those cars end up overnight. Sutton Council’s own ward parking survey for Wandle ward, which is Hackbridge, found 67 per cent of residents saying their street has a parking problem, 39 per cent parking on the street, and 74 per cent saying the trouble is on weekdays. A street parked solid on both sides is where mirrors get clipped, where doors get opened into the car alongside, and where a van reversing to a door takes a corner off something.

Which is why the bulk of what comes through our door from a hundred yards away is low-speed and cosmetic rather than dramatic. Scuffed bumpers, creased doors, kerbed wheels, mirror casings. Not motorway damage. Parked-up, built-up, somebody-was-in-a-hurry damage.

Before you decide whether to claim

  • Price it first. A single scuffed panel is often close to a typical excess, and claiming costs you the no-claims discount on top.
  • Check who you are claiming against. If nobody is identified, your insurer has nobody to recover from, so it goes down as a fault claim even though it was not your fault.
  • Notify either way. Most policies require it. Notifying is not claiming, and saying so on the call is worth doing.
  • Do not accept a roadside cash offer before you have a figure. They are reliably below what the job costs, and once accepted the way back is hard.

We will give you the repair number so you can do that sum properly. The other half of it comes from your insurer, and asking them directly is the only way to get it right.

Scratch Repair: what’s involved

Car paint is built up in layers: primer on the metal or plastic, a thin basecoat that gives the colour, and a clear coat on top that provides the gloss and protection. The clear coat is only hundredths of a millimetre thick, so how a scratch is repaired depends on which layer it has reached. Light swirls and marks that sit only in the clear coat can usually be machine polished out with no paint needed.

If your fingernail catches in the scratch, it has usually gone through the clear coat into the colour, or down to primer or bare metal. These need refinishing: the area is flatted, filled if needed, primed, painted in colour matched from your paint code and blended, then clear coated. Key scratches along one side often cross several panels, so we price each area honestly and explain where a smaller localised repair will do.

Types of scratch and what they need

Swirl marks and light hazing

Usually from automatic car washes or dirty wash mitts. These sit in the clear coat and normally polish out.

Fine scratches you can't feel

If your fingernail glides over it, the scratch is usually in the clear coat only and can be polished.

Scratches that catch a fingernail

These have usually gone into the colour or deeper, and need filling and paint rather than polish.

White or grey line in the scratch

Often primer showing through, which means the colour coat has been cut and the area needs refinishing.

Bare metal showing

Shiny metal or orange rust spots in the scratch. This needs repairing soon, before rust spreads under the paint.

Key scratches

Deliberate scratches are often deep and run across several panels, each of which needs refinishing and blending.

Stone chips on the bonnet and front

Small chips down to primer or metal. Depending on how many there are, they can be touched in or the panel refinished.

Scratches on bumpers and plastic trim

Painted plastic needs flexible preparation. Unpainted textured trim can't be polished in the same way and may need a specialist treatment or replacement.

When should it be done?

Repair scratches through to bare metal promptly, ideally within a few weeks, because exposed steel rusts and the rust spreads under the surrounding paint. Scratches in the clear coat only are cosmetic and can be polished out whenever it suits you. If the car has been keyed, report it to the police and check whether your policy covers vandalism before deciding whether to pay privately. Many owners also have scratches repaired before selling or returning a lease car.

  • Bare metal showing: as soon as practical, before rust starts
  • Scratches that catch a fingernail: within weeks, before the edges start to lift
  • Swirls and light marks: whenever convenient, often before a sale
  • Keyed panels: once the police and, if claiming, your insurer have been told
  • Before a lease or PCP return inspection

How we repair scratches

  1. Clean and inspectThe area is washed and inspected under strong light to judge the depth of each scratch, then we give you a written quote.
  2. Choose the methodWe explain whether the scratch needs polishing, a localised repair or a full panel refinish, and why.
  3. Machine polish clear coat marksA cutting compound followed by a finishing polish removes the scratch while taking off as little clear coat as possible.
  4. Prepare deeper scratchesThe scratch is flatted, filled with fine stopper or primer-filler where needed, and the edges feathered smooth.
  5. Colour match and paintPaint is mixed from your paint code, checked on a test card against the car, applied and blended, then clear coated.
  6. Cure and finishAfter controlled curing, the blend area is polished so the gloss matches the surrounding original paint.
  7. Inspect and hand overWe check the repair from different angles and in daylight, then go through it with you along with aftercare advice.

Materials we use

For polishing we use professional cutting compounds and finishing polishes, working the surface as little as possible because clear coat can't be put back once it's removed. For refinishing, paint is mixed to your car's paint code, checked on a test card against the car, and finished with a two-pack clear coat for gloss and durability. Primers are chosen for the surface underneath, including flexible primers for plastic parts.

  • Cutting compounds and finishing polishes
  • Primer-filler and fine stopper for deep scratches
  • Basecoat mixed to your paint code
  • Two-pack clear coat
  • Adhesion promoter and flexible primer for plastic parts

Polishing light scratches out of a panel often takes an hour or two. Refinishing a scratch that has gone through the paint usually needs the car for a day, or up to 2 days where several panels are involved.

If it’s left

Clear coat scratches are cosmetic and won't spread. Scratches to bare steel will rust, and once rust creeps under the paint around the scratch, the repair area and the cost both grow. Plastic bumpers don't rust, but the edges of damaged paint can lift and flake over time.
