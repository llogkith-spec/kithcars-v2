Category A and B Write-Offs

These are the two that are not a decision. A and B cars do not go back on the road, ever, and the only difference between them is whether anything can be taken off first.

The short answer

  • Both mean the car never returns to the road.
  • The only difference: B allows parts to be salvaged, A does not.
  • A car advertised as a runner but marked A or B is a serious problem, not a bargain.
  • The category does not change what you are paid — that is market value either way.

Both end the vehicle. The only thing B allows is salvage of serviceable components — an engine, a gearbox, doors, seats — before the shell is destroyed.

Why Category A exists at all

If B allows parts to be reused, why have a category that forbids it? Because some damage makes the parts themselves unsafe or untraceable.

  • Fire. Heat changes the properties of metal and plastic in ways you cannot see. A component that looks fine may have been weakened.
  • Flood, particularly salt water. Corrosion gets everywhere, including inside wiring looms and connectors, and it keeps working for years.
  • Severe contamination. Chemical spills and some incidents leave components that cannot safely be reused.
  • Total destruction. Nothing worth salvaging.

If a car you are looking at is marked A or B

Walk away, and then think about why it was advertised at all. A Category A or B vehicle cannot legally return to the road. If someone is selling one as a runner, either the identity has been changed or the record belongs to a different car.

That is not a bargain, it is a serious problem: an insurance policy on a vehicle whose identity does not match the record is not worth the paper it is on, and the car can be seized.

A history check costs a few pounds and takes a minute. On any used car that is worth doing; on a car with an unusually low price it is essential. If the check and the seller disagree, trust the check.

If yours has been categorised A or B

There is nothing to repair and nothing to retain. What there is to do is make sure you are paid properly.

  • Check the valuation. They owe you market value, and the category does not change that.
  • Take your belongings out, including anything in the boot, the glovebox and the parcel shelf.
  • Remove the private plate if you have one, before the car goes.
  • Cancel or transfer the insurance and tell the DVLA once the insurer confirms disposal.

Your questions answered

Can a Category B car be repaired?

No. The shell must be destroyed. Only parts may be salvaged, and only by those entitled to do so.

I have been offered Cat B parts. Is that legal?

Salvaging and selling serviceable parts from a Category B vehicle is legitimate. Buying a Category B shell to rebuild is not.

My car was written off as Cat A but only the interior burned.

Fire damage is categorised cautiously for good reason — heat affects structure and wiring well beyond the visible burn. If you think the category is wrong, ask for the engineer’s report and challenge it in writing.

Does the category affect what I get paid?

No. The settlement is the market value of the car before the damage, less any excess, whichever category it ends up in. What the category affects is whether you have the option to keep it.
