How Long Will My Car Be Off the Road?

Most of the time a car spends in a bodyshop, nobody is touching it. Paint has to cure, and on a claim an engineer has to authorise. Neither can be hurried, and both are why a day of work takes a week.

The short answer

  • Most of the time, nobody is touching the car.
  • Curing is chemistry — it cannot be paid to go faster.
  • On a claim the delays are authorisation and parts, not the workshop.
  • A real date beats an optimistic one. We will tell you when it moves.

By job

JobTypicalWhy
Light scuff, polished outSame dayNo paint, no curing
Localised repair, one panel1–2 daysPaint and bake
One panel off, painted, refitted2–3 daysStrip, cure, polish, refit
Two or three panels3–5 daysMore paint, more blending
Panel replacement3–5 daysPlus parts lead time
Structural repair1–2 weeksMeasuring, welding, corrosion protection

Those are working days with the car here, and they assume the parts are available and, on a claim, that authorisation is already in place.

Why it cannot be compressed

Filler, primer and lacquer each need to harden before the next stage touches them. Baking accelerates it but does not remove it, and a repair pushed through faster than the materials allow keeps shrinking afterwards.

That is the cause of a repair that looks perfect at handover and shows a faint outline of the repair area a few months later. Nothing was done wrong except the clock.

What actually causes the delays on a claim

CauseTypical delay
Waiting for authorisation2–10 days
Parts on back order1 day to several weeks
Supplementary after strip-down2–7 days
Engineer inspection backlog2–5 days
Disputed labour timesOpen-ended

Add those up and a three-day repair becomes a three-week claim, with the car sitting stripped for most of it. None of that is workshop time, which is why "how long does the repair take" and "when do I get my car back" have different answers.

What you can do

  • Report it the same day and name your repairer immediately.
  • Chase authorisation yourself if it stalls. A call from the policyholder moves things a call from the bodyshop does not.
  • Say early if the car is needed by a particular date — sometimes the order of work can be arranged around it.
  • Decide about a replacement car at the start rather than three days in.

We will give you a real date rather than an optimistic one, and tell you when it moves. An estimate that is always "tomorrow" for a week is worse than an honest fortnight.

Your questions answered

Can you do it faster if I pay more?

Not the curing, no — that is chemistry. What can sometimes be arranged is where your car sits in the queue, and that is worth asking about if you have a real deadline.

Can I drive it while I wait for parts?

Often yes, if it is roadworthy and the damage is not going to get worse. We will tell you honestly if it should not be driven.

Why was my car in for two weeks for a bumper?

Almost certainly authorisation and parts rather than work. Ask for the dates: when the estimate went in, when it was authorised, when the parts arrived.

Do you work weekends?

Ring and ask. On a job where the timing genuinely matters we will do what we can.
