Keyed, Scratched or Vandalised

Two things surprise people. A key scratch almost never polishes out, and a vandalism claim normally costs you your no-claims even though you did nothing wrong.

Why it will not polish out

Paint is layered: primer, then colour, then lacquer on top. Polishing works by taking a very small amount off the lacquer, so it can remove something sitting in the lacquer and cannot touch anything deeper.

DepthTestRepair
In the lacquer onlyYour fingernail does not catchMachine polish, sometimes
Through to the colourFingernail catches, no colour change in the linePaint repair
Through to primerThe line looks pale or greyPaint repair
Through to metalThe line looks silver or brownPaint repair, and soon — this is where rust starts

A deliberate key scratch is almost always at least the third row, because it is done with weight behind it. Anyone promising to buff out a keyed panel is either going to fail or going to thin your lacquer trying.

The claim sum, including the part people miss

Vandalism is covered by comprehensive insurance. The catch is that there is no third party to recover from, so the claim is recorded as a fault claim and your no-claims discount is affected, exactly as if you had caused it.

  1. Get a crime reference number. Report it to the police. Most insurers require it for a vandalism claim and will ask for it.
  2. Get the repair priced. One keyed panel is often close to a typical excess. Two or three panels is not.
  3. Find out what claiming costs you. Ask your insurer what happens to your discount and what the renewal effect is. Get it in writing.
  4. Then decide. Single panel, usually pay. Several panels or slashed tyres and broken glass alongside, usually claim.

Act faster if it is through to metal

A scratch that has reached bare metal is an open invitation to corrosion, and once rust is established under the paint around the line it spreads outward and the repair gets bigger.

Days and weeks are fine. Leaving it over a winter is how a one-panel job becomes a two-panel job with rust treatment in the middle.

If you are not repairing it immediately and it is down to metal, at minimum keep it clean and dry and get some protection on the line. It buys you time, it is not a repair.

Evidence, in case it matters later

  • Photograph the whole car, not just the worst panel — vandalism is often multiple panels and people claim for one and find another later
  • Note where it was parked and the window of time
  • Ask neighbours and nearby businesses about cameras, and ask quickly
  • Keep the crime reference number with the photographs and the estimate

If the same car is targeted repeatedly, that pattern is worth reporting as a pattern rather than as separate incidents.

Your questions answered

Can a keyed scratch be polished out?

Only if your fingernail does not catch in it. A deliberate key scratch almost always goes deeper than the lacquer, which means paint rather than polish.

Will claiming for vandalism affect my no-claims?

Usually yes, because there is no third party for your insurer to recover from, so it is recorded as a fault claim. It is the thing people find unfair and it is how it works.

Do I need a police report?

Most insurers require a crime reference number for vandalism. Get one even if you end up paying yourself.

They keyed three panels. Is that one repair?

It is one job but three panels of paint, and the price reflects that. It is also the situation where claiming usually does make sense.

How soon do I need to do something?

If the line shows bare metal, sooner. Corrosion starting along the scratch turns a paint job into a paint job plus rust treatment.
