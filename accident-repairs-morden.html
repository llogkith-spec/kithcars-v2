Accident Repairs: what’s involved

Modern cars are built with crumple zones, high-strength steels and bonded panels designed to absorb an impact in a controlled way. That means the damage often goes further than what you can see. A bumper hit can crack mounting brackets, push back the crash bar or front panel, damage the radiator or air conditioning condenser, and knock a radar sensor out of alignment. A proper repair starts with stripping enough of the car to find all of it.

We handle both insurance and self-pay work. If you're claiming, you're entitled to have your car repaired at a garage of your choice rather than one your insurer nominates, and we'll send photos and an estimate to the insurer or their engineer and deal with approvals. Paint is colour matched from your paint code and blended into adjacent panels, and where cameras or radar are disturbed we arrange recalibration so the driver assistance systems work as intended.

Signs of hidden accident damage

Uneven panel gaps

Doors, bonnet or boot lid with wider gaps on one side, or a door that now needs slamming, suggest the shell or mounting points have moved.

Steering off-centre or pulling

After an impact or heavy kerb strike this can mean a bent track rod, suspension arm or subframe, not just wheel alignment out.

Warning lights after a bump

Airbag, parking sensor, adaptive cruise or emergency braking warnings often mean a sensor or its bracket is damaged or misaligned.

Bumper sitting proud or loose

Broken clips and brackets, or a reinforcement bar pushed back behind the cover, stop the bumper sitting in its correct position.

Headlight misaligned or misting

Broken mounting tabs change the beam aim, and a cracked housing lets moisture in.

Ripples or cracked sealer away from the impact

Creases in an inner wing, floor or roof, or cracked seam sealer, show that force travelled through the structure.

Overheating or no air conditioning after a front impact

The radiator and air conditioning condenser sit right behind the bumper and are easily punctured or bent.

New wind noise or water leaks

A door or its aperture that has distorted slightly will stop the seals meeting properly.

When should it be done?

As soon as practical after any impact, even one that looks cosmetic. Damage behind the bumper or to suspension parts can affect how the car brakes and steers, and sensors that are out of alignment may not work correctly. Have it inspected before driving any distance if the car pulls, a warning light has come on or a light is broken. If you're claiming, tell your insurer promptly as your policy requires, and keep photos of the damage and the scene.

  • Any collision where the bumper, lights or a wheel took the hit, however minor it looks
  • Airbag, parking sensor or driver assistance warnings appearing after an impact
  • Steering off-centre or the car pulling after hitting a kerb, pothole or another vehicle
  • Before selling the car or returning a lease car with damage that would be charged for
  • Once your insurer has authorised the repair, or once you've decided to pay privately

If it’s left

Leaving accident damage usually costs more later. A broken bumper clip or cracked headlight lets water and road dirt in, bare metal exposed by the impact can start to rust, and a slightly bent suspension part wears tyres quickly. Insurers expect claims to be reported promptly, and broken lights, sharp edges or damaged structural areas can all mean an MOT failure.

About Morden

Morden is a suburban town at the southern end of the Northern line, and grew rapidly after Morden Underground station opened in 1926, with its population rising from about 1,300 in 1921 to over 12,000 by 1931. Much of the housing is 1920s and 1930s semis and terraces, including a large part of the London County Council's St Helier estate, laid out along wide avenues and cul-de-sacs. The town centre is compact and dominated by the station and the Crown House civic tower, with generous parks on either side. It is a family and commuter suburb where most journeys are by car or bus to the station, so the few main roads carry most of the traffic.

Accident Repairs in Morden: your questions answered

Another car pulled out between parked cars on the St Helier estate and hit my wing. What gets checked?

We check the wing, bumper and headlight, but also the wheel, suspension arm and steering components, because a side impact near the wheel can bend them. Panel gaps around the bonnet and door tell us whether anything has moved. It's all included in the assessment and quote.

My airbag light came on after a side impact. Can you deal with that?

Yes. We scan the airbag system to read the fault, then inspect the sensors, wiring and any deployed components. Anything that needs replacing is included in the quote, and the system is re-checked before the car goes back.

Can you arrange collection from Morden if I work in town all day?

Yes, collection and delivery can be arranged locally. We'll agree a time with you, collect the car, and keep you updated on the quote and the repair by phone.

Do I have to use my insurer's approved repairer?

No. You're entitled to choose where your car is repaired. Some policies only give certain benefits, such as a courtesy car or the insurer's own repair guarantee, when you use their network, so check your policy wording. If you choose us, we work with your insurer's engineer to agree the repair.

Should I claim on my insurance or pay myself?

For smaller damage, paying privately can work out cheaper than paying the excess and losing some of your no-claims discount. Our written quote lets you compare before you decide. Even if you don't claim, your policy may still require you to report the accident to your insurer.

Will the new paint match the rest of the car?

Paint is colour matched from your car's paint code and checked on a test card against the car before it's applied. We then blend the colour into adjacent panels, so there's no hard line where new paint meets old. This matters most on metallic and pearl colours, which change with viewing angle.

What is ADAS recalibration and will my car need it?

Many cars use a windscreen camera and radar sensors behind the bumper or badge for emergency braking, lane keeping and adaptive cruise. If these are removed, replaced or knocked out of position, the manufacturer often requires them to be recalibrated. We tell you at the quote stage whether it applies and arrange it where needed.

What happens if you find more damage once the car is stripped?

It's common for more damage to appear once the bumper and trim are off. We photograph it, update the quote and get approval from you or your insurer before carrying out any extra work.
