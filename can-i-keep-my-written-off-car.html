Can I Keep My Written-Off Car?

Usually yes, if it is a Category S or N and you say so before you accept the settlement. After the car has gone to salvage, it has gone.

The short answer

  • Say it before you accept the settlement. After salvage collection it is gone.
  • You get the payout less the salvage value, and keep the car.
  • Get an insurance quote first — not every insurer covers a recorded write-off.
  • Worth it if you are keeping the car for years; rarely if you are selling soon.

How retention works

The insurer pays you the market value, then deducts what they would have received from the salvage company. You keep the car and the reduced payout.

So the question is not really “can I keep it”. It is whether what you keep, after that deduction, is worth more to you than the deduction costs.

  1. Say it early. At the first mention of a write-off. Once it is collected for salvage, retention is off the table.
  2. Ask for both figures. The full settlement, and the settlement after the salvage deduction.
  3. Get a real repair quote. Bring it to us or send photographs. Guessing at this stage is how people end up with a car they cannot afford to fix.
  4. Then do the sum. Reduced payout, minus repair cost, against the full payout. Add the value hit when you later sell.

When it is worth it

  • The damage looks worse than it is — cosmetic panels on an otherwise sound car.
  • The car is worth more to you than to the market: unusual spec, low mileage, you know its history.
  • You intend to keep it for years, so the resale hit never actually lands on you.
  • Replacing it with something equivalent would cost more than the settlement.

When it is not

  • You plan to sell within a couple of years. You take the value hit without getting the use.
  • It is a Category S and you do not know who will do the structural work.
  • The repair quote is close to the settlement. At that point you are doing a lot of work to stand still.
  • You need the car now. Retaining and repairing takes time that a payout does not.

The things people forget

Retaining the salvage does not remove the category. It is recorded permanently and will appear on every history check from then on.

You will also need to insure it afterwards, and not every insurer quotes on a recorded write-off. Get an insurance quote before you commit to retention, not after — it is the step most often done in the wrong order.

Your questions answered

Can the insurer refuse to let me keep it?

On Category A or B, yes — those cannot return to the road and retention is not an option. On S and N it is usually allowed, though it is their salvage to sell and not an absolute right.

Do I have to repair it straight away?

No. It is your car. It does need to be roadworthy and have a valid MOT before you drive it, and an MOT does not check structural repair quality.

Will my insurance continue on it?

Not automatically. The policy usually ends on a total loss settlement, so you will be arranging new cover, on a vehicle with a recorded category.

Can you tell me if mine is worth retaining?

Yes, and it is the most useful thing we do at this stage. Bring it in or send photographs and the registration, and we will give you a proper repair figure to set against the numbers the insurer gave you.
