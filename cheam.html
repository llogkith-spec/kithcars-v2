Cheam drivers

What we see on cars from Cheam

Cheam runs a lot of newer, well-kept cars on modest mileage, and the damage we see from here is mostly the slow accumulation of suburban life. The village crossroads where the A232 meets The Broadway and Station Way is a pinch point at weekday peaks and on Saturdays, and the parades around the High Street mean short hops, tight bays and doors opening next to yours. Trolley knocks, sharp-edged door dings and a bumper corner scuffed on a bollard all end up in our bodyshop. On a metallic or pearl finish the colour match is the whole job, which is why panels are sprayed and blended rather than brush-touched, and why we would rather repair a bumper skin than fit a replacement that has never been on that car.

Low mileage has quieter effects under the bonnet. A car that does a run to the village, a trip up Malden Road to the leisure centre and back rarely charges its battery fully, and modern stop-start systems with AGM or EFB batteries are unforgiving about that. A slow crank on a cold morning, stop-start refusing to engage, or the radio resetting are usually the battery giving notice rather than an electrical gremlin, and a replacement has to be the right type and coded to the car on many makes so the charging strategy stays correct. Brake and clutch fluid age by time, not miles, and on a low-mileage car they are the items owners forget.

Then there is the A232 itself. It is part of TfL's road network through Cheam and into Sutton and designated traffic-sensitive all week, so one lane closure puts a queue back to the crossroads, and the North Cheam junction with the A24 adds supermarket and drive-through traffic to the mix. Long spells of crawling are hard on an automatic gearbox's fluid and on an engine that never fully warms through. Cheam is inside the London-wide ULEZ, while Nonsuch Park's southern side and Ewell beyond are in Surrey and outside it, so it is worth knowing your vehicle's status before a regular run in the other direction.

Questions

Cheam: your questions answered

Four miles is a fair way. Will you pick the car up from Cheam?

Yes, collection and delivery is available locally and Cheam is within the area we cover. Give us a couple of possible days when you book and we will fit the run in around them. It is worth considering when the car is going to be with us for a full day.

Is it better to do the trip in one go or leave the car?

For shorter work such as an MOT, an air conditioning regas or a filter change, one trip usually covers it rather than driving over twice. For a service, brakes or any bodywork, plan to leave the car, as paint in particular needs time to be prepared, sprayed and left to harden properly. Ask us when you book and we will tell you what is realistic for that job on the day.

Do you do insurance work as well as self-pay bodywork?

We handle both accident repair and self-pay bodywork at the same site. For smaller damage such as a single scuffed bumper or a kerbed alloy, paying directly is often cheaper than losing a no-claims discount, and we will quote in writing so you can compare before deciding.

Is your workshop inside the ULEZ zone?

Yes. Hackbridge, like Cheam, is inside the London-wide ULEZ that has covered every London borough since August 2023, so no extra charge is triggered by driving over to us. Nonsuch Park's southern side and Ewell are in Surrey and outside the zone, which matters more for journeys the other way.
