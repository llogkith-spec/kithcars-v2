What Gets Charged When You Hand a Lease Car Back

There are actual numbers for this, and they are smaller than most people assume. A scratch is fine up to 25mm. A dent is fine up to 10mm, and only two per panel. Over those, you pay.

The short answer

  • Scratches: 25mm, with no primer showing.
  • Dents: 10mm, two per panel, paint unbroken.
  • Alloys: 50mm of scuffing around the circumference.
  • Any rust is chargeable, and so is a bad repair.

The thresholds, in millimetres

Inspectors work to the BVRLA Fair Wear and Tear standard, and it is specific. These are its limits.

DamageAcceptableChargeable
ScratchesUp to 25mm, with no primer or bare metal showingOver 25mm, or anything through to primer or metal
DentsUp to 10mm, maximum two per panel, paint unbrokenOver 10mm, more than two per panel, or paint broken
Dents on the roof or a swage lineNoneAny
Alloy wheelsScuffs totalling up to 50mm around the circumferenceOver 50mm, dents or holes in the rim, any damage to spokes or hub
ChipsSmall chips, including door edge chippingAnything needing the whole panel or bumper repainted
Rust or corrosionNoneAny, on any painted area
Previous repairsGood onesFlaking paint, rippled finish or mismatched colour

Two of those catch people out more than the rest. A swage line is the styling crease that runs along the flank of most cars — a dent on it is chargeable at any size, because it is the hardest place on the car to repair invisibly. And any rust is chargeable, however small.

The sum worth doing

Handback charges are set by the leasing company and are frequently more than having the same damage repaired beforehand, because they are pricing a main-dealer repair plus their own administration.

  1. Inspect it yourself, early. Eight to ten weeks before handback, in daylight, with a tape measure. Not the week before.
  2. Measure, do not estimate. The difference between 22mm and 28mm of scratch is the difference between nothing and a charge.
  3. List what is over. Only the items past the thresholds matter. Everything under is free.
  4. Get those priced. Put the registration into the box below, or bring it in. Then you have a real figure.
  5. Compare against the estimated charges. Most leasing companies publish an indicative charge list. Ask for it.

Where repairing first usually wins

  • Kerbed alloys. The commonest handback charge there is, and refurbishment runs £108 to £144 per wheel including VAT — normally well under what is charged.
  • A single scuffed bumper. £290 to £390 with us, and bumpers are the most-charged panel because they are the most scuffed.
  • Several small scratches on one panel. Individually fine, but a panel needing a repaint is chargeable, and the charge is for the panel either way.
  • Anything with rust starting. Chargeable at any size, and it gets worse while you wait.

Where it does not

Be even-handed about this: do not repair damage that is already within the standard. A 15mm scratch with no primer showing costs you nothing at handback, and repairing it is money spent for no reason.

And a poor repair is explicitly chargeable — flaking, rippled or mismatched paint is on the not-acceptable list. A cheap job done badly before handback can cost you more than the original damage would have.

Whoever you claim through, the repairer is your choice. KITH is on no insurer’s panel, which means no labour time has been pre-agreed on your car’s behalf — we estimate the job as it needs doing and argue it with the engineer if we have to.

Company cars and fleets

The same standard is normally used, but who pays varies with your employer’s policy. Some pass handback charges to the driver, some absorb them, and many have a threshold.

Worth asking before you decide anything, because if your employer absorbs it there is no reason to repair anything at your own expense.

Your questions answered

How long before handback should I check?

Eight to ten weeks. That leaves time to get work done without paying a premium for speed, and time to challenge the inspection if you disagree with it.

Can I challenge the charges?

Yes. Ask for the inspection report with photographs and measurements, and check them against the standard. Charges for damage within the thresholds should be withdrawn, and inspectors do make mistakes.

Will they notice a repair?

A good one, no. A bad one, immediately — and a poor repair is itself chargeable. That is the argument for having it done properly rather than cheaply.

What about a chipped windscreen?

Usually assessed separately against glass criteria rather than the paint ones, and often covered by your insurance with a low or nil excess for a repair. Worth checking before you pay for it.

Do I need to fix every chip?

No. Small chips including door edge chipping are explicitly acceptable. Only chips that would need a whole panel repainted are chargeable.
