How we handle SMART repair at Hackbridge

Choosing the area comes first. A bumper corner, a door edge, a mirror cover or a wing behind the arch each offer a natural boundary to work to, and the repair gets planned around that boundary rather than around the damage itself. We measure the damage, look at where the nearest body line or panel edge falls, and mark it out in tape so it is a decision you are part of. Damage that crosses a swage line, or sits out in the open middle of a bonnet, roof or door skin, gets quoted as a conventional panel repair instead, because that is the only way it will genuinely disappear.

Preparation is not shortened because the area is small. The same flatting, the same feathered edge, the same degreasing and tack-off, and the same two-pack materials as a full panel job, with plastic parts getting adhesion promoter and a flexible primer so the repair can move with the bumper. Sensors, camera lenses, badges and trim are removed or properly masked rather than sprayed around, because overspray on a parking sensor is the detail that tells anyone looking that the work was rushed. Where a bumper is cracked rather than scuffed, the plastic gets repaired from behind before any paint goes near it.

Fading a small repair into aged paint can match better than painting a whole panel to a code, because the blend meets the colour the car actually is rather than the colour it left the factory as. Several areas on one car are priced together, since the masking, the mixing and the curing are shared and three scuffs cost nothing like three times one. Everything is quoted in writing before we start, from £120, and the work carries our 12-month workmanship warranty. If you are preparing a car for a lease return, book a few weeks ahead rather than in the week of the inspection.

SMART Repairs: what’s involved

A conventional paint repair refinishes the whole panel and blends colour into the panels either side. SMART repair confines the work to the damaged area: it is prepared, primed and painted within a small zone, then the colour and clear coat are faded into the surrounding original paint so the edge can't be seen. Less paint, less masking and less dismantling means a shorter job and a lower price.

It works best on bumper corners, door edges, mirror covers and other areas near the edge of a panel, where the damage is roughly palm-sized or smaller. It isn't the right choice for damage in the middle of a large flat panel such as a bonnet or roof, long scratches that cross panels, or some colours that are difficult to blend in a small area. There, a conventional repair gives a more reliable result, and we'll tell you honestly which suits your car.

Damage suited to SMART repair

Bumper corner scuffs

The most common SMART job. The curve of a bumper corner gives a natural area to blend into.

Short scratches through the colour

Scratches that don't cross a panel join and are close to an edge or corner.

Stone chips on leading edges

Chips on the front edge of the bonnet or bumper that are confined to a small area.

Door edge chips

Chips from doors opened against walls, posts or other cars.

Scuffed mirror covers

Small, removable parts that are quick to refinish on their own.

Small dents with paint damage

The dent is repaired first, then the paint is refinished locally rather than across the whole panel.

Scuffed spoilers and painted trim

Edges of spoilers, sill covers and painted trim pieces that take knocks.

When should it be done?

SMART repair is most useful while the damage is still small. A scuff or chip caught early can often be dealt with in a few hours, whereas one left to spread, lift or rust may need a full panel refinish. It's popular before lease or PCP returns and before a sale, when a handful of small marks can affect the price. If the damage crosses a panel join or covers a large area, a conventional repair is usually the better choice.

  • Scuffs and chips roughly palm-sized or smaller
  • Damage near the edge or corner of a panel
  • As soon as bare metal shows, to stop rust starting
  • Before an end-of-lease inspection or sale

How a SMART repair works

  1. AssessWe check the size, position and colour to confirm SMART repair is suitable, then give you a written quote.
  2. Clean and maskThe area is degreased and a limited zone is masked, protecting the surrounding paint, glass and trim.
  3. Prepare the damageThe damaged paint is flatted back, chips or scratches are filled, and the edges are feathered smooth.
  4. PrimePrimer is applied to the repaired area only and flatted smooth once dry.
  5. Colour match and basecoatColour is mixed from your paint code, checked on a test card, and applied over the repair, fading out into the original paint.
  6. Clear coat and blendClear coat is applied over the repair and the edge is faded into the original finish with blending thinner.
  7. Cure and polishAfter controlled curing, the blend edge is polished so the gloss matches the rest of the panel.
  8. Inspect and hand overWe check the repair from different angles in good light and go through it with you.

Materials we use

SMART repair uses the same quality materials as a full panel repair, just in smaller quantities: a primer suited to metal or plastic, basecoat mixed to your paint code, and two-pack clear coat with blending thinner to fade the edge into the original finish. On plastic bumpers we use adhesion promoter and flexible primer so the repair can move with the bumper without cracking.

If it’s left

Small damage doesn't stay small. Chips and scratches through to bare steel rust, and the rust spreads under the paint around them, while damaged paint on bumper corners can lift and flake. Once the damage grows beyond a small area, SMART is no longer suitable and the job becomes a full panel refinish, which takes longer and costs more.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about SMART repair

Thinking SMART means a quick smear

The word small refers to the area, not the preparation. A repair that skips flatting and feathering will show as a ring or start lifting at the edge within months, whatever it looked like on collection day.

Expecting a cracked bumper to be blended

A crack has to be repaired through the plastic from behind and reinforced before any paint goes on. Filling and painting over a crack only hides it until the bumper next flexes.

Judging the finish in a dark workshop

Look at any paint repair outside, in daylight, from several angles and from a few feet back. Poor colour matching and a hard blend edge show up in sunlight and hide under fluorescent light.

Touching in a scuff before bringing it in

Aerosol or brush paint applied over a scuff has to be flatted back off again before the proper repair can start, which adds time to the job. If in doubt, leave it alone and let us see it as it is.

SMART Repairs: your questions answered

How is a SMART repair priced?

By area rather than by the number of marks. A single bumper corner starts from £120, and several areas done at the same visit are priced together because the preparation, mixing and curing overlap. You get the figure in writing before we start, with each area listed separately so you can drop one if you would rather.

Should I claim on insurance for a scuffed bumper?

A localised repair is generally priced in the range where a claim makes no sense, because the work costs less than or little more than most excesses. The other thing worth knowing is that even a protected no-claims discount does not stop a claim being declared at every renewal from then on. Ask for the figure, and ring your insurer only if it surprises you.

Does the bumper have to come off?

Sometimes. Removing it gives clean access to the edges and avoids a masking line where the bumper meets the wing, which on some cars makes a noticeable difference to the result. On others the damage sits well away from any edge and taking it off would add time for no benefit. We tell you which applies and it is reflected in the quote.

Will the repair show up when the car is inspected or valued?

There is no central record of a paint repair, and a well-blended one is very hard to see. A thorough inspector with a paint depth gauge can find a repaired area, which is normal on a used car and quite different from finding damage. What actually affects value is unrepaired damage and obvious, badly matched work.

What does SMART stand for?

Small to Medium Area Repair Technique. It means refinishing only the damaged area and blending it into the surrounding original paint, rather than painting the whole panel.

Will I be able to see the repair?

When the damage suits SMART and the colour is matched well, the repair is very hard to spot. Blending is the key. Where we think a SMART repair would show, for example in the middle of a bonnet, we'll recommend a conventional repair instead.

Is a SMART repair as durable as a full panel repair?

It uses the same types of primer, basecoat and two-pack clear coat, so a well-prepared repair is durable. The blend edge is the most delicate area at first, so avoid machine polishing it or aiming a pressure washer at it for the first few weeks.

Is SMART repair suitable for metallic paint?

For most metallic colours, yes. Metallic, pearl and three-layer colours are harder to blend in a small area because the flake needs to sit the same way as the original, so for some colours a larger blend or a conventional repair gives a better match. We'll advise when we see the car.

Can you do SMART repairs at my home?

We carry out SMART repairs in our workshop, where we can control dust, temperature and curing, which gives a more consistent finish. If dropping the car off is awkward, we offer local collection and delivery.
