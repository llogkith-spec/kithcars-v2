When your car will not move, and what to do next

Cars stop at the worst possible moment, and the decisions made in the first half hour tend to be the ones that cost money afterwards. This page is about that half hour: making yourself safe, working out whether you need a truck or a jump lead, and avoiding the choices that turn an awkward evening into a bigger bill. Detail on the service itself, the loading equipment and how a vehicle is secured sits on the vehicle recovery page. Recovery here is not a separate outfit bolted onto a garage. The truck belongs to the same workshop that will diagnose and repair the vehicle, which changes what happens after it is loaded, because the car arrives somewhere it can be put on a ramp rather than at a storage yard waiting on the next decision. We move breakdowns and non-runners, accident-damaged cars, vehicles with a seized engine or gearbox, cars bought at auction, project vehicles that have not turned a wheel in years, and vehicles that simply need shifting from one address to another. We are a local garage with a truck rather than a national network selling a response time, and we work across South London and the roads immediately around it.

When you ring, the questions are about the situation rather than the car. Exactly where is it sitting, is it in a live lane, is anybody hurt, can it be pushed clear. On a motorway or a fast dual carriageway the answer is always the same: get out on the nearside, stand behind the barrier and dial 999 before anything else, because that scene belongs to the police until the lane is closed. Anywhere else, hazards on, stay out of the carriageway, and stop cranking the starter, which flattens the battery and on some faults makes the diagnosis harder. Once we know what we are coming to, you get a time we can realistically keep and a figure you agree to before we set off.

What a move costs is a list you can read rather than a figure you wait for. Measured in road miles from the workshop and including VAT, a vehicle that cannot be driven goes on the truck for £144 within 3 miles, £132 within 5, £180 within 7 and £216 within 10. If the car is still fit to drive and one of our staff takes the wheel instead, those same four distances are £36, £54, £72 and £90. Beyond ten miles, or to any destination other than our own unit, you get the figure on the phone, and help at the roadside such as a jump start or a wheel change is quoted when you ring, since it turns on what the car actually needs where it stands. If the vehicle comes back to us, the move and repair stay separate: the fault is investigated, a written quote follows, and nothing obliges you to have the work done here. Should you want it taken to a dealer, another garage or simply home, that is the job we will do. A hire vehicle from £36 a day inc VAT is available if you need to keep driving.

Questions

Common questions

My car will not start. Is that automatically a recovery job?

Often it is not. A car that turns over slowly and clicks is usually a flat battery, and that can be tested and sometimes sorted where it stands. A car that cranks normally but never fires, or that will not turn over at all with a known-good battery, generally needs to come in. Describe what you can hear when you ring and we will tell you which of the two it sounds like.

I have put the wrong fuel in. What should I do?

Do not start the engine, and if you have not already, do not even switch the ignition on, because on most modern cars that runs the fuel pump. If the engine has not been started the fix is usually draining and flushing the system. If it has been run, the damage can extend to the pump, injectors and lines, which is a much bigger job on a diesel than a petrol. Either way the car needs moving rather than driving.

The car is drivable after an accident. Should I still have it recovered?

Judge it on three things: whether the lights, brakes and steering all work properly, whether anything is rubbing on a tyre, and whether fluid is dripping underneath. Damage near a wheel arch or a suspension mounting is the usual reason a car that feels fine should not be driven. If any airbag has gone off, the vehicle should be transported.

Can you move a car with no MOT, no tax or no keys?

A vehicle carried on the back of a truck does not need to be taxed, tested or insured to be moved, which is why barn finds and auction cars are routine work for us. No keys is workable but changes how the car is winched and secured, and an automatic that cannot be shifted out of park needs skates. Tell us on the call so the right equipment comes out first time.
