Mitcham drivers

What we see on cars from Mitcham

The terraced streets between Fair Green, Figges Marsh and Eastfields are the reason a lot of Mitcham cars arrive here looking scuffed. Victorian and interwar terraces with parking on both sides leave single-file gaps, and everyone takes their turn at edging past a wing mirror or reversing into a space barely longer than the car. The results are familiar: rippled door skins, cracked bumper corners, broken mirror casings and alloys grazed along a kerb edge. Small dents where the paint is intact can often be pushed out and left with their original factory finish; anything through the lacquer needs proper preparation and paint, and on a solid colour as much as a metallic the blend into the neighbouring panel is what decides whether the repair shows.

The road layout adds its own pressure. Since the Fair Green stretch of London Road became buses and cycles only, general traffic loops the one-way system past the Clock Tower, the Figges Marsh signals queue at peak times, and Carshalton Road crosses the railway and tram lines on a bridge by Mitcham Junction that funnels everyone heading our way. Tram rails are worth respecting on a wet day, and crossing them at an angle is a quick route to a kerbed rim or a damaged tyre sidewall. Out on the A236 across Mitcham Common the road opens up and speeds rise, so a car that spends the rest of its week crawling suddenly gets asked for real braking at the roundabout at the end of it.

There are plenty of working vehicles here too, given the industrial estates around Streatham Road and Willow Lane, along with taxi and private-hire cars on the A217 and A236. A van that is loaded most days sits lower on its rear springs, works its clutch harder pulling away on a gradient and gets through rear tyres faster, and the MOT is stricter about load-related items than many owners expect. Merton has been rolling out 20mph limits across residential roads in phases, including Pollards Hill and Longthornton, so town mileage here is slow, cold mileage. On a diesel that is the pattern most likely to leave a particulate filter partly regenerated and gradually loading up.

The route

From Mitcham to Soho Mills

From the town centre, follow London Road, the A217, south to the Cricket Green, then take Cranmer Road, the A239, to join Carshalton Road, the A237, heading south. Carshalton Road crosses the railway and tram lines on the bridge beside Mitcham Junction station, then runs past Beddington Corner and becomes London Road into Hackbridge. Soho Mills is on London Road near Hackbridge station. It is about 2.5 miles and roughly eight minutes.

Questions

Mitcham: your questions answered

Where should I leave the car if I am dropping it early?

Call us on 020 4629 9469 before you set off and we will tell you exactly where to pull in at the unit and what to do with the keys. Knowing you are coming also means we can plan the job and get the parts ordered before the car arrives.

Can I get back into Mitcham after leaving the car with you?

Hackbridge station sits at the same end of London Road as the workshop, and Mitcham Junction is both a rail station and a Tramlink stop, so the train is usually the simplest way back. If that does not suit, ask about collection and delivery when you book.

Do you work on vans as well as cars?

Yes. Van servicing, MOT preparation, brakes, clutches and bodywork are all part of what we do, and plenty of that work comes from the estates around Willow Lane and Streatham Road. Tell us the days you cannot be without the vehicle and we will work to them where the job allows.

Is parking near the town centre a problem while I sort the car out?

Merton Council runs controlled parking zones in parts of Mitcham, so check the signs before leaving a vehicle near the town centre or the stations. If a car is off the road waiting for parts, it is usually easier for it to sit at our workshop than on a restricted street.
