Why these bands run upward and never down

Every price on our rate card is a band, and the band runs upward from the card figure rather than either side of it.

That is deliberate. The card figure is what we would charge for the job as described, so it is the floor. What we cannot know before the car is in front of us is how much more the job is — a crease that has stretched the metal, a clip that has broken behind the bumper, corrosion under the paint. Never how much less.

A range that runs in both directions advertises a number nobody actually pays. That is the single most common trick in published repair pricing and we do not use it.

What moves a price up

FactorEffect
Depth of damageA scuff in the lacquer, a scuff through to primer and a dent that has stretched the panel are three different jobs on the same panel
Size of the carA van panel is larger and flatter than a car’s, which makes the middle of it harder to finish invisibly
Whether the panel comes offSome repairs are done in place; others need the panel removed, which adds time at both ends
Sensors behind the panelParking sensors, radar and cameras may need recalibrating after a bumper is refitted
What the strip-down findsBroken brackets, previous filler, corrosion starting underneath

What does not move it: metallic, pearl or three-stage paint, a colour change, or an aluminium panel. There is no surcharge on our card for any of those.

What is included

  • Paint and materials
  • Blending into the adjacent panels, which is the first thing cut from a cheap quote and the main reason a repair shows in daylight
  • Colour matched to the car rather than to the paint code, using a spray-out card
  • Twelve months on the paint, in writing

And what is not: parts, where the panel needs replacing rather than repairing, and any recalibration a sensor needs afterwards. Both are itemised separately rather than buried.

Why we publish this at all

Most bodyshops will not put a number on a web page, and the reason given is that every job is different. Every job is different. That is an argument for a band and a proper look at the car, not an argument for making people ring round for three quotes to find out whether they can afford to fix a bumper.

The cost guides that fill this gap are written by directory sites. Their figures come from nowhere in particular and the ranges are wide enough to be useless — “between £250 and £1,500” does not help anybody decide anything.

Whoever you claim through, the repairer is your choice. KITH is on no insurer’s panel, which means no labour time has been pre-agreed on your car’s behalf — we estimate the job as it needs doing and argue it with the engineer if we have to.

Your questions answered

Is the from-price realistic or a teaser?

Realistic. It is what we charge for the smallest genuine version of that job, and plenty of repairs come in at it. Put your registration into the box and you will get the band for your actual damage.

Why is a bumper more than a wing?

A front bumper carries more: sensors, brackets, the grille, sometimes a radar. The paint area is similar; the work around it is not.

Do you charge extra for metallic or pearl?

No. There is no surcharge on our card for metallic, pearl, three-stage or a colour change. Those paints take longer to match, which is why we use a spray-out card, but the price is the same.

Is blending really included?

Yes, on every figure here. It is worth asking anyone else quoting you the same question, because the answer will tell you more than the price does.

What if the strip-down finds more?

We tell you before carrying on, with the revised figure, and you decide. We do not ring you at four o’clock with the work already done.
