In the workshop

How we handle suspension repair at Hackbridge

Every suspension job here starts with the wheels off the ground and a second pair of hands. One of us levers each joint while the other watches and listens, because a worn bush moves under load and looks perfectly healthy standing still. We work round the corner in the same order every time: top mount, spring, damper body, ball joint, arm bushes, drop link, anti-roll bar mounts, then the steering joints, so nothing gets missed because the obvious fault turned up first. If a noise only appears on the move, we will take you out in the car and ask you to point at it.

What you get shown is the play itself. We would rather you stood by the ramp and watched the joint move than took our word for it, and if a part is borderline we say so and leave the decision with you. Corroded fixings are part of the job in London: subframe bolts, drop link nuts and strut top nuts sit in road salt for years and some of them will shear. We tell you that before we start rather than afterwards, and if a bolt does snap we stop, call you with what it will take to put right, and wait.

A knock does not get quietened here by nipping up a worn joint or packing out a perished bush, because that comes back within weeks and you pay twice. Coil springs are never cut or heated to lower a car either, and we will talk you out of lowering springs on standard dampers, since the damper is not valved for the shorter travel and will not survive it. Prices are agreed part by part before any work starts, split into what needs doing now and what can safely wait until the next service, so you can spread the cost if you need to.

The cars we see

Most of what comes in is high-mileage urban stuff: hatchbacks and small family cars that live on pothole-scarred back roads, and private hire hybrids covering big weekly mileages. Suspension Repairs: what’s involved

Your suspension keeps the tyres in contact with the road and the car level and controlled. Coil springs carry the car's weight, shock absorbers control how fast the springs move, and control arms, ball joints and rubber bushes locate each wheel while letting it move up and down. An anti-roll bar, connected by drop links, limits body roll in corners.

London roads are hard on suspension. Potholes, speed humps and kerbs load these parts heavily, and rubber bushes perish with age. Worn suspension affects more than comfort: it changes how the car brakes, steers and grips, wears tyres unevenly and is a common MOT failure. Many parts, such as drop links and bushes, are inexpensive, so finding the actual worn component matters more than replacing parts by guesswork.

Signs your suspension needs attention

Knocking over bumps

A light knock or rattle over small bumps at low speed is very often a worn anti-roll bar drop link. A heavier clunk can be a ball joint or control arm bush.

Bouncing or floating

If the car keeps bouncing after a bump, or feels floaty at speed, the shock absorbers have lost their damping.

Leaning or sitting low on one corner

One corner lower than the others usually means a broken coil spring. Springs often break near the bottom coil and can go without warning.

Clunk or creak when turning the wheel

Noise when steering at a standstill or parking often comes from a worn top mount or top mount bearing.

Pulling or wandering

A car that wanders on the motorway or pulls to one side can have worn control arm bushes or ball joints, or incorrect wheel alignment after pothole damage.

Uneven tyre wear

Wear on the inner or outer edge, or scalloped patches, points to worn suspension parts or alignment out of specification.

Nose dive when braking

Excessive dipping under braking or squat when accelerating suggests worn shock absorbers.

Oily shock absorber

Oil running down the body of a shock absorber means the seal has failed and it is losing damping.

When should suspension be repaired?

Suspension parts are replaced when worn, not at fixed intervals, though they are checked at every service and MOT. Shock absorbers slowly lose damping, so the change can be hard to notice day to day. Drop links and bushes wear fastest on cars that see a lot of potholes and speed humps. Any new knock, a sudden lean or a change in handling after hitting a pothole should be checked promptly, as a broken spring or damaged arm can affect safety straight away.

  • Shock absorbers: commonly checked from around 60,000 miles, replaced in axle pairs when worn
  • Coil springs: replaced when broken or sagging, always in axle pairs
  • Drop links and bushes: replaced when play or knocking develops
  • After a heavy pothole or kerb strike: check for damage and alignment
  • Before an MOT if you have noticed knocks or uneven tyre wear

If it’s left

Worn suspension wears tyres faster, lengthens braking distances and makes the car less stable in emergency manoeuvres. A broken spring can puncture a tyre if it moves, and a badly worn ball joint can separate. Worn parts also place extra load on others, so one failed bush can speed up wear elsewhere.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about suspension repair

Alignment will fix the knock

Alignment sets angles; it cannot tighten a worn joint. Setting the geometry around a loose arm simply locks in the fault, and it drifts straight back as soon as the joint moves under load.

Budget dampers are all the same

A cheap damper often feels fine for a few months and then loses its damping quietly, so the car never feels obviously broken and the tyres wear out early instead.

A quiet car has good suspension

Dampers rarely make a noise as they fail. They fade, and drivers adapt to it, which is why body control and the bounce test are checked at every service rather than waiting for a complaint.

Lowering springs improve handling

Fitted to standard dampers they usually make the ride worse and shorten damper life, and they change camber and toe, which eats tyres. A matched spring and damper kit is a different proposition.

Suspension Repairs: your questions answered

Can I supply my own parts?

You can, and we will fit them, but say so when you book. Our 12-month workmanship warranty covers our labour; a part you bought yourself is covered by whoever sold it, so if it fails you would be paying the labour twice. We will also tell you honestly if what has arrived is the wrong specification for your car.

Do you need the car for the whole day?

Drop links, top mounts and most single items are comfortable waiting jobs. Pairs of struts, control arms and anything with badly corroded fixings are better left with us for the day. We collect and deliver locally if getting here is the awkward part.

What exactly does the written quote cover?

It lists the parts we have found worn, the labour to fit them and anything we recommend checking afterwards, such as alignment. If something else shows up once the corner is stripped, work stops and we ring you with a revised figure before carrying on.

Will I be left without a car if it turns into a bigger job?

Raise it while the job is being booked. Courtesy cars go out on a first-come basis and we will not know what is free until the diary is in front of us. If none is, we hire vehicles out from the same site, and we will tell you plainly which of the two is realistic for your dates.

Why do shock absorbers and springs have to be replaced in pairs?

The two sides of an axle must behave the same. A new shock absorber on one side and an old one on the other makes the car handle unevenly, particularly under braking and cornering.

What is the knocking noise over speed bumps?

The most common cause is a worn anti-roll bar drop link, which is a quick and inexpensive fix. Other causes include ball joints, control arm bushes, top mounts and loose exhaust brackets, so we check rather than guess.

Do I need wheel alignment after suspension work?

If parts that affect geometry have been replaced, such as control arms, subframe bushes or track rod ends, an alignment check is recommended. Drop links and most shock absorbers on their own do not usually change alignment.

Will worn suspension fail an MOT?

Excessive play in ball joints and bushes, broken springs, leaking shock absorbers and damaged components are MOT failure items. We can check your suspension before the test.

Do you repair air suspension?

We can diagnose air suspension faults such as leaking air springs and compressor problems, and quote individually, as parts and labour vary a lot between vehicles.
