Nobody Stopped. Who Pays for the Car?

There is a national scheme for untraced drivers, and most people assume it covers their car. For damage only, with no injury, it does not. This is the rule worth knowing before you spend a week chasing it.

The short answer

  • No injury means no MIB claim for your car.
  • Significant injury = 2+ nights inpatient or 3+ outpatient sessions.
  • Property damage excess under the scheme is £400.
  • With no third party, an insurance claim is recorded as fault.

The rule, stated plainly

The Motor Insurers’ Bureau runs the Untraced Drivers Agreement, which is the scheme for when the driver who hit you cannot be identified. Under it, damage to your vehicle is only recoverable where the same accident caused a significant injury.

Under the Untraced Drivers Agreement
Injury claimRecoverable
Vehicle damage, with a significant injury in the same accidentRecoverable
Vehicle damage, nobody injuredNot recoverable
Excess applied to property damage£400
Reporting to policeAs soon as reasonably practicable, if MIB asks

A significant injury means death, or injury needing two or more nights of inpatient treatment, or three or more outpatient sessions. A sore neck for a fortnight does not meet it.

So: car park shunt, nobody hurt, no note left, driver never identified. There is no MIB route for the repair. That is not us being unhelpful, it is the agreement.

What is actually left, then

  1. Try hard to identify the driver first. If you can, this becomes an ordinary third-party claim and everything changes. The next page covers where to look.
  2. Check whether you are comprehensive. If so, your own policy will repair the car, subject to your excess.
  3. Work out whether claiming is worth it. With no third party to recover from, your insurer cannot get its money back, so it is recorded as a fault claim and your no-claims takes the hit.
  4. Get it priced for paying yourself. On smaller damage this is very often the cheaper answer once the excess and the lost discount are counted. Put the registration in below.

The sum most people get wrong

A scuffed door is £240 to £340 with us. Suppose your excess is £350 and claiming costs you two years of no-claims discount.

On a single panel the arithmetic usually says pay for it. On a car with damage down one whole side it usually says claim. Get the real number before deciding, because both answers are right in different circumstances.

If somebody was hurt

Then the agreement does apply to the car as well, with a £400 excess on the property damage. Report it to the police, keep the crime reference, and make the MIB claim on their own form, which is now compulsory.

That is a personal injury claim with a vehicle damage element attached, and it is worth taking proper advice rather than handling it off a web page.

Your questions answered

Does my comprehensive policy cover a hit and run?

Yes, for the damage, subject to your excess. The question is whether claiming costs you more than the repair, because with no third party it is recorded as a fault claim.

Do I have to report it to the police?

For an MIB claim, yes, if they ask, and as soon as reasonably practicable. For your own insurer, a crime reference number makes the claim go more smoothly whether or not it is required.

What if I find the driver later?

Then it stops being an untraced claim and becomes an ordinary third-party one, which is a much better position. Tell your insurer immediately.

Will the MIB pay my excess back?

Under the untraced agreement there is a £400 excess on property damage, and that applies to the claim itself. It is not a route to recovering your own policy excess on a no-injury claim, because that claim is not recoverable at all.

Can I claim for the hire car I had to get?

Only as part of a claim that is itself recoverable. If the vehicle damage is not recoverable because nobody was injured, the associated costs are not either.
