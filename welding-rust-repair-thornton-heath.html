Welding & Rust Repair: what’s involved

Most cars have a monocoque body, meaning the shell itself acts as the chassis. Sills, floor pans, crossmembers and the areas around suspension and seat belt mountings carry load, so rust there weakens the car. Corrosion usually starts where water and road salt collect: inside sills, behind arch liners, along seams and around jacking points, and it often works from the inside out.

The MOT treats corrosion seriously. Excessive corrosion in load-bearing structure is a failure, particularly within 30cm of prescribed areas such as suspension, steering and seat belt mounting points. A proper repair means cutting back to sound metal, fitting a new steel section and welding it in, then sealing and protecting it inside and out. Covering rust with filler or a plate over rotten metal doesn't fix it, and a tester can fail an inadequate repair on a structural area.

Signs of rust that needs welding

Bubbling paint on sills or arches

Rust forming underneath the paint. It's usually more extensive than the visible bubbles suggest.

Holes or flaking metal underneath

Floor pans, sills, jacking points and crossmembers that flake or can be pushed through with a probe.

MOT advisory or failure for corrosion

An advisory is a warning worth acting on; a failure for structural corrosion must be repaired before the car can pass.

Soft or crumbling jacking points

If the sill crushes or the jack sinks into it, the structure around it is weakened.

Rust around suspension mountings

Corrosion near spring seats, strut tops, subframe or trailing arm mounts is structural and needs prompt repair.

Water in the footwells

Can be caused by holes in the floor, although blocked drains and door seals are more common causes, so we check.

Rust on door bottoms and boot floor

Often cosmetic rather than structural, but cheapest to repair while the rusted area is still small.

When should it be done?

Deal with structural rust as soon as it's found, ideally before the MOT so you have time to plan. Corrosion advisories are worth acting on, because another winter of salted roads can turn an advisory into a failure needing bigger repairs. Cosmetic rust on door bottoms or arch edges isn't a safety issue, but it's cheapest to fix while it's still at the bubbling stage. Older and classic cars benefit from a regular underside inspection.

  • MOT advisory mentioning corrosion: before the next winter or MOT
  • MOT failure for corrosion: before retest, and before driving at all if the defect was rated dangerous
  • Bubbling paint on sills, arches or door bottoms: while the rust is still small
  • Buying an older or classic car: underside inspection before or soon after purchase

Welding & Rust Repair in Thornton Heath: your questions answered

My old diesel is ULEZ compliant but rusty. Should I weld it or sell it?

If it runs well and the rust is limited to a few areas, welding is usually the cheaper option. We'll inspect the whole underside and give you an honest view, including if the corrosion is too widespread to be worth it.

Why do my sills rust when the rest of the car looks fine?

Sills sit low, collect road spray and are hollow, so water gets trapped inside. Kerb scrapes on tightly parked streets also remove the protection. Rust starts inside and only shows as bubbling paint later.

Can I drive to you with an MOT corrosion failure?

If the defects were rated major, your previous MOT certificate is still valid and the car is otherwise roadworthy, you can usually drive it to us. If a defect was rated dangerous, don't drive it at all. We can arrange collection instead.

Will my car pass the MOT after welding?

We repair the items on your failure sheet to a standard that meets the MOT inspection requirements, although the tester makes the final decision at the retest. If we find other corrosion nearby that's likely to cause a problem, we'll point it out before the retest.

Is it worth welding an older car?

It depends on how much rust there is and what the car is worth. A couple of sill repairs on an otherwise sound car are usually worthwhile. Widespread corrosion across floors, sills and mountings can cost more than the car is worth, and we'll give you an honest view.

Can you just patch over the rust?

Not on structural areas. Welding a plate over rotten metal traps moisture so the rust carries on underneath, and a tester can fail it as an inadequate repair. We cut out to sound metal and weld in new steel.

What's the difference between structural and cosmetic rust?

Structural rust affects load-bearing parts such as sills, floors, crossmembers, chassis legs and the areas around suspension and seat belt mountings. Cosmetic rust is on outer panels like door skins and bolt-on wings, which aren't load-bearing and usually only fail an MOT if they leave sharp edges.

How can I slow down rust?

Rinse the underside during winter to remove road salt, keep door and sill drain holes clear, and deal with stone chips early. On older cars, cavity wax in the sills and underbody protection make a real difference, and we can apply them.
