Audi Servicing & Repairs: what’s involved

Modern Audis share most of their engines and gearboxes with the wider Volkswagen Group, so the same known weak points appear across the range: the EA888 TFSI petrol engines, the 2.0 TDI diesel with its EGR and DPF systems, and the S tronic dual-clutch gearbox. Knowing those patterns means we can diagnose faster and check the right things at every service, rather than waiting for a warning light.

Audi uses either fixed or LongLife (variable) servicing, and the oil specification matters more than the brand name on the bottle. LongLife engines need oil approved to VW 504 00 or 507 00, and some newer engines call for VW 508 00 or 509 00. The wrong oil can shorten chain, turbo and DPF life. Under the Block Exemption rules, having an independent garage service your Audi to the manufacturer's schedule with parts of matching quality does not void the manufacturer's warranty.

Common Audi faults we fix

High oil use on 1.8 and 2.0 TFSI

Second-generation EA888 TFSI engines, fitted from around 2008, are known for burning oil past the piston rings. We rule out leaks and breather faults, then run a measured oil consumption test before recommending any repair.

PCV and oil separator failure

A split diaphragm in the crankcase breather causes a whistle or hiss at idle, rough running, lean-mixture fault codes and higher oil use. It is a common and relatively straightforward repair on TFSI engines.

TFSI timing chain rattle

Early EA888 engines used a chain tensioner that could let the chain jump a tooth, usually preceded by a rattle on cold start. We check camshaft-to-crankshaft correlation values in live data to assess chain stretch.

Coolant leaks from the water pump housing

Later TFSI engines use a plastic water pump and thermostat housing that can crack or seep, showing as a low coolant warning, a sweet smell or pink crust around the pump.

2.0 TDI EGR and DPF blockage

Short journeys stop the diesel particulate filter regenerating and let soot build up in the EGR valve and cooler. Expect a DPF warning light, flat performance and eventually limp mode.

S tronic judder and mechatronic faults

The 7-speed dry-clutch DQ200 gearbox in smaller models can develop judder on pull-away, jerky low-speed shifts, a flashing gear display or loss of some gears, often traced to clutch wear or the mechatronic unit.

Haldex coupling faults

Transverse-engine quattro models such as the A3, TT and Q3 use a Haldex coupling whose pump and filter can clog if the oil is never changed, leaving the car driving through the front wheels only.

Front suspension arm and bush wear

Heavier models such as the A4, A5 and A6 wear their front control arm bushes and ball joints, causing knocks over bumps, vague steering and uneven tyre wear.

When should your Audi be serviced?

Many Audis are set to LongLife servicing, where the car calculates the interval from how it is driven, up to two years or around 19,000 miles. Cars used mainly for short urban journeys, which covers a lot of South London driving, are usually better on fixed servicing every 12 months or 9,000–10,000 miles. Beyond the oil change, Audi's schedule includes brake fluid, spark plugs, filters and, depending on the car, gearbox and Haldex oil changes at set intervals, and we follow those for your specific engine and gearbox.

  • LongLife oil service: variable, up to 2 years or around 19,000 miles
  • Fixed oil service: every 12 months or 9,000–10,000 miles, better for short-journey driving
  • Brake fluid: typically first at 3 years, then every 2 years
  • Wet-clutch S tronic oil and filter: at the scheduled interval, typically about 60,000 km (roughly 37,000 miles); the dry-clutch DQ200 has no routine oil change
  • Haldex coupling oil (transverse quattro): at the interval in your car's schedule

If it’s left

Most Audi faults give some warning before they become expensive. A failed oil separator left alone leads to rough running and oil loss, a rattling TFSI timing chain can jump teeth and damage valves, and a blocked DPF can progress from a warning light to limp mode and a replacement filter. Picking these up at a service is almost always cheaper than dealing with them after a failure.

Audi Servicing & Repairs in Wimbledon: your questions answered

My Audi is under warranty. Why not just use the dealer in town?

You can, but you do not have to. An independent service to Audi's schedule with matching-quality parts keeps the warranty valid. We give a written quote first so you can compare, and we will tell you if anything needs to go to the dealer.

Do you work on hybrid Audis from Wimbledon?

We service and repair the conventional engine, brakes, suspension and general systems on many hybrid Audis. Some high-voltage work requires specific equipment, and we will tell you up front if your car's job falls into that category.

Can you collect my Audi from Wimbledon Chase?

Yes, we can collect from Wimbledon Chase or elsewhere in Wimbledon. That avoids crossing the town centre and dealing with parking controls. Let us know where the car will be when you book.

Will servicing my Audi with an independent garage affect the warranty?

No, provided the car is serviced to Audi's schedule using parts of matching quality and the work is recorded. This is protected under the Block Exemption rules, which the UK retains through the Motor Vehicle Block Exemption Order. Warranty repairs and recalls are still carried out free of charge by an Audi dealer, so we will tell you if something looks like a warranty or recall matter.

Should my Audi be on LongLife or fixed servicing?

LongLife suits cars that cover long, steady journeys. If your Audi mainly does short trips, stop-start traffic or school runs, fixed servicing every 12 months or 9,000–10,000 miles is usually kinder to the engine. We can explain the options and switch the car over if you decide to change.

My 2.0 TFSI is using oil. Is that normal?

Some oil use is normal, but second-generation EA888 engines can use far more than they should. We first check the oil separator and look for leaks, then carry out a measured consumption test over a set mileage before recommending bigger repairs such as piston and ring replacement.

Can you service an S tronic gearbox?

Yes. Wet-clutch S tronic units need an oil and filter change at the scheduled interval, which we carry out with the correct fluid before checking operation on the road. For dry-clutch DQ200 units we diagnose judder and mechatronic faults and quote for the repair.

Can you code new parts to my Audi?

For common jobs such as battery adaptation, electric parking brake service mode, and throttle or gearbox adaptations, yes, using dealer-level diagnostic equipment. Some component protection and key programming work needs Audi's online systems, and we will tell you up front if your job falls into that category.
