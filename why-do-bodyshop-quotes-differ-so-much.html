Why Is One Quote Half the Price of the Other?

Because they are usually quoting different work. Five things vary between bodyshop quotes, and four of them are invisible on the day you collect the car.

The short answer

  • They are usually quoting different work, not the same work at different prices.
  • Ask both: does that include blending? and is the panel coming off?
  • Spraying outdoors means dust, overspray and nothing baked.
  • Sometimes the cheap quote is right — a small scuff on a car you are not precious about.

None of those show at handover. All of them show within a year, which is why the comparison people make on the day is not the comparison that matters.

The two questions worth asking both shops

  1. “Does that include blending into the panels either side?” This single question separates most quotes. Blending is extra material, extra masking and time spent on panels that are not damaged, and leaving it out is invisible until the sun comes out.
  2. “Are you taking the bumper off or masking around it?” Off means the edges and the back get painted and the mounting tabs get checked. Masked means a line where the new paint stops.

Two answers tell you more than two prices do. If one shop says yes to both and the other says no, you are not comparing like with like and the cheaper one is cheaper for a reason rather than out of generosity.

Spraying outdoors

This is the one people underestimate. Paint is sprayed in a booth because the booth controls three things at once: dust, temperature and airflow. Outdoors you get none of them.

  • Dust and insects land in wet lacquer and stay there.
  • Wind carries overspray onto the rest of your car, which is a second job to put right.
  • Temperature decides how the paint flows and how the flake in a metallic lies down. A cold morning and a warm afternoon give different colours from the same tin.
  • Nothing bakes. Lacquer that air-dries keeps shrinking for weeks, which is why the outline of the repair sometimes appears a month later.

When the cheaper quote is the right one

Not every job needs a booth, and we would rather say so than pretend otherwise.

A small scuff in the middle of a bumper, on a car you are not precious about, repaired on your drive for less than half what we would charge, is a perfectly rational choice. Mobile repairers do a lot of that work and some of them do it well.

What it does not suit is a crack, a panel edge, a metallic that has to match its neighbours, or anything where the mounting points may have gone. Those are the jobs where the cheap quote becomes the expensive one.

What our figures include

A bumper scuff runs £290 to £390 and a dented or cracked bumper £370 to £650, including VAT. Both include taking the bumper off the car, blending into both front wings, baking, and checking the mounting tabs and any sensors behind it.

We do not quote a lower number by leaving the blend out and explaining afterwards. If you get a quote from us and a cheaper one from someone else, ask them the two questions above and you will usually find the difference.

Whoever you claim through, the repairer is your choice. KITH is on no insurer’s panel, which means no labour time has been pre-agreed on your car’s behalf — we estimate the job as it needs doing and argue it with the engineer if we have to.

Your questions answered

Is a £130 difference worth it?

It depends entirely which of the five things above the £130 buys. If it buys blending and the panel coming off, yes. If both shops are doing the same work and one is simply cheaper, no.

They quoted from photographs. Is that reliable?

For a price band, yes. For a firm figure, less so — no photograph shows a bent crash bar behind an intact bumper or a broken mounting tab. A shop that has seen the car is quoting on more information.

Should I just get more quotes?

Three is plenty, and ask all three the same two questions. More quotes without those questions just gives you more numbers that are not comparable.

Why is a main dealer so much more?

Higher labour rates and genuine parts at list price. The work is usually good; you are paying for the overhead as much as the repair.

Can I see where the work gets done?

Ask. A shop proud of its booth will show you it. That is a reasonable request and how it is received tells you something.
