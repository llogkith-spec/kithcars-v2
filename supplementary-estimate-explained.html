Supplementary Estimates: Why the Price Went Up

Your car is in pieces and the price has changed. That is a supplementary, it is normal, and it happens because no photograph has ever shown what is behind a panel.

What a supplementary is

A revised estimate raised after the original was authorised, covering damage that could not be seen until the car was stripped. It goes back to the engineer for a second authorisation before the extra work is done.

It is not a bodyshop changing its mind or finding a way to charge more. The first estimate is written from what is visible with the panels on, and that is a genuine limit rather than a failure of effort.

What turns up at strip-down

  • A bent crash bar behind an apparently intact bumper.
  • Broken mounting tabs and brackets, which hold the panel in line and are invisible from the front.
  • Damaged sensor wiring and connectors, pulled rather than cut.
  • Corrosion underneath the damaged area, which has to come out before any repair.
  • Impact carried further than it looked — a door hit that moved the pillar, a rear shunt that reached the boot floor.
  • Previous repairs nobody mentioned, including filler over something that should have been replaced.

That last one is more common than people expect on used cars, and it is nobody’s fault in the current claim. It still has to be dealt with before new paint goes on.

How it runs

  1. We photograph everything as it is found. Before anything moves, with the damage visible and in context.
  2. The supplementary is raised. Itemised, with the photographs attached, on the same system as the original.
  3. The engineer reviews it. Sometimes a second inspection, more often from the photographs.
  4. It is authorised, queried or reduced. Queries are normal and usually settled in a call.
  5. We tell you either way. Including what it does to the timescale, which is usually the part that matters to you.

Does it cost you more?

Normally not. Your excess is a fixed figure set by your policy and it does not rise because the repair did. The supplementary is between the bodyshop and the insurer.

Where it can affect you is on a borderline claim: enough additional damage can tip a repair into a total loss, and then the conversation changes from repairing your car to valuing it.

Why it is worth insisting on

A supplementary is the mechanism that gets hidden damage paid for. The alternative is a repairer who absorbs it by not doing the work — refitting a bumper onto broken tabs, painting over corrosion, leaving a sensor untested.

All of those look fine at handover. They show up months later, after the claim is closed and the paperwork says the car was repaired.

Your questions answered

Can the insurer refuse a supplementary?

They can query or reduce it. A properly evidenced one with photographs taken at strip-down is hard to refuse outright, which is why we photograph everything before touching it.

How much does it delay things?

Usually two to seven days for the second authorisation. The car sits stripped during that, which is the frustrating part.

Will I be told before the work is done?

Yes. We tell you what was found, what it means for the timescale, and whether it changes anything for you.

What if I am paying privately rather than claiming?

Same principle, simpler process. We stop, show you what we found, and agree the revised figure with you before carrying on. We do not do extra work and present the bill afterwards.
