Mobile SMART Repair, or a Bodyshop?

SMART repair is a real technique with a real limit: small areas, away from edges, where the panel is sound underneath. Inside that limit it is excellent value. Outside it, it is a repair you pay for twice.

The short answer

  • Small to Medium Area Repair Technique — the limit is in the name.
  • Good for: bumper scuffs, kerbed alloys, mid-panel scratches.
  • No for: bonnets, panel edges, cracks, rust, damage across two panels.
  • The harder your paint — metallic, pearl, three-stage — the smaller the damage has to be.

What SMART actually stands for

Small to Medium Area Repair Technique. The point is in the name: it refinishes the damaged area and blends into the surrounding original paint, rather than painting the whole panel. Less material, less masking, less time, lower price.

It is not a lesser version of a full repair. On the right damage it is the correct answer and a full panel respray would be overkill.

The bonnet question specifically

A dented bonnet is the single most common thing people are offered a mobile repair for, and it is the one where we would most firmly say no.

A bonnet is a large, flat, horizontal panel in full daylight — the second hardest panel on the car to match after the roof. A localised repair in the middle of it has nothing to hide behind. Metallics in particular show the join from across a car park, because the flake in the repaired area has not laid down the same way as the flake around it.

A bonnet done properly runs £260 to £540 including VAT on an ordinary five-seater, painted complete and blended into both wings.

Where it is done matters as much as how

A mobile repair happens on your driveway. That means no booth, so no control over dust, temperature or airflow, and wind carries overspray onto the rest of your car.

For a small bumper scuff on a solid colour, that is often fine and thousands of them are done every week. For anything where the colour has to match across a large area, it is the difference between a repair you cannot find and one you can.

How to tell which yours is

  1. Is it smaller than your hand? If not, SMART is already marginal.
  2. Is it at least a hand’s width from any edge or shut line? A blend needs original paint all around it.
  3. Is the panel sound underneath? No crack, no rust, no previous filler.
  4. Is it a flat horizontal panel in daylight? Bonnet, roof or boot lid — if yes, be cautious whatever the size.
  5. Is your paint a metallic, pearl or three-stage? The harder the finish, the smaller the damage has to be for SMART to work.

Four yeses and a solid colour: a mobile SMART repair is good value and we will tell you so. Any no: it is a bodyshop job, and doing it twice costs more than doing it once.

Whoever you claim through, the repairer is your choice. KITH is on no insurer’s panel, which means no labour time has been pre-agreed on your car’s behalf — we estimate the job as it needs doing and argue it with the engineer if we have to.

Your questions answered

Do you do SMART repairs?

No. SMART is a real technique and this page exists to help you judge whether yours is a job for it, but we do not offer it - we repair the panel properly and paint it in the booth. If your damage passes the four checks above, a mobile SMART outfit is the cheaper answer and we will say so. For damage that genuinely suits the technique it is the cheapest thing on our rate card.

A mobile repairer quoted half your price.

For a small bumper scuff they may well be right and we will say so. Ask them what happens if it rains, where the overspray goes, and whether they can do it if the paint is cracked.

Will a SMART repair last as long?

On the right damage, yes — same primer, same basecoat, same two-pack lacquer. What fails is a SMART repair used on damage it does not suit.

Can a SMART repair be done over a previous one?

Sometimes, and it is worth telling whoever quotes that the panel has been done before. A paint depth gauge will show it anyway, and it changes the preparation.

What if I have it done and it shows?

Then it gets done properly, and you have paid twice. That is the whole case for matching the technique to the damage first.
