Warning lights, LED headlamps and tyres on premium cars

Wimbledon has a high share of newer premium cars, SUVs and hybrids, from the big houses near Cannizaro Park and the Common to the streets around Wimbledon Park and Wimbledon Chase. These cars are packed with electronic systems, and the MOT checks that their warning lights behave. An illuminated airbag, seatbelt pretensioner, ABS, electronic parking brake or engine management light is a fail, as is a tyre pressure warning on cars first used from 2012. Often the fault is minor, but it has to be fixed before the car will pass.

Lighting is the other common trap. Many premium cars have xenon or LED headlamps with automatic levelling and, on some xenon units, headlamp washers; where these are fitted as standard and don't work, the car can fail. So can aftermarket HID bulbs fitted into headlamps designed for halogen, which scatter glare. The tester checks the beam pattern and aim with a beam-setting unit, and a lamp knocked out of line in a tight car park will show up.

Tyres wear quickly on heavy SUVs and powerful cars, especially in the stop-start traffic around the station and The Broadway and down the steep A219 Wimbledon Hill Road. Wide, low-profile tyres also hide inner-edge wear that's hard to see without the car on a lift. If the car fails, we explain every item plainly and give a written quote, using OE or OE-equivalent parts where repairs are needed. Leave it with us for repair and retest within 10 working days and the partial retest is free.

MOT

MOT Testing: what’s involved

The MOT is the annual legal check that a vehicle meets minimum road safety and environmental standards. It is required once a car is three years old, then every year. The test covers brakes, steering, suspension, tyres, lights, seatbelts, visibility, the body and structure, and exhaust emissions. It is a check of condition on the day, not a guarantee of the car's condition for the year ahead, and it does not look inside the engine, clutch or gearbox.

Defects are graded as minor, major or dangerous. Minor defects are recorded but the car still passes. A major or dangerous defect is a fail. Advisories are items to keep an eye on, such as tyres close to the legal limit. Class 4 covers cars, and vans and other goods vehicles up to 3,000kg design gross weight.

Common reasons cars fail the MOT

Lights and signalling

Blown bulbs, cloudy or misaimed headlamps and faulty indicators are among the most common failures. Most are quick and cheap to fix before the test.

Tyres

Tread below 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tread, cuts, bulges or exposed cords are a fail. A tyre pressure monitoring warning light on cars first used from 1 January 2012 also fails.

Brakes

Worn pads or discs, uneven braking side to side, a weak handbrake, or a brake or ABS warning light on the dash will fail the test.

Suspension

Broken coil springs, worn ball joints, split bushes and leaking shock absorbers are frequent failures, especially on cars that see a lot of potholes.

Visibility and wipers

Chips or cracks in the driver's line of sight, worn wiper blades and an empty washer bottle can all cause a fail.

Emissions and warning lights

High exhaust emissions, visible smoke from a diesel with a particulate filter, or an engine management light that is on will fail the test.

Corrosion and structure

Excessive corrosion within 30cm of a load-bearing mounting, such as a suspension or seatbelt mounting, is a fail on older cars.

When is your MOT due?

A car's first MOT is due by the third anniversary of its registration, then every 12 months. The expiry date is on your last certificate and on the gov.uk MOT history service. You can have the test up to a month, minus a day, before the expiry date and keep the same renewal date, so booking early costs you nothing. Driving without a valid MOT is only allowed to a pre-booked test or to repairs needed to pass.

  • First MOT by the car's third anniversary of registration
  • Then every 12 months
  • Up to a month (minus a day) early without losing your renewal date
  • After fitting new lights, tyres or wipers, if the old ones were close to failing
  • Class 4 covers cars and goods vehicles up to 3,000kg design gross weight

How our MOT works

  1. Booking and check-inWe confirm the registration and the vehicle class, and note anything you are worried about. You can wait or leave the car with us.
  2. Visual and interior checksThe tester checks seatbelts, seats, mirrors, the horn, warning lights, the windscreen, wipers and washers, doors, the VIN and number plates.
  3. Lights and electricsEvery light is checked for operation, colour and condition, and headlamp aim is measured with a beam-setting unit.
  4. Brake testBraking performance and balance are measured on a roller brake tester, and pads, discs, pipes and hoses are inspected where they can be seen.
  5. Underside inspectionFrom underneath the tester checks steering, suspension, driveshafts, the exhaust, fuel lines and for corrosion in load-bearing areas.
  6. Emissions testPetrol cars have exhaust gases measured with a gas analyser, and diesels have a smoke test. The tester also checks for signs of a removed or tampered particulate filter.
  7. Result and paperworkThe result is recorded on the DVSA system straight away. You get the certificate or a list of failures and advisories with the defect category for each.
  8. Repairs and retestIf the car fails, we explain each item and give you a written quote. Once repairs are approved and done, the retest is handled under the DVSA rules.

MOT Testing in Wimbledon: your questions answered

Will an airbag warning light fail the MOT?

Yes. An airbag or seatbelt pretensioner warning light that stays on is a fail, because it suggests the system may not work in a crash. Common causes include a faulty seat connector or clock spring. We can diagnose it before the test and quote for the repair.

My car has aftermarket LED bulbs in the headlamps. Will they pass?

Headlamps have to work as designed, with the correct beam pattern and colour. Fitting LED or HID bulbs into units designed for halogen bulbs is usually a fail because they scatter light. If you're unsure, we can check before the test.

How can I avoid the town-centre traffic when bringing the car over?

The town centre around the station and The Broadway is congested for much of the day, so a mid-morning slot is usually easiest. From Wimbledon Chase, heading for the A24 towards Morden avoids the worst of it. Or we can collect the car for you.

Is the retest free?

If you leave the car with us for repair and it is retested within 10 working days, the partial retest is free. If you take it away and bring it back within 10 working days, a partial retest fee may apply, except for certain minor items, such as lamps, wipers, mirrors, seatbelts or tyres, which are free if you return before the end of the next working day. After 10 working days a full test at the full fee is needed.

Can I drive my car if it fails?

If your current MOT hasn't expired and there are no dangerous defects, you can still drive it, but major defects should be fixed straight away. With a dangerous defect you must not drive it. If the MOT has expired, you can only drive to a pre-booked MOT or to have the failures repaired.

How early can I get my MOT done?

Up to a month, minus a day, before the current certificate expires, and the new certificate will still run to the same date the following year. Testing earlier than that is allowed, but the new certificate will then run for a year from the test date instead.

Does the MOT check the engine and gearbox?

No. The MOT doesn't cover the condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox. It checks emissions, fluid leaks and warning lights, but a clean pass doesn't mean the car doesn't need a service.

What is the difference between a minor and a major defect?

A minor defect has no significant effect on safety or the environment, so the car passes and the item is recorded. A major defect could affect safety or the environment and is a fail. A dangerous defect is an immediate risk, and the car should not be driven until it is fixed.
