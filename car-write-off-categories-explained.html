Write-Off Categories Explained

Four categories, and the only thing that matters first is which side of the line yours falls on. Two mean the car is finished. Two mean it is repairable and the decision is yours.

The short answer

  • A and B never return to the road. S and N can.
  • A write-off is arithmetic, not a safety verdict.
  • S is structural and needs DVLA notification; N is not and does not.
  • Say you want to keep the car before you accept the settlement.

All four, in one table

CategoryStructural damageBack on the road?What happens
AIrrelevantNeverEntire vehicle crushed, parts included
BSevereNeverBody shell crushed; serviceable parts may be reused

NNoYes, if repairedNon-structural damage; repairable

A and B are disposal decisions and there is nothing to discuss. S and N are financial decisions, and that is where people have options they do not realise they have.

The thing nobody explains

A write-off is not a safety verdict. It is arithmetic. The insurer compares what the repair costs against what the car is worth, less what the salvage is worth, and writes it off when repairing costs more than paying you out.

That is why a ten-year-old car with a damaged wing and door can be written off while identical damage on a three-year-old car is simply repaired. The damage is the same. The value either side of it is not.

S and N, the difference that matters

S is structural. The chassis, crumple zones, suspension mounting points or something else that carries load has been damaged. It can be repaired properly, but it has to be done properly, and the DVLA must be notified — the car is issued a new V5C.

N is non-structural. Panels, lights, electrics, cosmetics. The shell is sound. There is no DVLA notification and you keep the same V5C, though the category is still recorded against the vehicle.

Both show on a history check for ever, and both knock money off what the car will later sell for.

The old C and D

Before October 2017 the repairable categories were C and D, split on cost rather than on structure. Plenty of cars on the road still carry those markers and they have not been reclassified.

OldRoughly equivalent toNote
CSRepair cost exceeded value — said nothing about structure
DNCheaper damage, written off on economics alone

If you are buying a car marked C or D, the marker tells you less than S or N would. Get it inspected rather than assuming.

What to do if yours has been written off

  1. Ask which category, in writing. It changes everything that follows.
  2. Do not accept the settlement yet. Once the car goes to salvage it is gone, and so is the option to keep it.
  3. Check the valuation. They owe you market value, not the cheapest figure in a guide.
  4. Get a real repair quote. Then you can compare keeping it against taking the money, with numbers rather than guesses.

Your questions answered

Can I refuse to let them write it off?

You cannot force them to pay for a repair that costs more than the car is worth. You can ask to retain the salvage and repair it yourself, which is a different thing and usually possible.

Does a write-off always mean a payout?

On a comprehensive policy for a fault or non-fault accident, yes — the market value less any excess. Third-party-only cover does not pay for your own car.

Will I be told the category straight away?

Not always. It is set after the engineer assesses the car, so ask explicitly rather than waiting to be told.

Is a Cat S car safe to buy?

It can be, if it was repaired properly, and that is the whole question. Get an independent inspection. The category tells you what was damaged, not how well it was put right.
