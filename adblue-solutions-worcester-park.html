Plenty of A240 miles, so why the AdBlue fault?

Drivers from Worcester Park are sometimes puzzled by an AdBlue fault. Their diesel isn't a school-run car. Many households here run two cars, and the diesel is often the one kept for longer journeys. It regularly heads out along the A240 to join the A3, or past Nonsuch Park towards Ewell, runs at a steady speed and gets thoroughly hot, which is exactly what an SCR system likes. So why the warning?

The answer is usually wear rather than use. A diesel that covers big mileages reaches the age where the AdBlue pump, the tank heater or a NOx sensor simply comes to the end of its life. Wiring and connectors under the car take years of spray and road grime, and a poor connection can produce faults that come and go. Crystallisation still happens on high-mileage cars too, often from a slow leak at a joint rather than short trips.

Because the fault could be almost anywhere in the system, we test it in order. Codes and freeze-frame data first, then the dosing test, pump pressure and heater current, a close look at the wiring and connectors, and finally the fluid itself. That tells us whether a single component has failed or something else is causing it. Where a fault only appears on longer drives, we road test the car for long enough to reproduce it. AdBlue System Repairs: what’s involved

Many modern diesels, and nearly all built to the later Euro 6 standards, use selective catalytic reduction (SCR) to cut nitrogen oxide (NOx) emissions. AdBlue, a mix of urea and deionised water, is pumped from its own tank and sprayed into the exhaust by a dosing module ahead of the SCR catalyst. There it turns harmful NOx into nitrogen and water. NOx sensors before and after the catalyst tell the ECU whether the system is working, and a quality sensor checks the fluid in the tank.

Because the system is part of the car's emissions approval, the law requires it to enforce itself. If the ECU detects a fault, low fluid or poor-quality AdBlue, it shows a warning and starts a countdown. When that reaches zero, the engine will not restart once switched off. Common faults include crystallised AdBlue blocking the dosing module, failed pumps and tank heaters, and NOx sensor failures.

Signs of an AdBlue system fault

AdBlue or SCR warning light

A warning that stays on after the tank has been topped up points to a system fault rather than low fluid.

No-start countdown

A message such as 'engine start not possible in X miles' means the ECU has detected a fault. Once it reaches zero the engine will not restart after being switched off.

Engine management light

NOx sensor, dosing and SCR efficiency faults usually also light the engine management light and store emissions-related codes.

White crystals around the filler or exhaust

AdBlue leaves white urea crystals as it dries. Deposits around the dosing module, pipes or tank point to a leak or a blocked injector.

AdBlue use too low or too high

If the tank level barely drops, the system may not be dosing. If it empties much faster than usual, look for a leak.

Faults in cold weather

AdBlue freezes at around minus 11°C and relies on heaters in the tank and lines. A heater failure often shows up as a warning on cold mornings.

Wrong fluid in the tank

Diesel, water or screenwash in the AdBlue tank, or AdBlue in the fuel tank, needs dealing with before the car is driven any further.

When should it be done?

Book in as soon as an AdBlue or SCR warning appears that topping up doesn't clear. Don't wait for the countdown to run out, as once it reaches zero the car will not restart and may need recovery. If the wrong fluid has gone into either tank, don't start the engine. AdBlue itself should be topped up with fluid meeting ISO 22241, and the tank refilled well before it runs low.

  • Any AdBlue or SCR warning that remains after topping up
  • As soon as a no-start countdown message appears
  • Emissions or NOx-related codes with the engine management light on
  • Visible white crystal deposits or leaks around the AdBlue system
  • Immediately if the wrong fluid has been put in either tank

If it’s left

An AdBlue fault won't clear itself. Once the countdown starts, the car will eventually refuse to restart, which usually happens at the least convenient time and may mean recovery. A crystallising dosing module tends to get worse, and a NOx sensor or dosing fault left in place keeps the engine management light on, which is an MOT failure. Acting early usually means a simpler repair.

AdBlue System Repairs in Worcester Park: your questions answered

My diesel does lots of motorway miles. Why has the AdBlue system failed?

Long, hot runs reduce crystallisation, but they don't stop components wearing out. Pumps, heaters and NOx sensors all have a finite life, and high mileage brings that forward. We test each part to find which one has failed.

Why does the AdBlue warning disappear for a few days, then come back?

Intermittent warnings often come from a poor electrical connection or a sensor on the edge of failing. The car logs the fault when the reading drops out and may clear it when it recovers. We check the wiring and connectors as well as the parts themselves.

Can you replace just one NOx sensor, or do they go in pairs?

We replace the one that has failed. There's no rule that they must be changed in pairs, although on a high-mileage car we'll tell you if the other is showing signs of wear so you can decide.

Can you delete or disable the AdBlue system?

No. AdBlue delete software and emulators are illegal for road use because they remove part of the car's emissions control. They can also affect insurance and cause problems at MOT. We repair the system so it works as designed.

I've topped up but the light is still on. Why?

Some cars need a short drive or a few ignition cycles to recognise a refill. If the warning stays, the fault is usually in the dosing module, pump, heater, NOx sensors or quality sensor, and needs a diagnostic check.

I put AdBlue in the fuel tank. What should I do?

Don't start the engine. AdBlue can damage the fuel pump and injectors, so the tank and fuel lines need draining and flushing. The same applies to diesel in the AdBlue tank, which damages the pump and seals.

What causes crystallisation?

AdBlue leaves urea crystals behind as it dries, especially around the hot dosing module. Leaks, lots of short trips and a dosing module that isn't spraying properly all make it worse. Deposits can block the nozzle and trigger SCR efficiency faults.

Can I use any AdBlue?

Use fluid meeting ISO 22241, sold as AdBlue or diesel exhaust fluid. Never dilute it with water. Keep it sealed and out of heat, because old or contaminated fluid can trigger quality sensor warnings.
