What rear bumper actually is

Rear bumper is moulded thermoplastic over a rear crash bar. It unclips and unbolts, though the boot trim usually has to come out to reach the top fixings.

Behind it sits reversing sensors, usually a reversing camera, the reflectors, the rear crash bar, and on many cars a cut-out around a towbar or the exhaust tips. All of that comes out or gets protected before any paint goes near the car, and anything electrical is tested again before you collect it.

Rear bumpers take two completely different kinds of damage and they want different work. A shunt from behind pushes the centre in and usually only needs the skin repaired and repainted. Reversing into something cracks a corner, and the corner is exactly where the mounting bracket lives, so the repair has to rebuild the fixing as well as the face.

How rear bumpers get damaged

Four causes account for most of what comes through the door:

  • Reversing into posts and bollards, which cracks one corner rather than the centre.
  • Being reversed into while parked, which pushes the centre in.
  • Towbar loading and trailer work, which stresses the area around the mount.
  • Tight parallel parking, which scuffs the full width at bumper height.

The thing worth checking. The towbar area, if there is one. Damage around a towbar mount can have pushed the rear crash bar, and that is structural rather than cosmetic. It is also the one place where an insurer will want it properly inspected before authorising.

Repair or replace

Rear bumpers on common cars are cheap enough that replacement is sometimes the sensible answer on heavy corner damage. On anything with a parking camera or an unusual finish, repair is normally better value once the parts are priced.

How long it takes. A corner repair is a day or two. A full bumper off, repaired and painted is two to three days.

The reversing sensor problem

Almost every rear bumper now carries sensors, and they are bonded into holes moulded at specific positions. That matters for repair in a way it does not at the front: a rear bumper that has been repaired by someone who filled over a sensor aperture, or refitted a sensor at a slightly different angle, will read the ground as an obstacle and beep at nothing.

We test every sensor on the car before it goes back, not just refit them. If a sensor was already failing before the accident we will tell you, rather than leaving you to find out in a car park.

Towbars, and the thing nobody checks

If your car has a towbar, the rear of the shell is a different structure. The towbar mounts to the chassis legs through the rear crash bar, and a shunt into a towed car or a post transmits straight into that mounting rather than being absorbed by the bumper the way it would be on a car without one.

The bumper skin can look like the whole story and not be. We check the crash bar and the towbar mounting on any rear impact where a towbar is fitted, because a bent mounting is both a structural repair and a towing safety problem, and it is the single most commonly missed thing on a rear-end job.

It also matters for the claim. An insurer will want structural damage identified and documented properly, and finding it after the bumper has already been painted means raising a supplementary and starting again.

Your questions answered

Can you repair rear bumper without painting it?

Only where the damage is in the lacquer alone, and we will tell you if yours is one of those rather than selling you a respray. Once it is through the colour, refinishing is the only repair that lasts. On dents there is no paint-free option at all — we do not do paintless dent removal, so the panel is straightened and then painted.

Will the colour match?

It is mixed to the paint code on your car, then sprayed into both rear quarters and the lower edge of the boot lid rather than stopped at the shut line. Two cars with the same code are rarely identical after a few years of sun, and blending is what makes the difference invisible instead of nearly right.

Is it guaranteed?

One year on paint, three months on mechanical work, in writing.

Should I claim on insurance?

At £140 to £540 it is worth checking your excess first. Plenty of single-panel jobs cost less than the excess, and paying directly avoids declaring a claim for five years. We will price it either way and we are not on any insurer’s panel, so we have no reason to push you one way.

Do you need to see the car first?

Not to give you a price band — put your registration in the box above. We confirm the exact figure when we see it, because how far the damage goes is the one thing photographs do not show reliably.
