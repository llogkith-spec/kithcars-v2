Metallic, Pearl and Three-Stage Paint

Why flake changes everything

A solid colour is the same colour from any angle, because it is pigment and nothing else. Add aluminium flake and the paint now reflects light directionally: the shade you see depends on how the flake is lying and where you are standing.

That means spray technique matters as much as the mix. Two painters with the same tin can produce visibly different panels, because gun distance, air pressure and how the coats are laid down all change how the flake orients as the paint flashes off.

It is also why a metallic is blended into the next panel even when the mix is perfect. A perfect mix sprayed slightly differently still reads as a different panel in raking light.

Pearl and three-stage

Pearl uses ceramic particles rather than aluminium. They do not just reflect light, they refract it, which is where the colour shift comes from — a pearl white that goes faintly gold or pink depending on the angle.

Three-stage takes that further: a solid base, then a translucent tinted mid-coat, then lacquer. The final colour depends on how many coats of the mid went on and how evenly. Two coats and three coats are different colours, and there is no correcting it after the lacquer goes on.

This is why a three-stage repair is always sprayed out on a test card first, with the mid-coat applied in the same number of passes it will get on the car.

What it means for your repair

  • Solid white or black. Forgiving. A competent repair will match.
  • Metallic silver or grey. The most common finish on the road and genuinely tricky, because silver shows flake orientation more than any other colour.
  • Pearl white. Very common on modern cars and one of the hardest things in the trade.
  • Three-stage reds and pearls. Where blending stops being good practice and becomes the only way.

Your questions answered

How do I know which my car has?

The paint code tells us. Broadly: if the colour looks different as you walk past it, there is flake or pearl in it. Pearl whites and three-stage reds are the usual ones people are surprised by.

Is a pearl repair more likely to go wrong?

It is less forgiving, which is not the same thing. Done with a spray-out card and a proper blend, it matches. Done from the code with no test card, it is the finish most likely to show.

Can you repair a colour-change or wrapped car?

Painted colour changes, yes — we match what is on the car now. A wrap is a different trade: we repair the panel underneath and you have it re-wrapped.

Does a harder finish take longer?

A little, mostly in the matching rather than the spraying. It is usually within the same timescale band.
