ABS Sensor Replacement: what’s involved

Each wheel has a speed sensor that reads a toothed or magnetic ring turning with the hub or driveshaft. The ABS module constantly compares the four signals. If one wheel slows too quickly under braking, the system releases and reapplies that brake to stop it locking. The same signals feed traction control, stability control and often the speedometer, hill-hold and tyre pressure warning.

Older cars use passive sensors that generate their own signal. Most modern cars use active sensors, which are powered by the ABS module and often read a magnetic encoder built into the wheel bearing seal. Sensors sit right next to the brakes, exposed to heat, water, salt and brake dust, so corrosion, damaged wiring and failed connectors are common. When a signal drops out, the system switches ABS and stability control off and lights the warning. Your normal brakes still work.

Signs of an ABS sensor fault

ABS warning light on

A steady ABS light means the system has switched itself off. Normal braking still works, but the wheels can lock under heavy braking.

Traction and stability lights too

Because they rely on the same wheel speed signals, the traction control or ESP light usually comes on with the ABS light.

Light comes and goes

An intermittent warning, often after bumps, in wet weather or at a certain speed, usually points to damaged wiring, a loose connector or a corroded sensor.

ABS pulsing at low speed

If the pedal pulses and the ABS activates when stopping gently, a sensor may be giving a false reading from a dirty sensor or a cracked reluctor ring.

Speedometer or tyre warning faults

On cars that take road speed or tyre pressure monitoring from the wheel sensors, a faulty sensor can upset the speedometer or set a tyre warning.

Other systems disabled

Hill-hold, cruise control, stop-start and some electric parking brake functions may stop working when a wheel speed signal is missing.

When should it be done?

ABS sensors have no replacement interval. They are replaced when they fail or when their wiring is damaged. Get the car checked as soon as the ABS light comes on, because the car won't stop as well in an emergency on a wet road without ABS and stability control. An ABS warning light that stays on is also an MOT failure. Faults often show up after brake, hub or suspension work, when a sensor or its wiring may have been disturbed.

  • ABS, traction or stability warning light on
  • Intermittent warnings in wet weather or over bumps
  • Warning lights after brake, wheel bearing or suspension work
  • Before an MOT if the ABS light is showing

How we replace an ABS sensor

  1. Check-in and symptomsWe ask when the light appears and whether any work has been done recently, as disturbed wiring after brake or suspension work is common.
  2. Diagnostic scanWe read the ABS module fault codes to identify which wheel is affected and whether the fault is signal, circuit or plausibility related.
  3. Live data and testingWe compare live wheel speed readings on a road test or ramp and test the sensor circuit with a multimeter or oscilloscope to separate sensor, wiring and ring faults.
  4. Inspect the ring and bearingWe check the reluctor ring or magnetic encoder for cracks, corrosion or damage and check the wheel bearing for play, as either can cause a false signal.
  5. Written quoteWe explain the cause and give you a written quote. If the encoder is built into the wheel bearing, we'll explain why the bearing may need replacing.
  6. Replace the sensorWe carefully remove the old sensor, which is often seized in the hub, clean the mounting, and fit the new sensor with its wiring routed and secured correctly.
  7. Clear codes and road testWe clear the fault codes, road test to confirm all four wheel speeds read correctly and the warning lights stay off, and run any calibration the car needs.

ABS Sensor Replacement in Purley: your questions answered

Can driving through floodwater cause an ABS fault?

It can. Sensors and connectors sit low down by the hubs, and prolonged exposure to water and grime can corrode them or get into damaged wiring. It may not show up straight away, so if the light appears in the weeks after, mention it when you book.

Why has my hill-hold stopped working at the same time as the ABS light?

Hill-hold and some electronic parking brake functions rely on wheel speed signals. When a sensor fails, the car switches them off along with ABS and stability control. Fixing the sensor usually restores them.

Is a seized ABS sensor expensive to remove?

It can take longer, and occasionally the sensor breaks and needs drilling out. We'll tell you if yours looks badly seized before we start, and include it in your written quote.

Is it safe to drive with the ABS light on?

The car will still brake normally, so you can usually drive it carefully to the garage. But without ABS and stability control the wheels can lock under hard braking, so leave extra distance. If the brake warning light is on as well, get advice before driving.

Will an ABS light fail the MOT?

Yes. An ABS warning light that stays on, or doesn't come on when the ignition is switched on, is an MOT failure. The same applies to the electronic stability control warning on cars that have it.

Can I just clear the code?

If the fault is still there, the ABS module will find it again, usually within a short drive. Clearing codes without finding the cause just delays the repair.

Why might I need a wheel bearing as well?

On many modern cars the magnetic ring that the sensor reads is built into the wheel bearing seal. If that encoder is damaged or the bearing has excessive play, a new sensor won't cure the fault, and the bearing has to be replaced.

Why did the light come on after my brakes were done?

ABS sensors and their wiring sit close to the brakes and hubs, so they can be knocked, stretched or left unplugged during brake or suspension work. Sometimes an old sensor that was already corroded fails when disturbed. A quick scan shows which wheel is affected.
