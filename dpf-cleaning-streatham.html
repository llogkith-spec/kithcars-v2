DPF Cleaning: what’s involved

A diesel particulate filter (DPF) sits in the exhaust and traps soot from the engine. It has to be emptied by burning that soot off, which is called regeneration. Passive regeneration happens on its own when the exhaust runs hot for long enough, typically on steady motorway or A-road driving. When soot builds up, the ECU starts an active regeneration: it adds extra fuel late in the combustion cycle to raise exhaust temperature and burn the soot away. This needs a sustained run, usually 10–20 minutes at a steady speed.

Short journeys interrupt these regenerations, so soot keeps building until the DPF warning light comes on and the car may go into limp mode. Burning soot also leaves behind ash, which cannot be burnt off and slowly fills the filter over the car's life. A blocked DPF is usually a symptom, so finding the cause matters as much as clearing the filter.

Signs your DPF is blocked

DPF warning light

The light comes on when the soot load passes a set level and a regeneration hasn't completed. On many cars, a sustained drive at steady speed can clear it if you act early.

Limp mode and loss of power

Once the soot load gets too high the ECU limits power to protect the engine and filter. At this stage a drive will not normally clear it.

Engine management or glow plug light

Some makes show a blocked filter or a failed regeneration through the engine or glow plug light rather than a dedicated DPF symbol.

Cooling fan running after switch-off

A fan that keeps running and a raised idle can mean a regeneration was in progress when you stopped. Regular interrupted regenerations are an early warning sign.

Rising oil level

Repeated failed regenerations can wash diesel past the piston rings into the sump. A rising oil level or an oil service warning soon after a service needs checking promptly.

Higher fuel consumption

Frequent active regenerations use extra fuel, so a noticeable drop in economy can point to a filter that is loading up quickly.

Differential pressure sensor faults

The ECU judges soot load from a pressure sensor across the filter. A failed sensor or split pipe can give false readings and stop regenerations happening at all.

When should it be done?

Act as soon as the DPF light appears. Try a steady 20-minute drive on a dual carriageway first if the handbook allows it. If the light stays on, or the car goes into limp mode, book it in before the soot load climbs further, because a heavily blocked filter can go past the point where a forced regeneration is safe. Cars used mostly for short trips, or with a history of EGR, injector or sensor faults, are the most likely to need attention.

  • DPF warning light that stays on after a sustained drive
  • Limp mode on a diesel with DPF or glow plug warnings
  • Rising oil level between services on a DPF-equipped diesel
  • Cars used mainly for short journeys or stop-start town driving
  • Higher-mileage filters where ash loading is building up (varies widely by engine and use)

How we clean a DPF

  1. Check-in and historyWe ask how the car is used and whether the light has appeared before. Short-trip driving and previous regeneration faults tell us a lot.
  2. Diagnostic scanWe read all fault codes and check live data for soot load, ash load, differential pressure, exhaust temperatures and the number of failed regenerations.
  3. Find the root causeWe check the differential pressure sensor and pipes, exhaust temperature sensors, EGR valve, injectors, boost system and oil level, as any of these can stop the filter regenerating.
  4. Fix underlying faultsAny faulty sensor or component is quoted and, once approved, repaired first. Cleaning a filter without fixing the cause is money wasted.
  5. Clean the filterWhere the filter is suitable, we apply a professional DPF cleaning treatment to break down the soot, then run a forced regeneration with diagnostic equipment while monitoring temperatures.
  6. Reset and relearnWe reset soot and ash values where required and carry out any adaptations the manufacturer specifies, for example after a sensor or additive fluid top-up.
  7. Road test and verifyWe road test the car and recheck the live data to confirm the pressure readings and soot load are back within the normal range.
  8. Handover adviceWe explain what caused the blockage and how to drive and maintain the car so regenerations can complete in future.

If it’s left

Driving on with a blocked DPF usually makes things worse. Soot load keeps rising until a safe forced regeneration is no longer possible and the filter has to be replaced, which costs far more than cleaning. Questions

DPF Cleaning in Streatham: your questions answered

Does 20mph driving on Streatham High Road cause DPF problems?

Slow, short journeys make it hard for the filter to regenerate, whatever the limit. The problem is the lack of sustained heat, so an occasional longer run at a steady speed makes a real difference.

My fan keeps running after I park on my street. Is something wrong?

It often means a regeneration was in progress when you switched off. Once in a while is normal, but if it happens most times, the filter is loading up and it's worth having it checked before the warning light comes on.

Can you collect a diesel in limp mode from Streatham?

Yes, we can arrange collection and delivery. With the traffic on Mitcham Lane and through Mitcham, it's usually easier than driving a car on reduced power.

Can I remove the DPF instead?

No. Removing or tampering with a DPF is illegal for road use, and since 2018 the MOT fails any car where the filter has been removed or shows evidence of tampering. A DPF-equipped diesel showing visible smoke on the emissions test also fails. We don't carry out DPF removal or delete software.

Why does my DPF keep blocking?

The most common reason is lots of short journeys that never let a regeneration finish. Faulty differential pressure sensors, EGR valves, injectors, thermostats or boost leaks can also stop the filter regenerating or make the engine produce more soot, so we check these every time.

Will a motorway run clear the light?

Often, if you catch it early. A steady run of around 20 minutes at a consistent speed, in a lower gear to keep revs up if your handbook suggests it, can let an active regeneration complete. If the car is already in limp mode, driving is unlikely to clear it.

Is cleaning always enough?

Cleaning removes soot, but it cannot remove the ash that builds up over many years of regenerations, and it can't repair a cracked or melted filter. If the data shows high ash load or the filter is damaged, we will tell you honestly and quote for a replacement.

Do DPF cleaning additives from a shop work?

Fuel additives may help a healthy filter regenerate a little more easily, but they won't clear a blocked filter or fix a sensor fault. Some Peugeot and Citroën systems use a separate additive tank that the car genuinely needs, and that is a different thing.
