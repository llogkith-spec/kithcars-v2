Kerb Damage Repair

Kerb damage is the damage people live with longest, because it hides from face-on and most of us look at our cars from the front. Walk to the end of the car and look along it — that is the view everyone else gets.

Is this what you have?

Four checks, in order. They take about two minutes in daylight and they decide which repair you are actually looking at.

  1. A long horizontal graze low on the panel, often with the paint back to primer in patches rather than evenly. A long horizontal graze low on the panel, often with the paint back to primer in patches rather than evenly.
  2. Frequently carries grey or black transfer from the kerb or bollard itself. Frequently carries grey or black transfer from the kerb or bollard itself.
  3. Check the wheel on the same corner. Kerbing a sill or a bumper corner usually kerbs the alloy as well, and the two are often quoted separately when they should not be.
  4. Look for cracking at the ends of the graze on a plastic sill cover, where it has been forced away from its clips. Look for cracking at the ends of the graze on a plastic sill cover, where it has been forced away from its clips.

How we repair it

The transferred material comes off first. The damaged run is then flatted, primed where the colour has gone, and refinished along its length, blended upward into the panel so the top edge of the repair does not read as a line in raking light.

On a plastic sill cover or arch moulding, we check the clips and mounting points as well: a cover that has been dragged along a kerb is frequently no longer fully attached at the bottom edge.

What you can try yourself

Compound will take off the transferred kerb colour and sometimes that is most of what you can see. It will not restore primer-deep damage. Worth ten minutes before you decide whether to get it quoted.

When to stop. If two careful attempts have not improved it, stop there. Almost every job we see that cost more than it should have is one where a reasonable first attempt turned into a fourth.

How soon it needs doing

Not urgent on plastic. On a metal sill or an arch lip it is worth dealing with before winter, because kerb damage sits exactly where road salt and standing water collect.

Your questions answered

It also kerbed the alloy. Can you do both?

Yes, and it is worth doing together. Alloy refurbishment runs £108 to £144 per wheel including VAT, depending on how badly the face is gouged and whether the finish is diamond-cut.

Can the sill cover be repaired or does it need replacing?

Depends on the finish. A painted cover repairs well. A textured black one cannot be polished back without going shiny in patches, so it is repaint or replace, and on common cars a new moulding is sometimes the cheaper of the two.

Will it happen again in the same place?

If it is a ramp or a drive you use daily, honestly yes. It is worth considering protective film on that corner if it is a known pinch point.

Does kerb damage affect the car structurally?

Not usually — it is a surface graze. What is worth checking after a hard kerb strike is the wheel and the suspension geometry on that corner, which is a separate job from the paint.
