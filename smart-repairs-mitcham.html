Mitcham Common speeds and terraced parking: two sources of damage

The A236 Croydon Road across Mitcham Common is one of the few roads around here that feels open, and speeds creep up between the junctions. Higher speed means stones from the car in front arrive harder, which is why Mitcham cars that use it daily often show a cluster of small chips on the bonnet edge and front bumper. Each one is tiny, but once through to bare metal they will start to rust, and on a light-coloured car they show up quickly.

Back in the residential streets of Longthornton and around Figges Marsh, terraces with parking on both sides leave single-file gaps. Mirrors get clipped, door edges catch as people get out against a neighbour's car, and rear corners get scuffed during parallel parking. These are exactly the marks SMART repair was developed for: small, close to an edge, and not worth refinishing a whole panel over. A small mark on a bumper corner also tends to get knocked again, so sorting it before it grows is worthwhile.

We treat the two types a little differently. A chipped front edge is flatted, filled and primed chip by chip before the colour is laid on and faded back into the original paint. A scuffed bumper corner, being plastic, gets adhesion promoter and flexible primer so the repair moves with the bumper. Either way, we check the colour on a test card against your car and put the price in a written quote before any work starts.

Mitcham is close enough that a lot of people drop the car off on the way to work. SMART Repairs: what’s involved

A conventional paint repair refinishes the whole panel and blends colour into the panels either side. SMART repair confines the work to the damaged area: it is prepared, primed and painted within a small zone, then the colour and clear coat are faded into the surrounding original paint so the edge can't be seen. Less paint, less masking and less dismantling means a shorter job and a lower price.

It works best on bumper corners, door edges, mirror covers and other areas near the edge of a panel, where the damage is roughly palm-sized or smaller. It isn't the right choice for damage in the middle of a large flat panel such as a bonnet or roof, long scratches that cross panels, or some colours that are difficult to blend in a small area. There, a conventional repair gives a more reliable result, and we'll tell you honestly which suits your car.

Scuffed spoilers and painted trim

Edges of spoilers, sill covers and painted trim pieces that take knocks.

When should it be done?

SMART repair is most useful while the damage is still small. A scuff or chip caught early can often be dealt with in a few hours, whereas one left to spread, lift or rust may need a full panel refinish. It's popular before lease or PCP returns and before a sale, when a handful of small marks can affect the price. If the damage crosses a panel join or covers a large area, a conventional repair is usually the better choice.

  • Scuffs and chips roughly palm-sized or smaller
  • Damage near the edge or corner of a panel
  • As soon as bare metal shows, to stop rust starting
  • Before an end-of-lease inspection or sale

How a SMART repair works

  1. AssessWe check the size, position and colour to confirm SMART repair is suitable, then give you a written quote.
  2. Clean and maskThe area is degreased and a limited zone is masked, protecting the surrounding paint, glass and trim.
  3. Prepare the damageThe damaged paint is flatted back, chips or scratches are filled, and the edges are feathered smooth.
  4. PrimePrimer is applied to the repaired area only and flatted smooth once dry.
  5. Colour match and basecoatColour is mixed from your paint code, checked on a test card, and applied over the repair, fading out into the original paint.
  6. Clear coat and blendClear coat is applied over the repair and the edge is faded into the original finish with blending thinner.
  7. Cure and polishAfter controlled curing, the blend edge is polished so the gloss matches the rest of the panel.
  8. Inspect and hand overWe check the repair from different angles in good light and go through it with you.

Materials we use

SMART repair uses the same quality materials as a full panel repair, just in smaller quantities: a primer suited to metal or plastic, basecoat mixed to your paint code, and two-pack clear coat with blending thinner to fade the edge into the original finish. On plastic bumpers we use adhesion promoter and flexible primer so the repair can move with the bumper without cracking.

  • Fine filler and primer for the repair area
  • Basecoat mixed to your paint code
  • Two-pack clear coat
  • Blending thinner to fade the repair edge
  • Adhesion promoter and flexible primer for plastic parts

If it’s left

Small damage doesn't stay small. Chips and scratches through to bare steel rust, and the rust spreads under the paint around them, while damaged paint on bumper corners can lift and flake. Once the damage grows beyond a small area, SMART is no longer suitable and the job becomes a full panel refinish, which takes longer and costs more.

SMART Repairs in Mitcham: your questions answered

Is it worth repairing a handful of stone chips on the front of an older car?

If the chips have gone through to bare metal, it's worth sealing them before they rust, even on an older car. SMART repair on the front edge is often the cheapest proper fix. If the car is worth little, a careful touch-up may be enough, and we'll say so.

Can a SMART repair on a white car blend in properly?

Solid white is one of the easier colours to blend, but whites can yellow slightly with age, so we check the mixed colour against your car rather than relying only on the code. Pearl whites are harder, as they use a separate pearl layer, and may need a larger blend area.

Can I drop the car off on my way to work from Mitcham?

Yes, we open at 8am, and Hackbridge station is a short walk from us if you're carrying on by train. Most single repairs are ready the same day, and we'll call when it's done.

What does SMART stand for?

Small to Medium Area Repair Technique. It means refinishing only the damaged area and blending it into the surrounding original paint, rather than painting the whole panel.

Will I be able to see the repair?

When the damage suits SMART and the colour is matched well, the repair is very hard to spot. Blending is the key. Where we think a SMART repair would show, for example in the middle of a bonnet, we'll recommend a conventional repair instead.

Is a SMART repair as durable as a full panel repair?

It uses the same types of primer, basecoat and two-pack clear coat, so a well-prepared repair is durable. The blend edge is the most delicate area at first, so avoid machine polishing it or aiming a pressure washer at it for the first few weeks.

Is SMART repair suitable for metallic paint?

For most metallic colours, yes. Metallic, pearl and three-layer colours are harder to blend in a small area because the flake needs to sit the same way as the original, so for some colours a larger blend or a conventional repair gives a better match. We'll advise when we see the car.

Can you do SMART repairs at my home?

We carry out SMART repairs in our workshop, where we can control dust, temperature and curing, which gives a more consistent finish. If dropping the car off is awkward, we offer local collection and delivery.
