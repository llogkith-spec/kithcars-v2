Beddington Lane vans, the ULEZ and the BlueHDi AdBlue system

Past the Beddington Lane tram stop, the industrial estates keep plenty of Berlingo and Dispatch vans busy, and most of the newer ones run BlueHDi diesels. Those engines meet Euro 6, which is what keeps them clear of the ULEZ charge that has applied across Beddington since August 2023. The catch is that Euro 6 depends on the AdBlue system working, and a van that spends its day crawling between yards and recycling sites gives its pump, heater and NOx sensors a hard life.

When an AdBlue fault appears, the dashboard usually starts a countdown to a no-start, which is the last thing a working van needs. We read the fault codes, check the fluid quality and test the pump and heater before deciding whether the tank assembly has to be replaced, as it does on many models. Older HDi vans with an Eolys-type DPF get their additive level checked at the same visit, because a van that rarely sees a long run relies on that additive to regenerate.

Round Beddington village and the streets off Plough Lane, the Citroëns are more often family C3s, C4s and C5 Aircross SUVs with the 1.2 PureTech petrol. Those cars mostly do short trips, which is exactly the use where oil quality matters most for the wet timing belt. We gauge the belt at each service, check the oil level and condition, and tell you plainly where it sits against the revised replacement interval. Low mileage does not stretch that interval, because oil ages with time as well as miles.

Every job is quoted in writing before we start, and repairs carry our 12-month workmanship warranty. Citroën Servicing & Repairs: what’s involved

Citroën shares its engines with Peugeot, DS and other Stellantis brands, so the known issues are well understood. The three-cylinder 1.2 PureTech petrol uses a timing belt that runs inside the engine, bathed in oil. On many of these engines the belt degrades early and sheds material that can block the oil pump pickup and the oil feed to the brake vacuum pump.

On the diesel side, older 1.6 HDi engines are known for injector seal leaks, and BlueHDi models use AdBlue to cut NOx emissions, with their own pumps, heaters and sensors that can fail. Regular servicing with the right oil and checks of known weak points is the most effective way to avoid big bills. Using an independent garage to Citroën's schedule with parts of matching quality keeps the manufacturer warranty valid under the Block Exemption rules.

Common Citroën faults we fix

1.2 PureTech timing belt degradation

Low oil pressure warnings, engine fault messages or a hard brake pedal can all trace back to belt debris in the oil. We inspect the belt and measure it with the correct gauge.

PureTech oil consumption

Some PureTech engines use more oil than they should. Checking the level regularly between services matters, as running low adds to the risk of engine damage.

1.6 HDi injector seal leaks

A chuffing noise, a diesel or exhaust smell in the cabin and black carbon deposits around the injectors point to failed injector seals. Early replacement avoids seized injectors.

1.6 HDi turbo oil feed blockage

A blocked filter in the turbo oil feed pipe can starve the turbo of oil, leading to whining, smoke and turbo failure. We replace the feed pipe as part of any turbo repair.

BlueHDi AdBlue faults

Messages to top up AdBlue when the tank is full, or a countdown to a no-start, often mean a failed pump, heater or sensor. On many models these are built into the tank assembly.

Eolys additive and DPF warnings

Older HDi models with an additive-type DPF need their Eolys fluid refilling. When it runs out, the DPF cannot regenerate properly and a warning appears.

Automated manual gearbox faults

The EGS and ETG automated manual gearboxes in models such as the C3 and C4 Picasso can jerk, hesitate or drop into neutral, often due to clutch wear, actuator faults or a missing clutch relearn.

Electric parking brake faults

First-generation C4 Picasso and Grand C4 Picasso models are known for parking brake motor and control unit faults, with a parking brake faulty message.

When should your Citroën be serviced?

Citroën typically specifies a service every 12 months or at a mileage limit of around 10,000–20,000 miles depending on engine and year, whichever comes first. On the 1.2 PureTech, the correct oil and regular belt inspection matter more than the mileage, and the manufacturer has shortened the belt replacement interval on many versions. We check the current interval for your specific engine rather than relying on the original handbook.

  • Oil service: typically every 12 months or at your engine's mileage limit, whichever comes first
  • 1.2 PureTech: belt inspection at services, replacement at the current revised interval or sooner if the gauge shows wear
  • Eolys DPF additive (older HDi): refill at the scheduled interval or when warned
  • Brake fluid: typically every 2 years
  • MOT: first at 3 years, then every year

Citroën Servicing & Repairs in Beddington: your questions answered

Will a Citroën van working off Beddington Lane be ULEZ compliant?

Most diesel Berlingo and Dispatch vans registered from late 2016 onwards meet Euro 6 and are compliant, but it depends on the exact vehicle, so check the registration on TfL's vehicle checker. Staying compliant in practice means keeping the AdBlue and DPF systems working, which we check at every service.

Can my van be serviced early so it is back on the road in Beddington the same day?

We open at 8am Monday to Saturday, and a standard service usually takes 1.5–3 hours, so an early drop-off normally means collection the same day. Tell us if the van is needed back by a set time and we will plan around it. Anything extra is quoted before we do it.

My C3 only does short runs around Beddington. Does it still need a yearly service?

Yes. Citroën's schedule is time-based as well as mileage-based, and on a 1.2 PureTech the oil's condition matters for the timing belt even when the mileage is low. Short trips also let moisture and unburnt fuel collect in the oil, so an annual change is sensible.

Will servicing my Citroën with an independent garage affect the warranty?

No, provided the car is serviced to Citroën's schedule using parts of matching quality and the work is recorded. This is protected under the Block Exemption rules, which the UK retains through the Motor Vehicle Block Exemption Order. Warranty repairs and recalls are still carried out free of charge by a Citroën dealer, so we will tell you if something looks like a warranty or recall matter.

Is my 1.2 PureTech affected by the belt problem?

Many PureTech engines with the oil-bathed belt are affected to some degree, but the only way to know the condition of yours is to inspect and measure the belt. We can do this at a service and give you a clear picture. It may also be worth asking a Citroën dealer whether any manufacturer goodwill applies to your car.

What oil does a PureTech engine need?

It needs oil meeting the PSA/Stellantis B71 approval listed for your engine, usually a low-viscosity 0W or 5W grade. Using a non-approved oil is widely linked to faster belt degradation, so we always use the correct specification.

Why does my Citroën show an AdBlue fault when the tank is full?

The fault is usually in the pump, heater, level sensor or NOx sensor rather than the fluid itself. On many BlueHDi models the pump and heater are part of the tank, so the whole assembly is replaced. We diagnose the cause before quoting.

My HDi is chuffing and smells of diesel. What is it?

Most often it is a failed injector seal letting combustion gas escape. It is a routine repair if caught early, but if left, carbon builds up and the injector can seize, which makes the job longer.
