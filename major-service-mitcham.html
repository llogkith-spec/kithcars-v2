Water, debris and the filter standing between them

Mitcham runs on diesel vans. They come off the estates near Willow Lane and Streatham Road, out past Figges Marsh and the Three Kings Pond, across the Common on the A236 and back through the Fair Green one-way system. Those vehicles reach the 20,000 to 40,000-mile window where a serviceable fuel filter falls due quickly, and they often spend time with the tank low, which is where water condensing in a half-empty tank gets concentrated.

A diesel fuel filter does two jobs: it separates water and it traps fine particles, protecting a high-pressure pump built to tolerances measured in microns. Leave it restricted and the pump works against resistance until it wears, and the debris from that wear travels forward into the injectors. That is the difference between an hour of labour and a filter, and a fuel system rebuild. If your dashboard has shown a water-in-fuel warning, that is the filter asking to be drained.

We fit OE or OE-equivalent filters from suppliers such as MANN, Bosch or Delphi with the seals supplied in the kit, and we prime the system properly afterwards instead of cranking the starter until it fires. On petrol vehicles the equivalent item is a set of plugs, fitted to the specified type, heat range and gap and torqued correctly. Either way the brake fluid is changed completely and moisture tested before and after.

The full service is included: oil and oil filter, air filter, cabin filter, wheels off with brake measurements recorded, coolant and battery tests, fault code scan and a road test. Allow most of the day. We give you a written quote first, ring you when it is done, and guarantee our workmanship for 3 months. The run down is the A217, Cranmer Road and Carshalton Road, about eight minutes.

Local tip

Keep the tank above a quarter if the van sits over weekends. A half-empty diesel tank breathes damp air and condenses water into the fuel, and the filter can only hold so much of it before the warning light does your thinking for you.

Major Service: what’s involved

Some parts do their job quietly for years and then start costing money. Spark plug electrodes erode a fraction with every firing, and as the gap widens the ignition coil has to work harder to jump it, which is how a worn plug ends up taking a coil with it. A diesel fuel filter collects water and debris until flow through it falls away, starving a high-pressure pump built to very fine tolerances.

Brake fluid is hygroscopic: it draws moisture out of the air through the reservoir and the flexible hoses, and a few per cent of water is enough to pull its boiling point down towards where hard braking takes it. None of these three gives much warning before it becomes a problem. A major service renews them on schedule rather than after they fail, and it is the natural point to take stock of what is coming next.

Signs your car is due a major service

Two years or 24,000 miles since the last one

The longer-interval items run to time as well as distance, so the date matters even on a car that has not covered the mileage.

Hesitation or a stumble under load

Worn plugs struggle most when cylinder pressure is highest, so the misfire shows up pulling uphill or accelerating hard rather than at idle.

Engine management light flashing

A flashing light means a misfire serious enough to risk damaging the catalytic converter with unburnt fuel. Ease off and get it looked at rather than carrying on.

Longer cranking on a cold morning

A petrol engine that takes an extra second or two to fire when cold is often running plugs with a gap that has widened past its best.

A diesel that runs out of puff at speed

Power that falls away after a sustained motorway run, or under load with the car fully laden, can be a fuel filter restricting flow just when demand is highest.

Water in fuel warning on a diesel

Many diesel filters include a water trap with a sensor. The warning means the trap needs draining and the filter is usually at or past the end of its service life.

Brake pedal softens on a long descent

Fluid that has absorbed moisture boils at a lower temperature, and the vapour compresses where fluid would not. A pedal that goes long after repeated heavy braking points at fluid rather than pads.

No record of plugs or fluid ever being changed

On a car bought used with a patchy history, assume the long-interval items are overdue until the paperwork shows otherwise.

When should it be done?

For most cars a major service falls every 24 months or 24,000 miles, but the parts inside it run to their own intervals and they do not all come due together. Spark plug life depends on the type fitted: nickel plugs are often specified every 20,000 to 30,000 miles, while platinum and iridium plugs can run 40,000 to 60,000 miles or more. Diesel fuel filters are commonly every 2 years or 20,000 to 40,000 miles. Brake fluid is a strict two-year item on most cars whatever the mileage.

  • Typically every 24 months or 24,000 miles, or at your manufacturer's longer interval
  • Spark plugs: around 20,000 to 30,000 miles for nickel, 40,000 to 60,000 miles or more for platinum and iridium
  • Diesel fuel filter: commonly every 2 years or 20,000 to 40,000 miles where the filter is serviceable
  • Brake fluid: every 2 years on most cars, counted by time rather than mileage
  • Low-mileage cars: the time-based items still fall due even when the mileage items do not

How we carry out a major service

  1. Work out what is actually dueBefore quoting we check your engine's schedule and the service record to see which long-interval items genuinely fall now. If your plugs were done 8,000 miles ago we say so and leave them alone.
  2. Oil, oil filter and the annual filtersEngine oil and filter are renewed with Castrol to your manufacturer's approval, and the air and cabin filters are replaced as they would be at a full service.
  3. Spark plugs on petrol enginesCoil packs come off and each plug well is checked for oil from failed tube seals before the new plugs go in. They are tightened with a torque wrench to the manufacturer's figure, because over-tightening damages an aluminium head and under-tightening stops heat escaping through the seat.
  4. Fuel filter on dieselsWhere the car has a serviceable external filter we renew it with the seals from the kit, drain the water trap and prime the system so the car starts without long cranking. Some cars use an in-tank filter that is not a service item, and we will tell you if yours is one of them.
  5. Brake fluid changeFresh fluid from a sealed container is pushed through with a pressure bleeder in the sequence the manufacturer specifies, and on cars that require it the ABS pump is cycled with diagnostic equipment so old fluid is not left in the unit.
  6. Wheels off and full inspectionPad and disc thickness are measured and recorded, and suspension joints, bushes, gaiters, hoses, pipes and the exhaust are checked with the car raised and the wheels off.
  7. Battery, charging and diagnosticsThe battery is tested for condition, the charging output measured, and the control modules scanned for stored and pending fault codes.
  8. Reset and recordThe service indicator is reset, the book stamped or the digital record updated as a major service, and the items now due next are noted, including the cambelt interval where your engine has one.
  9. Road test and handoverThe car is road tested with particular attention to pedal feel after the fluid change and to how the engine pulls after new plugs, then we go through the report and the measurements with you.

Questions

Major Service in Mitcham: your questions answered

A water-in-fuel light came on and went off. Can I ignore it?

No. It means water has collected in the filter housing to the level of the sensor, and water in a modern common-rail system causes corrosion and poor lubrication in parts that cost a great deal to replace. Draining the filter is quick. Ignoring the light until it stays on is how pumps get damaged.

Do you prime the fuel system or just start it?

We prime it, using the hand pump or the system's own electric priming where fitted, so the new filter fills before the engine runs. Cranking a dry system to fill a filter puts the high-pressure pump through exactly the unlubricated running you are trying to avoid. It takes a few minutes longer and is worth doing properly.

My van is on a lease and has to go back next year. Is a major service worth it?

If the schedule says it is due, yes, because a gap in the history is the kind of thing that gets charged back at the end of a lease. Have the invoice to hand when it goes in. We stamp the book or update the digital record either way.

Does a major service include the cambelt?

No, and any garage implying it does is worth questioning. Cambelt intervals are set by engine, commonly somewhere between 60,000 and 100,000 miles or five to ten years, and the job involves removing covers, mounts and ancillaries. We tell you when yours falls due and quote it separately.

My car is a diesel. What do I get instead of spark plugs?

A diesel has no spark plugs, so the equivalent long-interval item is the fuel filter where the car has a serviceable one. Glow plugs are not a service item: they are replaced when one fails or when a diagnostic scan shows a circuit fault, and that is a separate repair.

I do very low mileage. Do I need a major or will a full do?

The time-based part of the major still applies. Brake fluid is a two-year item whether the car moves or not, and oil and filters age by date. The mileage items, particularly spark plugs, may well not be due, and we will not charge you for plugs you do not need.

Does it include coolant, gearbox oil or an air conditioning service?

No. Coolant, gearbox and differential oils and air conditioning all run to their own intervals, which vary widely between makes. We check the record and flag anything that has fallen due, then quote it separately so you can decide what to do and when.

Will a major service clear my engine management light?

Only if the cause happens to be something the service renews, such as a misfire code from worn plugs. A light with any other cause needs proper diagnosis, and clearing a code without fixing the fault simply hides it until the light returns. We tell you which situation you are in before we start.
