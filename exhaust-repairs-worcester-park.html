Age, not mileage: exhausts on older, cared-for cars

A car that has been looked after for years by the same owner in Worcester Park may not have covered many miles, but its exhaust still ages. Rubber hangers perish and crack with time, clamps and flange bolts rust solid, and silencers slowly corrode even on a car that lives on a driveway off one of the wide 1930s roads. Many of those cars do mostly local driving too, along Central Road or the school run, which keeps the system cooler than it would like.

The challenge with an older system is taking the failed part off without harming the good parts next to it. Fixings that have been in place for fifteen years rarely come undone cleanly. Force them carelessly and a sound centre pipe or a flange on the front section can twist or snap, turning a rear-box job into something bigger. We use heat and penetrating fluid, cut where cutting is the right answer, and check the joint we're connecting to before fitting anything.

Parts for older models can take a little longer to source, so we'll check availability before you bring the car in. We fit OE or OE-equivalent sections made for the vehicle, with new hangers and clamps, and we'll tell you if an adjoining section is so corroded it's likely to fail when disturbed, so you can decide before we start. Where the original system is still largely sound, we'd rather keep it than replace it for the sake of it. The quote comes in writing.

Exhaust Repairs: what’s involved

An exhaust does more than carry fumes away. The manifold collects gases from the engine, the catalytic converter cleans them, and on diesels the DPF traps soot. Silencers reduce noise, a flexible section absorbs engine movement, and rubber hangers hold everything clear of the body. Lambda sensors before and after the catalyst tell the ECU how well the engine is burning fuel and whether the catalyst is working.

Exhausts live in a harsh place. Short journeys leave acidic condensation sitting inside silencers, road salt attacks the outside, and speed bumps and kerbs knock pipes out of line. A leak changes more than the noise. It can let fumes into the cabin, upset lambda sensor readings and cause an MOT failure. Catalytic converters are also targeted by thieves, particularly on some hybrids, and a stolen cat needs replacing before the car can be driven legally.

Signs your exhaust needs attention

Louder engine or blowing noise

A deeper roar or a chuffing noise that rises with engine speed usually means a hole, a failed joint or a split flexi pipe.

Rattling underneath

Loose heat shields, broken clamps or a collapsed internal baffle in a silencer all rattle, often most noticeably at idle or over bumps.

Exhaust hanging low or knocking

Perished rubber hangers or broken brackets let the system drop and knock against the floor or suspension.

Fumes smell in the cabin

A leak near the front of the system can draw fumes into the car. This needs checking promptly because exhaust gas contains carbon monoxide.

Engine light with catalyst or lambda codes

Catalyst efficiency and lambda sensor codes can mean a failing cat, a faulty sensor or an air leak upstream of the sensor that upsets its readings.

Sudden very loud noise on start-up

An exhaust that sounds dramatically louder overnight, especially on a hybrid, may mean the catalytic converter has been cut out.

Visible rust or holes

Heavy scaling, holes in silencer boxes and corroded joints are usually spotted at service or MOT before they start to blow.

When should it be done?

There is no fixed replacement interval for an exhaust. Life depends on the materials, the car and how it is used, with lots of short trips shortening it most. Get the system checked as soon as the note changes, something is rattling or hanging low, or you smell fumes. A leak or insecure exhaust is an MOT failure, so it makes sense to deal with it before the test rather than after.

  • Any change in exhaust noise, blowing or rattling
  • Fumes smell inside the car
  • Catalyst or lambda sensor fault codes
  • Advisories for corrosion or insecure mountings at MOT or service
  • After suspected catalytic converter theft

How we repair an exhaust

  1. Check-in and listenWe ask what you have noticed and listen to the car running, as the type of noise often points to the area of the leak.
  2. Lift and inspectWith the car on a ramp we inspect the whole system, from the manifold back, including joints, flexi pipe, silencers, hangers, heat shields and sensors.
  3. Check codes where relevantIf there are catalyst or lambda codes we scan the car and check sensor data, so we don't replace a cat when the cause is a sensor or a leak.
  4. Written quoteWe explain which sections need replacing and why, and give you a written quote. Where a section is sound, we keep it.
  5. Remove the old sectionsWe remove the failed parts, cutting seized fixings where necessary, and inspect the joints and brackets we are connecting to.

  7. Leak test and road testWe run the engine and check every joint for leaks, clear any related codes and road test the car to check for noise and knocks.

About Worcester Park

Worcester Park is a quiet, largely inter-war suburb about 10 miles south-west of central London, built up after the railway arrived and named after the 4th Earl of Worcester, keeper of Henry VIII's Great Park of Nonsuch from 1606. It sits across several council boundaries: most of it is in Sutton, the Cuddington side is in Surrey's Epsom and Ewell, and the station itself and part of Old Malden are in Kingston. Housing is mostly semi-detached family homes on long residential roads, focused on the half-mile Central Road shopping street. Driving is generally easy away from Central Road, though the single main road through the centre gets slow at peak times.

Exhaust Repairs in Worcester Park: your questions answered

Can you still get exhaust parts for my older car?

For most popular models, yes, from suppliers such as Bosal, Walker or BM Catalysts. Rarer or older models can take longer to source. Give us the registration when you book and we'll check before you bring it in.

Will removing the old silencer damage the rest of the system?

It can on a badly corroded system, which is why we take care over seized fixings and inspect the joining pipe first. If a neighbouring section is likely to break when disturbed, we'll tell you beforehand and let you decide.

My car rarely goes further than Central Road. Why is the exhaust failing?

Short, low-speed journeys let condensation collect inside the silencers, and rubber hangers perish with age regardless of mileage. Low-mileage cars often need exhaust work sooner than people expect.

Do I need a whole new exhaust?

Usually not. Exhausts are made in sections, and we only replace the parts that have failed. If a neighbouring section is badly corroded and likely to break when disturbed, we will tell you and let you decide.

Can you weld my exhaust instead?

Sometimes, where the pipe around a small hole or split is still sound. On thin, corroded silencers a weld often won't last, so a new section is better value. We'll give you an honest view once we see it.

Will an exhaust leak fail the MOT?

Yes. A leaking exhaust, an insecure system or a missing catalytic converter where one was fitted as standard is an MOT failure. Excessive noise and failing the emissions test are also reasons for failure.

My catalytic converter was stolen. What now?

Don't drive the car far. It will be very loud and may run poorly, and it isn't legal on the road without its cat. Report the theft to the police and your insurer, then we can quote for a type-approved replacement.

Do you replace DPFs too?

Yes, where a diesel particulate filter is damaged or too ash-loaded to clean we can quote for replacement. If it is simply blocked with soot, cleaning may be the better option, which we cover under our DPF cleaning service.
