What Coulsdon's hills do to a loaded van

Coulsdon sits on the North Downs, and anyone driving a van here knows it. The climb up to Old Coulsdon and Bradmore Green is steep, the lanes towards the downs are narrow and winding, and plenty of residential roads rise and fall sharply. Add a tonne of tools and materials and every descent asks a lot of the brakes, while every hill start with a full load wears the clutch. Tradespeople's vans are common on the newer estates too, including the homes built at Cane Hill, and those roads have their own climbs.

Down in the valley, the A23 Brighton Road and the Farthing Way relief road carry through traffic towards the M25, and the Farthing Way junction with Lion Green Road is a well-known queuing point. So a local van might mix a motorway run in the morning with hill work around Grange Park in the afternoon. That's why we don't treat every van the same. At the service we measure pad and disc thickness, check rear drums or shoes, test the handbrake's hold, check brake fluid condition and assess the clutch, because on hill work those items can wear out well before the schedule expects them to.

We also look at the parts that carry the load: springs, shock absorbers, suspension bushes and tyres, which should carry the load index the van needs. Diesel vans get a scan for faults and a check of the particulate filter and AdBlue system. Oil is to your van maker's approval, and brake and suspension parts are commercial-rated rather than car-grade.

The drive to us is about 17 minutes along the A237 via Woodcote and Wallington. Most services are done in two to four hours, and anything more is quoted in writing before we start. If the van's brakes or clutch feel unsafe, don't drive it down the hill to us; we can arrange collection or recovery. Our repair work comes with a 12-month workmanship warranty.

Local tip

On long descents from Old Coulsdon, use a lower gear with a load on board rather than riding the brakes; it keeps the discs cooler and makes pads last longer.

Van Servicing: what’s involved

Vans work harder than most cars. They carry heavy loads, stop and start all day and often cover high mileage, which puts extra strain on brakes, suspension, tyres, the clutch and the engine oil. Most modern diesel vans also have a diesel particulate filter, and many use AdBlue, both of which suffer when a van spends its day on short urban runs.

A van service follows the manufacturer's schedule with the correct approved oil, but we also pay attention to load-related wear: rear brakes, springs and shock absorbers, tyre condition and load rating, and the sliding door, rear doors and their hinges. For self-employed tradespeople and small businesses, catching a worn part at a planned service is far cheaper than losing a working day to a breakdown.

Signs your van needs attention

Service indicator or overdue service

Vans on long-life schedules can still need oil sooner if they do lots of short trips or idling, so don't stretch past the maximum interval.

Brakes feel weaker when loaded

Longer stopping distances with a load, a spongy pedal or grinding noises point to worn pads, discs or rear drums and shoes.

Sagging or bouncing rear

A van that sits low at the back, bounces over bumps or bottoms out can have broken springs or worn shock absorbers.

DPF or AdBlue warnings

Stop-start work makes vans prone to blocked particulate filters, and AdBlue system faults can lead to a restricted-start countdown on the dash.

Clutch slipping or heavy

Revs rising without the van speeding up, especially uphill or loaded, is a sign of a worn clutch or dual mass flywheel.

Uneven tyre wear

Heavy loads and kerbing wear tyres quickly. Van tyres need the right load index, usually marked with a C for commercial.

Doors sticking or not latching

Sliding door rollers, hinges and locks wear with constant use. A door that won't latch is a security and safety problem.

When should a van be serviced?

Follow the manufacturer's service schedule, which for most light vans is based on time and mileage, whichever comes first, and use the service indicator as a guide. Vans that do mainly short urban trips, carry heavy loads or tow may benefit from an interim oil and filter change between full services. The MOT is due at three years old, then annually. We can book services and MOTs together to minimise time off the road.

  • Full service to the manufacturer's time or mileage schedule
  • Interim oil and filter change for high-mileage or heavy-use vans
  • First MOT at three years, then every 12 months
  • Brake and suspension checks at every service, especially if regularly loaded
  • Cambelt or timing chain at the manufacturer's interval, which varies by engine

About Coulsdon

Coulsdon is a town on the chalk hills of the North Downs at the southern edge of Greater London, with Old Coulsdon on the hill to the east being London's southernmost settlement. The town centre grew up in the valley of Smitham Bottom after the railway arrived, and the surrounding streets are mainly inter-war and post-war semis and detached houses, with newer homes on the former Cane Hill Hospital site. It feels semi-rural, with Farthing Downs and the commons on the doorstep, and is popular with families and commuters; driving means hills, winding lanes and the A23 relief road taking through traffic past the town centre.

Van Servicing in Coulsdon: your questions answered

My van smells hot after coming down from Old Coulsdon. Is that normal?

A hot smell after a long, loaded descent usually means the brakes are working very hard, and it can also point to a sticking calliper. Use a lower gear on the hill and have the brakes checked at your next service, or sooner if the pedal feels different.

Can you check my van's clutch at a service?

We assess clutch operation as part of the road test and inspection, including where it bites and whether it slips under load. If it's close to the end, we'll tell you so you can plan the job.

Is my handbrake strong enough to hold a loaded van on Coulsdon's hills?

We test the handbrake's hold at every service and adjust or quote for repairs if it's weak. If your van ever creeps when parked on a slope, get it checked straight away.

Can you MOT my van?

Class 4 covers vans and other goods vehicles up to 3,000kg design gross weight; heavier vans up to 3,500kg fall into Class 7. Your van's design gross weight is printed on the VIN plate. Quote us that number when you ring and we will tell you before you book which test applies and whether we can carry it out.

Does my van need an operator's licence?

For most businesses using vans at or under 3,500kg gross weight in Great Britain, no. An operator's licence is generally needed for goods vehicles over 3,500kg, and since 2022 also for vans over 2,500kg used for hire or reward internationally. Check the gov.uk guidance for your situation.

Will servicing at an independent garage affect my van's warranty?

No. Warranties can't be made conditional on dealer servicing. As long as the van is serviced to the manufacturer's schedule with matching-quality parts and the correct oil, the warranty remains valid.

Why does my diesel van keep getting DPF warnings?

Short trips and lots of idling stop the filter regenerating properly. Faulty sensors, EGR valves or injectors can also cause it. We check the cause at the service, and a regular run at steady speed helps.

Do you look after several vans for one business?

Yes. Businesses with more than one vehicle can use our fleet service, with priority booking, consolidated invoicing and records for each vehicle.
