Creased Panel Repair

A crease is where a dent stops being a dent. The metal has folded rather than pushed, and folded metal is longer than it was — which is why it will not simply go back.

Is this what you have?

Four checks, in order. They take about two minutes in daylight and they decide which repair you are actually looking at.

  1. There is a distinct line within the damage, not just a smooth depression. There is a distinct line within the damage, not just a smooth depression.
  2. The paint is often cracked or lifted along the fold itself even where the rest of the panel is fine. The paint is often cracked or lifted along the fold itself even where the rest of the panel is fine.
  3. Pressing it does nothing. A crease has no give in it.
  4. Sight along the panel: a crease throws a hard shadow line where a dent throws a soft one. Sight along the panel: a crease throws a hard shadow line where a dent throws a soft one.

How we repair it

Worked back in stages with heat and careful shrinking rather than pulled, because a crease forced out quickly leaves the panel wavy and no amount of filler hides a wave on a flat surface in daylight.

Once the shape is back as far as the metal allows, it is filled, flatted, primed and refinished with a blend into the panels either side.

What you can try yourself

Nothing. This is the damage type where attempting it yourself most reliably makes the professional repair more expensive, because a stretched panel that has been pulled further is sometimes past saving when it would not have been.

When to stop. If two careful attempts have not improved it, stop there. Almost every job we see that cost more than it should have is one where a reasonable first attempt turned into a fourth.

How soon it needs doing

Not urgent unless the paint has split along the fold, which it often has. A split along a crease holds water in exactly the place the metal is already thinnest.

Your questions answered

Why is a crease dearer than a dent the same size?

Because size is not what drives it. A smooth dent is pushed back; a crease has stretched metal that has to be shrunk back, and that is slow, skilled work done in small increments.

Should I just replace the panel?

On a bolt-on panel — wing, bonnet, boot, door — a heavy crease is genuinely where replacement starts to win, and we will price both so you can choose. On a welded panel it is rarely the cheaper route.

Will it be completely invisible?

On a well-executed repair, yes. The honest caveat is that a crease across a sharp body line is the hardest thing in bodywork to make perfect, and we will tell you before we start if yours is one of those.

How long does it take?

Longer than a dent — usually three days rather than two, because the metalwork cannot be rushed without showing.
