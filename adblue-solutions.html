How we handle AdBlue system repair at Hackbridge

The scan tool is only the beginning. The fault memory tells you the module has noticed something; the live data tells you what. We look at the dosing quantity the ECU is requesting against what the system reports delivering, at the upstream and downstream NOx readings and whether the gap between them makes chemical sense, at tank level and temperature, and at the state of the quality sensor. On most makes we can then command a dosing test and a pressure build and watch the pump hold, or fail to.

Physical checks follow the data. The dosing module comes out and is examined properly, because a nozzle furred with hard urea deposits sprays a dribble instead of a mist, and the system reads that as poor efficiency. Urea dissolves in warm water, so a module that is only crystallised can sometimes be cleaned rather than replaced, and we will tell you when that is worth attempting and when the part is past it. The fluid itself is checked too. AdBlue drawn from an open drum, or watered down, will set a quality fault however sound the hardware is.

Then there is the part people do not expect: the drive. The SCR system runs its own monitors over a journey, and on many cars the warning and the countdown clear only once those monitors have completed and passed. That means a real road test, not a lap of the industrial estate and a code clear. Two things we will not do. We will not fit an emulator or load delete software; both leave a car on the road pretending to meet a standard it no longer meets. And on a high-mileage car facing a tank assembly, we will tell you what the part costs before you commit, because sometimes the honest answer is that the repair is worth more than the car.

The cars we see

Almost every Euro 6 diesel car and van from around 2015 has SCR, and the ones that give trouble are those used as short-trip urban transport: builders' vans, courier vehicles and family diesels that rarely see a long run. Heat is what keeps the dosing nozzle clear, and stop-start town work never provides enough of it. Tank layouts vary a great deal. Some vehicles keep the pump, heater and level sender as separate parts; on others they arrive as one sealed assembly, which changes the cost of the repair considerably.

AdBlue System Repairs: what’s involved

Many modern diesels, and nearly all built to the later Euro 6 standards, use selective catalytic reduction (SCR) to cut nitrogen oxide (NOx) emissions. AdBlue, a mix of urea and deionised water, is pumped from its own tank and sprayed into the exhaust by a dosing module ahead of the SCR catalyst. There it turns harmful NOx into nitrogen and water. NOx sensors before and after the catalyst tell the ECU whether the system is working, and a quality sensor checks the fluid in the tank.

Because the system is part of the car's emissions approval, the law requires it to enforce itself. If the ECU detects a fault, low fluid or poor-quality AdBlue, it shows a warning and starts a countdown. When that reaches zero, the engine will not restart once switched off. Common faults include crystallised AdBlue blocking the dosing module, failed pumps and tank heaters, and NOx sensor failures.

Signs of an AdBlue system fault

AdBlue or SCR warning light

A warning that stays on after the tank has been topped up points to a system fault rather than low fluid.

No-start countdown

A message such as 'engine start not possible in X miles' means the ECU has detected a fault. Once it reaches zero the engine will not restart after being switched off.

Engine management light

NOx sensor, dosing and SCR efficiency faults usually also light the engine management light and store emissions-related codes.

White crystals around the filler or exhaust

AdBlue leaves white urea crystals as it dries. Deposits around the dosing module, pipes or tank point to a leak or a blocked injector.

AdBlue use too low or too high

If the tank level barely drops, the system may not be dosing. If it empties much faster than usual, look for a leak.

Faults in cold weather

AdBlue freezes at around minus 11°C and relies on heaters in the tank and lines. A heater failure often shows up as a warning on cold mornings.

Wrong fluid in the tank

Diesel, water or screenwash in the AdBlue tank, or AdBlue in the fuel tank, needs dealing with before the car is driven any further.

When should it be done?

Book in as soon as an AdBlue or SCR warning appears that topping up doesn't clear. Don't wait for the countdown to run out, as once it reaches zero the car will not restart and may need recovery. If the wrong fluid has gone into either tank, don't start the engine. AdBlue itself should be topped up with fluid meeting ISO 22241, and the tank refilled well before it runs low.

  • Any AdBlue or SCR warning that remains after topping up
  • As soon as a no-start countdown message appears
  • Emissions or NOx-related codes with the engine management light on
  • Visible white crystal deposits or leaks around the AdBlue system
  • Immediately if the wrong fluid has been put in either tank

How we repair an AdBlue system

  1. Check-in and historyWe ask when the warning appeared, how much mileage is left on any countdown, and whether the tank was recently filled and with what.
  2. Diagnostic scanWe read codes from the engine and SCR control modules and review NOx sensor readings, dosing quantities, tank level, temperature and quality data.
  3. Test the systemWe carry out the manufacturer's dosing quantity test, check pump pressure and heater operation, and inspect the dosing module, lines and wiring.
  4. Check fluid qualityWe check the AdBlue for contamination or dilution. Contaminated fluid has to be drained and the system flushed before new parts are fitted.
  5. Quote and repairWe explain the fault and give you a written quote. Once approved we clear crystallisation or replace the dosing module, pump, heater, sensor or wiring as needed.
  6. Adaptations and resetsWe carry out the adaptations the car requires after the repair, such as resetting learned values and running the dosing test again.
  7. Road testWe road test long enough for the SCR system to monitor itself, then rescan to confirm the warning and countdown have cleared.

Parts we fit

AdBlue parts have to match the vehicle's emissions system exactly, so we fit OE or OE-equivalent components, such as Bosch or Continental dosing and sensor parts where those are the original fitment. On many cars the pump, heater and level sender are built into the tank as a single unit. We only use AdBlue meeting ISO 22241. We do not fit AdBlue emulators or delete software, which are illegal for road use.

If it’s left

An AdBlue fault won't clear itself. Once the countdown starts, the car will eventually refuse to restart, which usually happens at the least convenient time and may mean recovery. A crystallising dosing module tends to get worse, and a NOx sensor or dosing fault left in place keeps the engine management light on, which is an MOT failure. Acting early usually means a simpler repair.

Worth knowing

What people get wrong about AdBlue system repair

Treating AdBlue as a fuel additive

It never touches combustion. It is sprayed into the exhaust ahead of the SCR catalyst, so it does nothing for economy or power, and nothing about it belongs anywhere near the fuel tank.

Topping up from an open container

AdBlue is made to a tight specification and picks up contamination easily. A part-used drum in a shed or a dusty funnel is a common cause of a quality fault that then gets blamed on the hardware.

Resetting the countdown with a cheap tool

Some generic tools claim to clear the no-start countdown. The underlying fault is untouched, the countdown starts again, and the stored data that would have shortened the diagnosis has gone.

Assuming every modern diesel has it

Some Euro 6 diesels use a lean NOx trap in the exhaust instead and carry no AdBlue tank at all. It is worth knowing which you have before buying fluid or worrying about a warning that cannot apply to your car.

AdBlue System Repairs: your questions answered

The countdown has hit zero and the car will not start. Will you come out to it?

We will. Recovery is run in-house, so a vehicle that has locked itself out can be collected and brought straight to the workshop. Tell us the mileage showing on the countdown when you call, as it helps us judge how much room there is if the car can still be driven in.

Can you check the AdBlue system on a diesel I am thinking of buying?

It is worth doing. A pre-purchase scan shows stored and pending SCR codes, dosing figures and whether an emulator has been fitted, none of which is obvious from the dashboard. A seller who cleared the codes that morning cannot hide the readiness monitors.

How much of the bill is diagnosis and how much is parts?

Diagnosis on an SCR system takes real time, because the data has to be read, tested and usually road tested. We agree that time with you first, and it is itemised separately from parts and fitting on the written quote so you can see what you are paying for.

Is the repair guaranteed, and could the warning come back?

The repair itself is covered for a year. Injectors, pumps and NOx sensors sit behind that on their maker's terms. What we cannot promise is that another component in the same system will not fail later, particularly on a vehicle that has been driven with a fault for some time. If anything else looks marginal we will tell you at handover.

Can you delete or disable the AdBlue system?

No. AdBlue delete software and emulators are illegal for road use because they remove part of the car's emissions control. They can also affect insurance and cause problems at MOT. We repair the system so it works as designed.

I've topped up but the light is still on. Why?

Some cars need a short drive or a few ignition cycles to recognise a refill. If the warning stays, the fault is usually in the dosing module, pump, heater, NOx sensors or quality sensor, and needs a diagnostic check.

I put AdBlue in the fuel tank. What should I do?

Don't start the engine. AdBlue can damage the fuel pump and injectors, so the tank and fuel lines need draining and flushing. The same applies to diesel in the AdBlue tank, which damages the pump and seals.

What causes crystallisation?

AdBlue leaves urea crystals behind as it dries, especially around the hot dosing module. Leaks, lots of short trips and a dosing module that isn't spraying properly all make it worse. Deposits can block the nozzle and trigger SCR efficiency faults.

Can I use any AdBlue?

Use fluid meeting ISO 22241, sold as AdBlue or diesel exhaust fluid. Never dilute it with water. Keep it sealed and out of heat, because old or contaminated fluid can trigger quality sensor warnings.
